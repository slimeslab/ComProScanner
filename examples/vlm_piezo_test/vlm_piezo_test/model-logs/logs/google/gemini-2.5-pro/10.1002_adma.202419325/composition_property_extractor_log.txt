# ABSTRACT
Piezoelectric materials with a high piezoelectric coefficient (d33) and high mechanical quality factor (Qm) are vital for advanced high‐power applications. However, achieving this combination is challenging, particularly for lead‐free piezoelectrics, because a high d33 value relies on mobile domain walls, which increase dissipative losses and reduce Qm. In this study, this longstanding trade‐off is overcome by introducing defect dipoles (via Mn doping) into the quadruple point (QP) composition of the lead‐free Ba(Sn, Ti)O3 system. The resultant 0.5%Mn‐doped Ba(Sn0.11Ti0.89)O3 (BST‐0.5%Mn) ceramic exhibits a high d33 value of 710 pC/N and high Qm value of 929, while the BST‐1%Mn ceramic achieves a d33 value of 614 pC/N and Qm value of 1138. These values represent a 10‐fold increase in Qm and 1.6‐fold increase in d33 for BST‐0.5%Mn, compared to those for undoped BST. High‐resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy and phase‐field simulations reveal that the enhanced d33 and Qm are attributable to the coexistence of multiple phases of QPs with symmetry‐conforming defect dipoles, challenging the long‐held notion of physical incompatibility between high d33 and high Qm.

# ABSTRACT
Piezoelectric materials with a high piezoelectric coefficient (d33) and high mechanical quality factor (Qm) are vital for advanced high‐power applications. However, achieving this combination is challenging, particularly for lead‐free piezoelectrics, because a high d33 value relies on mobile domain walls, which increase dissipative losses and reduce Qm. In this study, this longstanding trade‐off is overcome by introducing defect dipoles (via Mn doping) into the quadruple point (QP) composition of the lead‐free Ba(Sn, Ti)O3 system. The resultant 0.5%Mn‐doped Ba(Sn0.11Ti0.89)O3 (BST‐0.5%Mn) ceramic exhibits a high d33 value of 710 pC/N and high Qm value of 929, while the BST‐1%Mn ceramic achieves a d33 value of 614 pC/N and Qm value of 1138. These values represent a 10‐fold increase in Qm and 1.6‐fold increase in d33 for BST‐0.5%Mn, compared to those for undoped BST. High‐resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy and phase‐field simulations reveal that the enhanced d33 and Qm are attributable to the coexistence of multiple phases of QPs with symmetry‐conforming defect dipoles, challenging the long‐held notion of physical incompatibility between high d33 and high Qm.

# ABSTRACT
Piezoelectric materials with a high piezoelectric coefficient (d33) and high mechanical quality factor (Qm) are vital for advanced high‐power applications. However, achieving this combination is challenging, particularly for lead‐free piezoelectrics, because a high d33 value relies on mobile domain walls, which increase dissipative losses and reduce Qm. In this study, this longstanding trade‐off is overcome by introducing defect dipoles (via Mn doping) into the quadruple point (QP) composition of the lead‐free Ba(Sn, Ti)O3 system. The resultant 0.5%Mn‐doped Ba(Sn0.11Ti0.89)O3 (BST‐0.5%Mn) ceramic exhibits a high d33 value of 710 pC/N and high Qm value of 929, while the BST‐1%Mn ceramic achieves a d33 value of 614 pC/N and Qm value of 1138. These values represent a 10‐fold increase in Qm and 1.6‐fold increase in d33 for BST‐0.5%Mn, compared to those for undoped BST. High‐resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy and phase‐field simulations reveal that the enhanced d33 and Qm are attributable to the coexistence of multiple phases of QPs with symmetry‐conforming defect dipoles, challenging the long‐held notion of physical incompatibility between high d33 and high Qm.

According to the Landau phenomenological theory, a high d 33 can be obtained via flattening of the Gibbs free-energy density profile with respect to polarization. [ 15] QP composition presents a coexistence of multiple phases (C, T, O, R), where the polarization rotation energy barrier nearly vanishes (Figure S9, Supporting Information), [12b,c,15c] theoretically leading to high dielectric and piezoelectric properties. Consequently, in ferroelectric systems with QP, QP composition usually exhibits the highest dielectric constant at T C . [ 15c,16] However, owing to the coexistence of multiphase structures with extremely small domain sizes of QP composition in this work, the unpoled QP samples are prone to depolarization after poling. # ABSTRACT
Piezoelectric materials with a high piezoelectric coefficient (d33) and high mechanical quality factor (Qm) are vital for advanced high‐power applications. However, achieving this combination is challenging, particularly for lead‐free piezoelectrics, because a high d33 value relies on mobile domain walls, which increase dissipative losses and reduce Qm. In this study, this longstanding trade‐off is overcome by introducing defect dipoles (via Mn doping) into the quadruple point (QP) composition of the lead‐free Ba(Sn, Ti)O3 system. The resultant 0.5%Mn‐doped Ba(Sn0.11Ti0.89)O3 (BST‐0.5%Mn) ceramic exhibits a high d33 value of 710 pC/N and high Qm value of 929, while the BST‐1%Mn ceramic achieves a d33 value of 614 pC/N and Qm value of 1138. These values represent a 10‐fold increase in Qm and 1.6‐fold increase in d33 for BST‐0.5%Mn, compared to those for undoped BST. High‐resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy and phase‐field simulations reveal that the enhanced d33 and Qm are attributable to the coexistence of multiple phases of QPs with symmetry‐conforming defect dipoles, challenging the long‐held notion of physical incompatibility between high d33 and high Qm.
