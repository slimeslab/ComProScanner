# TITLE
Microstructure, phase structure, and electrical properties of Zr-modified BaTiO3-based lead-free ceramics

# ABSTRACT
Lead-free piezoelectric ceramics of (BaCa)(TiSnZr)O (BCTSZ) were synthesized by the conventional solid-state method. The evolution of their microstructure, phase structure, and electrical properties of BCTSZ ceramics in the composition range of 0 ≤  ≤ 0.06 was systematically investigated. A dense microstructure is developed, and a coexistence of orthorhombic and tetragonal phases is identified in the ceramics, showing an enhanced electrical properties (e.g.,  ~ 545 pC/N,  ~ 0.52,  ~ 3135, tan  ~ 0.008, strain ~ 0.106%, and * ~ 503 pm/V) as well as relatively high Curie temperature ( ~ 100 °C) in the composition of  = 0.02.

# INTRODUCTION
In the past several years, lead zirconate titanate (PZT) ceramics have dominated the market owing to their excellent comprehensive properties [, ]. However, the toxicity raw material of PbO in PZT is more than 60 wt%, which can cause damage to our health and environmental pollution. Therefore, it highly urgent to develope environment-friendly lead-free materials for partial substitution of PZT. Recently, (K0.50Na0.50)NbO3 and Bi0.50Na0.50TiO3-based perovskite materials have been considered as the promising candidates because of their excellent electrical properties [, , , , , , , \u2013]. As far as the piezoelectric constant (d33) is concerned, lead-free materials are difficult to be superior to those of lead-based materials [, , , , , , , \u2013]. In 2009, a material system of the Ca and Zr-doped BaTiO3 ceramics with highly d33 value of ~ 620 pC/N by forming tricritical triple point has been reported, which is superior to or comparable to those of lead-based ones, initiating worldwide interest. In 1956, BaTiO3 material with a high permittivity and relatively high piezoelectricity is obtained by Bechmann et al.. Subsequently, some attempts have been carried out the investigations on BaTiO3-based ceramics by researchers, including chemical modifications, new preparation methods, and so on [, , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , \u2013]. Among those, the dopant engineering (e.g., Ca, Sr, Zr, Sn, Hf) becomes an effective tool to promote their piezoelectric properties by driving the formation of phase boundaries, and a series of breakthroughs in d33 are attained [, , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , \u2013]. It is well known that the enhanced piezoelectricity can be attributed to the phase boundaries (e.g., R-T, R-O-T, O-T) [, , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , \u2013]. For example, Xue et al. reported a BaTiO3-based material with high d33 value of ~ 530 pC/N by constructing the R-O-T phase boundary ; Liu et al. was obtained a material system of the Ca and Zr-doped BaTiO3 ceramics with highly d33 value of ~ 620 pC/N by forming the tricritical triple point, which is even higher than that of some lead-based ceramics. However, there are few systematical reports on the effect of Ca, Zr, and Sn on the microstructure, phase boundary, and electrical properties of BaTiO3 ceramics. In this work, lead-free piezoelectric ceramics of (Ba0.98Ca0.02)(Ti0.94Sn0.06\u2212xZrx)O3 (BCTS0.06\u2212xZx) were synthesized by the conventional solid-state method. The evolution of their microstructure, phase structure, and electrical properties of BCTS0.06\u2212xZx ceramics in the composition range of 0 \u2264 x \u2264 0.06 was systematically investigated. A dense microstructure is developed, and a coexistence of orthorhombic and tetragonal phases (O-T) is identified in the ceramics, showing an enhanced electrical properties (e.g., d33 ~ 545 pC/N, kp ~ 0.52, \u03b5r ~ 3135, tan \u03b4 ~ 0.008, strain ~ 0.106%, and d33* ~ 503 pm/V) as well as relatively high Curie temperature (TC ~ 100 \xb0C) in the composition of x = 0.02, One can find that such a good comprehensive performance is superior to or comparable to those of reported results of BaTiO3-based ceramics, as is shown in Table [, , , , , ]. As a result, the addition of Zr content can effectively enhance the piezoelectricity of BaTiO3-based ceramics without expensing sharply of TC, which is a promising candidate for lead-free piezoelectric ceramics. # CONCLUSION
In this work, lead-free ceramics concerning (Ba0.98Ca0.02)(Ti0.94Sn0.06\u2212xZrx)O3 were prepared by the conventional solid-state method, and the composition dependence of the microstructure, phase structure, electrical properties was systematically investigated. An enhanced electrical properties (e.g., d33 ~ 545 pC/N, kp ~ 0.52, \u03b5r ~ 3135, tan \u03b4 ~ 0.008, strain ~ 0.106%, and d33* ~ 503 pm/V) as well as relatively high Curie temperature (TC ~ 100 \xb0C) was obtained in the composition of x = 0.02 owe to the dense microstructure and coexistence of O and T phases. As a result, the addition of Zr content can effectively enhance the piezoelectricity of BaTiO3-based ceramics without sacrificing the TC sharply, which is a potential lead-free candidate material for the piezoelectric applications.

# TITLE
Microstructure, phase structure, and electrical properties of Zr-modified BaTiO3-based lead-free ceramics

# ABSTRACT
Lead-free piezoelectric ceramics of (BaCa)(TiSnZr)O (BCTSZ) were synthesized by the conventional solid-state method. The evolution of their microstructure, phase structure, and electrical properties of BCTSZ ceramics in the composition range of 0 ≤  ≤ 0.06 was systematically investigated. A dense microstructure is developed, and a coexistence of orthorhombic and tetragonal phases is identified in the ceramics, showing an enhanced electrical properties (e.g.,  ~ 545 pC/N,  ~ 0.52,  ~ 3135, tan  ~ 0.008, strain ~ 0.106%, and * ~ 503 pm/V) as well as relatively high Curie temperature ( ~ 100 °C) in the composition of  = 0.02.

# INTRODUCTION
In the past several years, lead zirconate titanate (PZT) ceramics have dominated the market owing to their excellent comprehensive properties [, ]. However, the toxicity raw material of PbO in PZT is more than 60 wt%, which can cause damage to our health and environmental pollution. Therefore, it highly urgent to develope environment-friendly lead-free materials for partial substitution of PZT. Recently, (K0.50Na0.50)NbO3 and Bi0.50Na0.50TiO3-based perovskite materials have been considered as the promising candidates because of their excellent electrical properties [, , , , , , , \u2013]. As far as the piezoelectric constant (d33) is concerned, lead-free materials are difficult to be superior to those of lead-based materials [, , , , , , , \u2013]. In 2009, a material system of the Ca and Zr-doped BaTiO3 ceramics with highly d33 value of ~ 620 pC/N by forming tricritical triple point has been reported, which is superior to or comparable to those of lead-based ones, initiating worldwide interest. In 1956, BaTiO3 material with a high permittivity and relatively high piezoelectricity is obtained by Bechmann et al.. Subsequently, some attempts have been carried out the investigations on BaTiO3-based ceramics by researchers, including chemical modifications, new preparation methods, and so on [, , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , \u2013]. Among those, the dopant engineering (e.g., Ca, Sr, Zr, Sn, Hf) becomes an effective tool to promote their piezoelectric properties by driving the formation of phase boundaries, and a series of breakthroughs in d33 are attained [, , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , \u2013]. It is well known that the enhanced piezoelectricity can be attributed to the phase boundaries (e.g., R-T, R-O-T, O-T) [, , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , , \u2013]. For example, Xue et al. reported a BaTiO3-based material with high d33 value of ~ 530 pC/N by constructing the R-O-T phase boundary ; Liu et al. was obtained a material system of the Ca and Zr-doped BaTiO3 ceramics with highly d33 value of ~ 620 pC/N by forming the tricritical triple point, which is even higher than that of some lead-based ceramics. However, there are few systematical reports on the effect of Ca, Zr, and Sn on the microstructure, phase boundary, and electrical properties of BaTiO3 ceramics. In this work, lead-free piezoelectric ceramics of (Ba0.98Ca0.02)(Ti0.94Sn0.06\u2212xZrx)O3 (BCTS0.06\u2212xZx) were synthesized by the conventional solid-state method. The evolution of their microstructure, phase structure, and electrical properties of BCTS0.06\u2212xZx ceramics in the composition range of 0 \u2264 x \u2264 0.06 was systematically investigated. A dense microstructure is developed, and a coexistence of orthorhombic and tetragonal phases (O-T) is identified in the ceramics, showing an enhanced electrical properties (e.g., d33 ~ 545 pC/N, kp ~ 0.52, \u03b5r ~ 3135, tan \u03b4 ~ 0.008, strain ~ 0.106%, and d33* ~ 503 pm/V) as well as relatively high Curie temperature (TC ~ 100 \xb0C) in the composition of x = 0.02, One can find that such a good comprehensive performance is superior to or comparable to those of reported results of BaTiO3-based ceramics, as is shown in Table [, , , , , ]. As a result, the addition of Zr content can effectively enhance the piezoelectricity of BaTiO3-based ceramics without expensing sharply of TC, which is a promising candidate for lead-free piezoelectric ceramics. # CONCLUSION
In this work, lead-free ceramics concerning (Ba0.98Ca0.02)(Ti0.94Sn0.06\u2212xZrx)O3 were prepared by the conventional solid-state method, and the composition dependence of the microstructure, phase structure, electrical properties was systematically investigated. An enhanced electrical properties (e.g., d33 ~ 545 pC/N, kp ~ 0.52, \u03b5r ~ 3135, tan \u03b4 ~ 0.008, strain ~ 0.106%, and d33* ~ 503 pm/V) as well as relatively high Curie temperature (TC ~ 100 \xb0C) was obtained in the composition of x = 0.02 owe to the dense microstructure and coexistence of O and T phases. As a result, the addition of Zr content can effectively enhance the piezoelectricity of BaTiO3-based ceramics without sacrificing the TC sharply, which is a potential lead-free candidate material for the piezoelectric applications.
