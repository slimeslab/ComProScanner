# ABSTRACT
To understand the effects of A-site isovalent doping on the electrical properties of Bi-based lead-free piezoelectric ceramics, BiLaNaBaTiO with  = 0.00, 0.01, 0.03, and 0.05 were studied. The -ray diffraction and the Rietveld refinement patterns of the samples show the Pm3m cubic phases. The addition of LaE^{3+} decreases the average grain sizes of BiLaNaBaTiO samples from 1.039 μm for  = 0.00 to 0.831 μm for  = 0.05. The dielectric losses of samples are below 7% up to 400 °C at different frequencies. The temperature-dependent dielectric curves of poled BiLaNaBaTiO samples confirm a phase transition from a nonergodic relaxor to an ergodic relaxor phase when  ≥ 0.03. Interestingly, the composition near the phase transition region, BiLaNaBaTiO, shows a maximum strain of ~ 0.356% at a low applied electric field of 5 kV/mm, showing a potential lead-free candidate for piezo actuator applications.

# INTRODUCTION
The Pb-based piezoelectric ceramics are mainly used today because of their high piezoelectric coefficients near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB). However, these materials contain more than 60wt.% of Pb, which can cause damage to the kidneys, brain, nervous system, and especially intelligence of children. An intensive research effort has been devoted to studying Pb-free piezoelectrics for more than two decades [, , , \u2013]. In that respect, the Pb-free piezoelectric ceramics, such as barium titanate (BT), potassium sodium niobate (KNN), sodium bismuth titanate (NBT), and their solid solutions, are considered promising candidates [, , , \u2013]. Among Pb-free ceramics, NBT is a superior candidate for high actuation power, which has 1.4 times the actuation energy density of polycrystalline PMN piezoelectric ceramic. However, pure NBT has a large coercive field and relatively large conductivity, causing its difficulty to be electrically polarized and piezoelectric properties desirable [, ]. Various NBT-based solid solutions have been developed to reduce the poling field and improve the piezoelectric properties of NBT-based ceramics [, ]. Among them, (1-x)NBT-xBT ceramics receive the most research attention due to the inconsistent phase structure and the excellent electrical properties at the MPB [, \u2013]. Besides, La2O3 is an effective modification for improving strain properties of piezoelectric ceramics, as seen in La-modified PbZrTiO3 , La-modified BiFeO3-BaTiO3 ceramics , or La-modified Na1/2Bi1/2TiO3-Bi1/2K1/2TiO3 ceramics. Some studies relating to the effects of La2O3 on the properties of (1-x)NBT-xBT have been reported [, \u2013]. However, the effects of La2O3 on the crystal structure and electrical properties of (1-x)NBT-xBT at a low applied electric field are unopened so far. Therefore, this study investigates the effects of La2O3 on the structural and electrical properties of 0.94Na1/2Bi1/2TiO3-0.06BaTiO3 ceramics.

# CONCLUSION
This study investigated the microstructures, crystal structures, dielectric, ferroelectric, piezoelectric, and strain properties of Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 with x = 0.00, 0.01, 0.03, and 0.05. FE-SEM images of the samples show dense microstructures with clear grain boundaries. The addition of LaE^{3+} changes grain morphology and decreases the grain size of Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The XRD and Rietveld refinements results confirm the Pm3m pseudocubic phase for all Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The dielectric constant and TF-R of samples decrease as increase LaE^{3+}. The dielectric loss was below 7% for all samples. The dielectric properties of the samples were stable up to 400 \xb0C at different frequencies. A transition from a NER phase to an ER phase happened in the Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics when x \u2265 0.03. More importantly, the Bi0.44La0.03Na0.47Ba0.06TiO3 sample shows the maximum strains of 712 pm/V at a low applied electric field of 5 kV/mm, suggesting a potential candidate for piezo actuator applications.

# ABSTRACT
To understand the effects of A-site isovalent doping on the electrical properties of Bi-based lead-free piezoelectric ceramics, BiLaNaBaTiO with  = 0.00, 0.01, 0.03, and 0.05 were studied. The -ray diffraction and the Rietveld refinement patterns of the samples show the Pm3m cubic phases. The addition of LaE^{3+} decreases the average grain sizes of BiLaNaBaTiO samples from 1.039 μm for  = 0.00 to 0.831 μm for  = 0.05. The dielectric losses of samples are below 7% up to 400 °C at different frequencies. The temperature-dependent dielectric curves of poled BiLaNaBaTiO samples confirm a phase transition from a nonergodic relaxor to an ergodic relaxor phase when  ≥ 0.03. Interestingly, the composition near the phase transition region, BiLaNaBaTiO, shows a maximum strain of ~ 0.356% at a low applied electric field of 5 kV/mm, showing a potential lead-free candidate for piezo actuator applications.

# INTRODUCTION
The Pb-based piezoelectric ceramics are mainly used today because of their high piezoelectric coefficients near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB). However, these materials contain more than 60wt.% of Pb, which can cause damage to the kidneys, brain, nervous system, and especially intelligence of children. An intensive research effort has been devoted to studying Pb-free piezoelectrics for more than two decades [, , , \u2013]. In that respect, the Pb-free piezoelectric ceramics, such as barium titanate (BT), potassium sodium niobate (KNN), sodium bismuth titanate (NBT), and their solid solutions, are considered promising candidates [, , , \u2013]. Among Pb-free ceramics, NBT is a superior candidate for high actuation power, which has 1.4 times the actuation energy density of polycrystalline PMN piezoelectric ceramic. However, pure NBT has a large coercive field and relatively large conductivity, causing its difficulty to be electrically polarized and piezoelectric properties desirable [, ]. Various NBT-based solid solutions have been developed to reduce the poling field and improve the piezoelectric properties of NBT-based ceramics [, ]. Among them, (1-x)NBT-xBT ceramics receive the most research attention due to the inconsistent phase structure and the excellent electrical properties at the MPB [, \u2013]. Besides, La2O3 is an effective modification for improving strain properties of piezoelectric ceramics, as seen in La-modified PbZrTiO3 , La-modified BiFeO3-BaTiO3 ceramics , or La-modified Na1/2Bi1/2TiO3-Bi1/2K1/2TiO3 ceramics. Some studies relating to the effects of La2O3 on the properties of (1-x)NBT-xBT have been reported [, \u2013]. However, the effects of La2O3 on the crystal structure and electrical properties of (1-x)NBT-xBT at a low applied electric field are unopened so far. Therefore, this study investigates the effects of La2O3 on the structural and electrical properties of 0.94Na1/2Bi1/2TiO3-0.06BaTiO3 ceramics.

# CONCLUSION
This study investigated the microstructures, crystal structures, dielectric, ferroelectric, piezoelectric, and strain properties of Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 with x = 0.00, 0.01, 0.03, and 0.05. FE-SEM images of the samples show dense microstructures with clear grain boundaries. The addition of LaE^{3+} changes grain morphology and decreases the grain size of Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The XRD and Rietveld refinements results confirm the Pm3m pseudocubic phase for all Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The dielectric constant and TF-R of samples decrease as increase LaE^{3+}. The dielectric loss was below 7% for all samples. The dielectric properties of the samples were stable up to 400 \xb0C at different frequencies. A transition from a NER phase to an ER phase happened in the Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics when x \u2265 0.03. More importantly, the Bi0.44La0.03Na0.47Ba0.06TiO3 sample shows the maximum strains of 712 pm/V at a low applied electric field of 5 kV/mm, suggesting a potential candidate for piezo actuator applications.

# ABSTRACT
To understand the effects of A-site isovalent doping on the electrical properties of Bi-based lead-free piezoelectric ceramics, BiLaNaBaTiO with  = 0.00, 0.01, 0.03, and 0.05 were studied. The -ray diffraction and the Rietveld refinement patterns of the samples show the Pm3m cubic phases. The addition of LaE^{3+} decreases the average grain sizes of BiLaNaBaTiO samples from 1.039 μm for  = 0.00 to 0.831 μm for  = 0.05. The dielectric losses of samples are below 7% up to 400 °C at different frequencies. The temperature-dependent dielectric curves of poled BiLaNaBaTiO samples confirm a phase transition from a nonergodic relaxor to an ergodic relaxor phase when  ≥ 0.03. Interestingly, the composition near the phase transition region, BiLaNaBaTiO, shows a maximum strain of ~ 0.356% at a low applied electric field of 5 kV/mm, showing a potential lead-free candidate for piezo actuator applications.

# INTRODUCTION
The Pb-based piezoelectric ceramics are mainly used today because of their high piezoelectric coefficients near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB). However, these materials contain more than 60wt.% of Pb, which can cause damage to the kidneys, brain, nervous system, and especially intelligence of children. An intensive research effort has been devoted to studying Pb-free piezoelectrics for more than two decades [, , , \u2013]. In that respect, the Pb-free piezoelectric ceramics, such as barium titanate (BT), potassium sodium niobate (KNN), sodium bismuth titanate (NBT), and their solid solutions, are considered promising candidates [, , , \u2013]. Among Pb-free ceramics, NBT is a superior candidate for high actuation power, which has 1.4 times the actuation energy density of polycrystalline PMN piezoelectric ceramic. However, pure NBT has a large coercive field and relatively large conductivity, causing its difficulty to be electrically polarized and piezoelectric properties desirable [, ]. Various NBT-based solid solutions have been developed to reduce the poling field and improve the piezoelectric properties of NBT-based ceramics [, ]. Among them, (1-x)NBT-xBT ceramics receive the most research attention due to the inconsistent phase structure and the excellent electrical properties at the MPB [, \u2013]. Besides, La2O3 is an effective modification for improving strain properties of piezoelectric ceramics, as seen in La-modified PbZrTiO3 , La-modified BiFeO3-BaTiO3 ceramics , or La-modified Na1/2Bi1/2TiO3-Bi1/2K1/2TiO3 ceramics. Some studies relating to the effects of La2O3 on the properties of (1-x)NBT-xBT have been reported [, \u2013]. However, the effects of La2O3 on the crystal structure and electrical properties of (1-x)NBT-xBT at a low applied electric field are unopened so far. Therefore, this study investigates the effects of La2O3 on the structural and electrical properties of 0.94Na1/2Bi1/2TiO3-0.06BaTiO3 ceramics.

# CONCLUSION
This study investigated the microstructures, crystal structures, dielectric, ferroelectric, piezoelectric, and strain properties of Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 with x = 0.00, 0.01, 0.03, and 0.05. FE-SEM images of the samples show dense microstructures with clear grain boundaries. The addition of LaE^{3+} changes grain morphology and decreases the grain size of Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The XRD and Rietveld refinements results confirm the Pm3m pseudocubic phase for all Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The dielectric constant and TF-R of samples decrease as increase LaE^{3+}. The dielectric loss was below 7% for all samples. The dielectric properties of the samples were stable up to 400 \xb0C at different frequencies. A transition from a NER phase to an ER phase happened in the Bi0.47-xLaxNa0.47Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics when x \u2265 0.03. More importantly, the Bi0.44La0.03Na0.47Ba0.06TiO3 sample shows the maximum strains of 712 pm/V at a low applied electric field of 5 kV/mm, suggesting a potential candidate for piezo actuator applications.
