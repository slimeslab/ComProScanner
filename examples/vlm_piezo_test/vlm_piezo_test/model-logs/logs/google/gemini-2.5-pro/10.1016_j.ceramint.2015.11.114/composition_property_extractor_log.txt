# INTRODUCTION
Nowadays, Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT)-based piezoelectric ceramics have been widely used in various electronic applications such as buzzers, actuators, transducers, sensors, transformers and piezoelectric energy harvesting. As the main raw material of PZT, lead oxide caused the environmental problems. The ceramic Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 (BNT) with a rhombohedral perovskite structure is considered to be a good lead-free piezoceramic candidate due to its strong ferroelectric behavior at room temperature and high Curie temperature. However, the use of pure BNT materials in piezoelectric application is limited by the difficulty to pole adequately due to its large coercive fields. For further enhancement of the piezoelectric properties in BNT, it has been modified with other perovskite materials, such as BaTiO3 (BT) , Sr3FeNb2O9 (SFN) , (K,Na)NbO3 (KNN) , Bi2NiMnO6 (BNM) , BaSrTiO3 (BST) , Bi2FeCrO6(BFC) and Bi0.5K0.5TiO3 (BKT). Moreover, a giant electric field-induced strain (EFIS) comparable to that in soft PZT counterparts has been reported for BNT\u2013BaTiO3\u2013(K,Na)NbO3 (BNT\u2013BT\u2013KNN) ceramics. On the other hand BNT\u2013BT (BNBT) solid solutions exhibit higher piezoelectric properties near the morphotropic phase boundary, where the BT concentration ranges from 0.06 to 0.07. Jin et al. introduced (Al0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} into the B-site and reported a large strain response in (Bi0.5Na0.5)0.93Ba0.07Ti1\u2212x (Al0.5Nb0.5) x O3 with a large strain S max /E max of 575pm/V and high piezoelectric constant d 33 of 210pC/N. Lead-free (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}-modified BNT\u2013BaTiO3 system which delivers a significant large electric-field strain response has been achieved by Cheng et al.. Yao et al. prepared PrE^{3+}-modified 0.93(Bi0.5Na0.5)TiO3\u20130.07BaTiO3 ceramics with a large unipolar strain of 0.43% obtained under 50kV/cm, as well as desirable photoluminescence performances. Pham et al. studied a Nb-doped Bi0.5(Na0.82K0.18)0.5TiO3 system and reported a significant strain of 0.44% at 70kV/cm with 3mol% Nb. Sun et al. found an enhancement of ferroelectric properties by Pr doping in BNT. In light of these observations, a new ternary solid solution was designed and characterized through introducing B-site complex-ion (Pr1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} into the MPB solid solution (Bi1/2Na1/2)0.935+x Ba0.065TiO3 (BNBT). In this work, we doped BNBT with (Pr1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} using a conventional solid-state reaction method and systematically studied the influence of compositional modification on the crystal structure, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties and field-induced strain behaviors.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, lead-free BNBT\u2013x PN piezoelectric ceramics were prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method and the crystal structure and electrical properties were systematically investigated. All BNBT\u2013x PN ceramics form the pure perovskite phase structure, and no obvious change in the crystal structure is observed. A large field-induced strain of 0.34% at a low applied field of 60kV/cm was observed at the critical composition of x=0.0075 which corresponds to the S max /E max of 567pm/V. The dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of BNBT\u2013x PN ceramics were enhanced by a proper amount of (Pr1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}. Improved P r =32.8\u03bcC/cmE^{2}, low E c of 19.9kV/cm, and high d 33=221pC/N were observed at x=0.0050. These results show our research can benefit the developments of Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 ceramics and widen their applications range.

# INTRODUCTION
Nowadays, Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT)-based piezoelectric ceramics have been widely used in various electronic applications such as buzzers, actuators, transducers, sensors, transformers and piezoelectric energy harvesting. As the main raw material of PZT, lead oxide caused the environmental problems. The ceramic Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 (BNT) with a rhombohedral perovskite structure is considered to be a good lead-free piezoceramic candidate due to its strong ferroelectric behavior at room temperature and high Curie temperature. However, the use of pure BNT materials in piezoelectric application is limited by the difficulty to pole adequately due to its large coercive fields. For further enhancement of the piezoelectric properties in BNT, it has been modified with other perovskite materials, such as BaTiO3 (BT) , Sr3FeNb2O9 (SFN) , (K,Na)NbO3 (KNN) , Bi2NiMnO6 (BNM) , BaSrTiO3 (BST) , Bi2FeCrO6(BFC) and Bi0.5K0.5TiO3 (BKT). Moreover, a giant electric field-induced strain (EFIS) comparable to that in soft PZT counterparts has been reported for BNT\u2013BaTiO3\u2013(K,Na)NbO3 (BNT\u2013BT\u2013KNN) ceramics. On the other hand BNT\u2013BT (BNBT) solid solutions exhibit higher piezoelectric properties near the morphotropic phase boundary, where the BT concentration ranges from 0.06 to 0.07. Jin et al. introduced (Al0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} into the B-site and reported a large strain response in (Bi0.5Na0.5)0.93Ba0.07Ti1\u2212x (Al0.5Nb0.5) x O3 with a large strain S max /E max of 575pm/V and high piezoelectric constant d 33 of 210pC/N. Lead-free (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}-modified BNT\u2013BaTiO3 system which delivers a significant large electric-field strain response has been achieved by Cheng et al.. Yao et al. prepared PrE^{3+}-modified 0.93(Bi0.5Na0.5)TiO3\u20130.07BaTiO3 ceramics with a large unipolar strain of 0.43% obtained under 50kV/cm, as well as desirable photoluminescence performances. Pham et al. studied a Nb-doped Bi0.5(Na0.82K0.18)0.5TiO3 system and reported a significant strain of 0.44% at 70kV/cm with 3mol% Nb. Sun et al. found an enhancement of ferroelectric properties by Pr doping in BNT. In light of these observations, a new ternary solid solution was designed and characterized through introducing B-site complex-ion (Pr1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} into the MPB solid solution (Bi1/2Na1/2)0.935+x Ba0.065TiO3 (BNBT). In this work, we doped BNBT with (Pr1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} using a conventional solid-state reaction method and systematically studied the influence of compositional modification on the crystal structure, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties and field-induced strain behaviors.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, lead-free BNBT\u2013x PN piezoelectric ceramics were prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method and the crystal structure and electrical properties were systematically investigated. All BNBT\u2013x PN ceramics form the pure perovskite phase structure, and no obvious change in the crystal structure is observed. A large field-induced strain of 0.34% at a low applied field of 60kV/cm was observed at the critical composition of x=0.0075 which corresponds to the S max /E max of 567pm/V. The dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of BNBT\u2013x PN ceramics were enhanced by a proper amount of (Pr1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}. Improved P r =32.8\u03bcC/cmE^{2}, low E c of 19.9kV/cm, and high d 33=221pC/N were observed at x=0.0050. These results show our research can benefit the developments of Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 ceramics and widen their applications range.
