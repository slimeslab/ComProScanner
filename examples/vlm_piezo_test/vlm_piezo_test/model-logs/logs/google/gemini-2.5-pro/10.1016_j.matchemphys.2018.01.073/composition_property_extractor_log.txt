# INTRODUCTION
The most piezoelectric materials used in many applications are Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT). The (PZT) has a characteristic properties which may be enhanced by the addition of various ions at \u201cA\u201d or \u201cB\u201d sites. These dopants are mainly of two types: \u201cdonor\u201d dopants, such as LaE^{3+}, NbE^{5+}, CeE^{3+} and TaE^{5+}, which produce \u201csoft\u201d PZT; and \u201cacceptor\u201d dopant, such as KE^{+}, NaE^{+}, ScE^{3+} and FeE^{3+}, which produce \u201chard\u201d PZT. The soft dopants facilitate the domain wall motion; thereby, the electronic properties are much better compared to undoped PZT. La is one of the most widely studied dopants in PZT system. LaE^{3+}ions increases the squareness of the hysteresis loop and decreases the coercive field (Ec), improves the dielectric constant, and electromechanical coupling coefficient. NdE^{3+} ions decrease the grain size of Pb(Zr,Ti)O3. It was found that the pure phase Perovskite structure can be obtained at the samples annealed at 600 \xb0C if all compound were used as nitrates. An accurate measurement of piezoelectric coefficient d33 thin films through the use of basic principles in mechanics of vibration were reported in Ref.. Both static and dynamic measurement methods for d33 become impractical for PZT thin films. In the case of static forces, PZT thin films often have a much smaller time constant than bulk or thick-film PZT. Moreover, as PZT becomes thin (e.g., 1 \u03bcm), the output voltage is significantly reduced and the signal-to-noise ratio drops. The large force could, in turn, fracture or depolarize the PZT thin film. The aim of the work is to study the effect of ZrO2 doping in PbTiO3 to obtain high quality of piezoelectric modulus d33 and enhance the coupling factor Kp compared with those published in previous work.

# INTRODUCTION
The most piezoelectric materials used in many applications are Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT). The (PZT) has a characteristic properties which may be enhanced by the addition of various ions at \u201cA\u201d or \u201cB\u201d sites. These dopants are mainly of two types: \u201cdonor\u201d dopants, such as LaE^{3+}, NbE^{5+}, CeE^{3+} and TaE^{5+}, which produce \u201csoft\u201d PZT; and \u201cacceptor\u201d dopant, such as KE^{+}, NaE^{+}, ScE^{3+} and FeE^{3+}, which produce \u201chard\u201d PZT. The soft dopants facilitate the domain wall motion; thereby, the electronic properties are much better compared to undoped PZT. La is one of the most widely studied dopants in PZT system. LaE^{3+}ions increases the squareness of the hysteresis loop and decreases the coercive field (Ec), improves the dielectric constant, and electromechanical coupling coefficient. NdE^{3+} ions decrease the grain size of Pb(Zr,Ti)O3. It was found that the pure phase Perovskite structure can be obtained at the samples annealed at 600 \xb0C if all compound were used as nitrates. An accurate measurement of piezoelectric coefficient d33 thin films through the use of basic principles in mechanics of vibration were reported in Ref.. Both static and dynamic measurement methods for d33 become impractical for PZT thin films. In the case of static forces, PZT thin films often have a much smaller time constant than bulk or thick-film PZT. Moreover, as PZT becomes thin (e.g., 1 \u03bcm), the output voltage is significantly reduced and the signal-to-noise ratio drops. The large force could, in turn, fracture or depolarize the PZT thin film. The aim of the work is to study the effect of ZrO2 doping in PbTiO3 to obtain high quality of piezoelectric modulus d33 and enhance the coupling factor Kp compared with those published in previous work.
