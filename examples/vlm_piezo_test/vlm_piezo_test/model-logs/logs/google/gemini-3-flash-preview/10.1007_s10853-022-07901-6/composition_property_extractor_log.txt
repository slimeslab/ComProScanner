# INTRODUCTION
As a high-performance piezoelectric material, Lead Zirconate Titanate (Pb(Zr, Ti)O3, PZT) ceramics are widely used in electronic devices, such as dielectric capacitors, ultrasonic transducers, actuators and sensors. However, the main raw material PbO for preparing PZT is a volatile toxic substance, which might cause great harm to human health and environment. At present, lead-free systems mainly cover three categories: barium titanate [, \u2013] (BaTiO3, BT), potassium sodium niobate [, , \u2013] (K0.5Na0.5NbO3, KNN) and sodium bismuth titanate [, , \u2013] (Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3, NBT). For example, by the construction of rhombohedral\u2013orthorhombic (R\u2013O)  or orthorhombic\u2013tetragonal (O\u2013T) phase boundary [, ], the piezoelectric constant d33 of KNN-based ceramics can be improved to 200\u2212400 pC/N. In recent years, the creation of R\u2013T  or R\u2013O\u2013T [, ] new phase boundary, which is usually accompanied by the formation of nano-domains or local structure heterogeneity, can boost the d33 of BT and KNN-based ceramics to be higher than 500 pC/N. Very recently, Wu\u2019s group combined the effects of new phase boundary and multilayer composite, a high d33 of > 330 pC/N with the superior temperature stability over 25\u2013100 \xb0C was achieved in KNN-based ceramics. Moreover, some new physical phenomenon, such as nanoscale bubble domains with polar topologies in NBT-based bulk ferroelectrics , open up opportunity for advanced ferroelectric nanodevices. The rapid developments of lead-free piezoelectric systems enable them to replace traditional PZT and gain wide applications in the future. Among lead-free systems, KNN is most attractive due to its good piezoelectricity and high Curie temperature (TC > 300 \xb0C). On approaching the phase boundary, the piezoelectric activity of KNN-based ceramics will increase due to the enhanced polarization rotation (intrinsic contribution) and irreversible non-180\u02da domain wall motion (extrinsic contribution) arising from the flattened thermodynamic free energy profile and/or domain refinement. The optimized d33 is acquired in the compositions on the phase boundary, however, their mechanical quality factor Qm is usually very low (~ 30\u201350) owing to the significant internal friction [, , \u2013], which is difficult to meet the application requirements of high-power and high-frequency devices. Therefore, the research on KNN-based ceramics with high Qm is also of importance. Similar to hard-type PZT ceramics, acceptor doping with aliovalent dopants in KNN-based ceramics will result in the formation of charge-compensating oxygen vacancies and corresponding defect dipoles. Acceptor doping ions including ZnE^{2+}, CuE^{2+}, and FeE^{3+} have shown an effective \u201chardening\u201d effect on KNN-based ceramics with improved Qm [, \u2013]. However, the suppressed polarization rotation and domain wall motion reduce the piezoelectric activity, leading to a low d33. The trade-off between d33 and Qm is a longstanding challenge for KNN-based ceramics. Typically, in orthorhombic KNN ceramics, the addition of CuO leads to the incorporation of CuE^{2+} at B-site NbE^{5+}. In terms of the electromechanical properties, the addition of 1 mol% CuO into KNN significantly improves the Qm from 72 to 1965, however, the d33 decreases from 125 to 83 pC/N. Moreover, the role of acceptor doping is different in the KNN-based ceramics with different phase structures. Acceptor-doped KNN-based ceramics in pure O-phase tend to show a very high Qm of > 1000, but the d33 is usually less than 100 pC/N [, , , \u2013]. In the cases of R\u2013O [, ]/O\u2013T /R\u2013O\u2013T  multiphase coexistence, the d33 can remain a high value of > 150 pC/N after acceptor doping, but the improvement of Qm is unsatisfactory due to the intrinsic easy polarization rotation and active domain wall motion in the phase boundary region. It is difficult to realize a superior comprehensive electromechanical performance with d33 > 150 pC/N and Qm > 500 in KNN-based ceramics up to now. In addition to d33 and Qm, a high TC and favorable thermal stability are also desired in practical applications. Based on the above results, the direction of our exploration is to achieve high d33, Qm, TC, and good thermal stability simultaneously in KNN-based ceramics. In order to obtain a well-balanced performance of d33 and Qm, we select (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 as the matrix in this work and CuO is used as the acceptor dopant to modulate the microstructure and electromechanical properties. (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 is chosen for the following reasons. First, the introduction of LiE^{+} and TaE^{5+} into KNN has been indicated to lower the O\u2013T phase transition temperature, leading to the gradual increase of room-temperature d33 (200\u2013250 pC/N) without seriously sacrificing the TC (> 300 \xb0C) [, , , \u2013]. # CONCLUSION
In this work, CuO was doped into the preferable KNN-based lead-free system, (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 (LT-KNN), which locates near the O\u2013T phase boundary but in the O phase region. The introduction of 1 mol% CuO causes the greatly improved ceramic quality with high density and uniform grain growth, contributing to the decrease of dielectric loss and improvement of poling efficiency. Compared with the poor-quality LT-KNN ceramics, the CuO-modified LT-KNN ceramics display a higher d33. Moreover, the acceptor doping of CuE^{2+} instead of (Nb/Ta)E^{5+} promotes the generation of oxygen vacancies and defect dipoles, which have the pinning effect on the domain wall motion and lead to obvious increase of Qm. However, it is noted that excessive addition of CuO will lead to the decline of d33 and Qm due to the deteriorated ceramic quality and worse \u201chardening\u201d effect at a high CuO content. As a result, a superior comprehensive electromechanical performance with d33 = 180 pC/N, kp = 48% and Qm = 665 is achieved in the 1 mol % CuO doped LT-KNN ceramics. Moreover, the addition of CuO delays the O\u2013T phase transition while maintaining a high Tc of ~ 310 \xb0C. Combined with the pinning effect of internal bias field, the 1 mol% CuO modified LT-KNN ceramics show favorable d33 stability in the wide temperature range of 20\u2013280 \xb0C, and a high Qm of larger than 250 can be achieved until 100 \xb0C, demonstrating their good potential in high-temperature applications.

# INTRODUCTION
As a high-performance piezoelectric material, Lead Zirconate Titanate (Pb(Zr, Ti)O3, PZT) ceramics are widely used in electronic devices, such as dielectric capacitors, ultrasonic transducers, actuators and sensors. However, the main raw material PbO for preparing PZT is a volatile toxic substance, which might cause great harm to human health and environment. At present, lead-free systems mainly cover three categories: barium titanate [, \u2013] (BaTiO3, BT), potassium sodium niobate [, , \u2013] (K0.5Na0.5NbO3, KNN) and sodium bismuth titanate [, , \u2013] (Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3, NBT). For example, by the construction of rhombohedral\u2013orthorhombic (R\u2013O)  or orthorhombic\u2013tetragonal (O\u2013T) phase boundary [, ], the piezoelectric constant d33 of KNN-based ceramics can be improved to 200\u2212400 pC/N. In recent years, the creation of R\u2013T  or R\u2013O\u2013T [, ] new phase boundary, which is usually accompanied by the formation of nano-domains or local structure heterogeneity, can boost the d33 of BT and KNN-based ceramics to be higher than 500 pC/N. Very recently, Wu\u2019s group combined the effects of new phase boundary and multilayer composite, a high d33 of > 330 pC/N with the superior temperature stability over 25\u2013100 \xb0C was achieved in KNN-based ceramics. Moreover, some new physical phenomenon, such as nanoscale bubble domains with polar topologies in NBT-based bulk ferroelectrics , open up opportunity for advanced ferroelectric nanodevices. The rapid developments of lead-free piezoelectric systems enable them to replace traditional PZT and gain wide applications in the future. Among lead-free systems, KNN is most attractive due to its good piezoelectricity and high Curie temperature (TC > 300 \xb0C). On approaching the phase boundary, the piezoelectric activity of KNN-based ceramics will increase due to the enhanced polarization rotation (intrinsic contribution) and irreversible non-180\u02da domain wall motion (extrinsic contribution) arising from the flattened thermodynamic free energy profile and/or domain refinement. The optimized d33 is acquired in the compositions on the phase boundary, however, their mechanical quality factor Qm is usually very low (~ 30\u201350) owing to the significant internal friction [, , \u2013], which is difficult to meet the application requirements of high-power and high-frequency devices. Therefore, the research on KNN-based ceramics with high Qm is also of importance. Similar to hard-type PZT ceramics, acceptor doping with aliovalent dopants in KNN-based ceramics will result in the formation of charge-compensating oxygen vacancies and corresponding defect dipoles. Acceptor doping ions including ZnE^{2+}, CuE^{2+}, and FeE^{3+} have shown an effective \u201chardening\u201d effect on KNN-based ceramics with improved Qm [, \u2013]. However, the suppressed polarization rotation and domain wall motion reduce the piezoelectric activity, leading to a low d33. The trade-off between d33 and Qm is a longstanding challenge for KNN-based ceramics. Typically, in orthorhombic KNN ceramics, the addition of CuO leads to the incorporation of CuE^{2+} at B-site NbE^{5+}. In terms of the electromechanical properties, the addition of 1 mol% CuO into KNN significantly improves the Qm from 72 to 1965, however, the d33 decreases from 125 to 83 pC/N. Moreover, the role of acceptor doping is different in the KNN-based ceramics with different phase structures. Acceptor-doped KNN-based ceramics in pure O-phase tend to show a very high Qm of > 1000, but the d33 is usually less than 100 pC/N [, , , \u2013]. In the cases of R\u2013O [, ]/O\u2013T /R\u2013O\u2013T  multiphase coexistence, the d33 can remain a high value of > 150 pC/N after acceptor doping, but the improvement of Qm is unsatisfactory due to the intrinsic easy polarization rotation and active domain wall motion in the phase boundary region. It is difficult to realize a superior comprehensive electromechanical performance with d33 > 150 pC/N and Qm > 500 in KNN-based ceramics up to now. In addition to d33 and Qm, a high TC and favorable thermal stability are also desired in practical applications. Based on the above results, the direction of our exploration is to achieve high d33, Qm, TC, and good thermal stability simultaneously in KNN-based ceramics. In order to obtain a well-balanced performance of d33 and Qm, we select (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 as the matrix in this work and CuO is used as the acceptor dopant to modulate the microstructure and electromechanical properties. (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 is chosen for the following reasons. First, the introduction of LiE^{+} and TaE^{5+} into KNN has been indicated to lower the O\u2013T phase transition temperature, leading to the gradual increase of room-temperature d33 (200\u2013250 pC/N) without seriously sacrificing the TC (> 300 \xb0C) [, , , \u2013]. Especially, the composition (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 is near the O\u2013T phase boundary but in the O phase region at room temperature according to the phase diagram proposed by Saito et al.. Such a subtle control of composition is expected to ensure a favorable piezoelectricity of KNN-based matrix and in the meanwhile help the addition of CuO generate an apparent \u201chardening\u201d effect and effectively improve the Qm. On this basis, it is found that the addition of 1 mol% CuO into (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 can produce a high d33 ~ 180 pC/N, kp ~ 48%, and Qm ~ 665 simultaneously. Moreover, the TC is at a high level of 310 \xb0C with the variation of d33 less than 20% in the wide temperature range of 20\u2013280 \xb0C, and a high Qm of larger than 250 can be maintained until 100 \xb0C. # CONCLUSION
In this work, CuO was doped into the preferable KNN-based lead-free system, (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 (LT-KNN), which locates near the O\u2013T phase boundary but in the O phase region. The introduction of 1 mol% CuO causes the greatly improved ceramic quality with high density and uniform grain growth, contributing to the decrease of dielectric loss and improvement of poling efficiency. Compared with the poor-quality LT-KNN ceramics, the CuO-modified LT-KNN ceramics display a higher d33. Moreover, the acceptor doping of CuE^{2+} instead of (Nb/Ta)E^{5+} promotes the generation of oxygen vacancies and defect dipoles, which have the pinning effect on the domain wall motion and lead to obvious increase of Qm. However, it is noted that excessive addition of CuO will lead to the decline of d33 and Qm due to the deteriorated ceramic quality and worse \u201chardening\u201d effect at a high CuO content. As a result, a superior comprehensive electromechanical performance with d33 = 180 pC/N, kp = 48% and Qm = 665 is achieved in the 1 mol % CuO doped LT-KNN ceramics. Moreover, the addition of CuO delays the O\u2013T phase transition while maintaining a high Tc of ~ 310 \xb0C. Combined with the pinning effect of internal bias field, the 1 mol% CuO modified LT-KNN ceramics show favorable d33 stability in the wide temperature range of 20\u2013280 \xb0C, and a high Qm of larger than 250 can be achieved until 100 \xb0C, demonstrating their good potential in high-temperature applications.

# INTRODUCTION
As a high-performance piezoelectric material, Lead Zirconate Titanate (Pb(Zr, Ti)O3, PZT) ceramics are widely used in electronic devices, such as dielectric capacitors, ultrasonic transducers, actuators and sensors. However, the main raw material PbO for preparing PZT is a volatile toxic substance, which might cause great harm to human health and environment. At present, lead-free systems mainly cover three categories: barium titanate [, \u2013] (BaTiO3, BT), potassium sodium niobate [, , \u2013] (K0.5Na0.5NbO3, KNN) and sodium bismuth titanate [, , \u2013] (Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3, NBT). For example, by the construction of rhombohedral\u2013orthorhombic (R\u2013O)  or orthorhombic\u2013tetragonal (O\u2013T) phase boundary [, ], the piezoelectric constant d33 of KNN-based ceramics can be improved to 200\u2212400 pC/N. In recent years, the creation of R\u2013T  or R\u2013O\u2013T [, ] new phase boundary, which is usually accompanied by the formation of nano-domains or local structure heterogeneity, can boost the d33 of BT and KNN-based ceramics to be higher than 500 pC/N. Very recently, Wu\u2019s group combined the effects of new phase boundary and multilayer composite, a high d33 of > 330 pC/N with the superior temperature stability over 25\u2013100 \xb0C was achieved in KNN-based ceramics. Moreover, some new physical phenomenon, such as nanoscale bubble domains with polar topologies in NBT-based bulk ferroelectrics , open up opportunity for advanced ferroelectric nanodevices. The rapid developments of lead-free piezoelectric systems enable them to replace traditional PZT and gain wide applications in the future. Among lead-free systems, KNN is most attractive due to its good piezoelectricity and high Curie temperature (TC > 300 \xb0C). On approaching the phase boundary, the piezoelectric activity of KNN-based ceramics will increase due to the enhanced polarization rotation (intrinsic contribution) and irreversible non-180\u02da domain wall motion (extrinsic contribution) arising from the flattened thermodynamic free energy profile and/or domain refinement. The optimized d33 is acquired in the compositions on the phase boundary, however, their mechanical quality factor Qm is usually very low (~ 30\u201350) owing to the significant internal friction [, , \u2013], which is difficult to meet the application requirements of high-power and high-frequency devices. Therefore, the research on KNN-based ceramics with high Qm is also of importance. Similar to hard-type PZT ceramics, acceptor doping with aliovalent dopants in KNN-based ceramics will result in the formation of charge-compensating oxygen vacancies and corresponding defect dipoles. Acceptor doping ions including ZnE^{2+}, CuE^{2+}, and FeE^{3+} have shown an effective \u201chardening\u201d effect on KNN-based ceramics with improved Qm [, \u2013]. However, the suppressed polarization rotation and domain wall motion reduce the piezoelectric activity, leading to a low d33. The trade-off between d33 and Qm is a longstanding challenge for KNN-based ceramics. Typically, in orthorhombic KNN ceramics, the addition of CuO leads to the incorporation of CuE^{2+} at B-site NbE^{5+}. In terms of the electromechanical properties, the addition of 1 mol% CuO into KNN significantly improves the Qm from 72 to 1965, however, the d33 decreases from 125 to 83 pC/N. Moreover, the role of acceptor doping is different in the KNN-based ceramics with different phase structures. Acceptor-doped KNN-based ceramics in pure O-phase tend to show a very high Qm of > 1000, but the d33 is usually less than 100 pC/N [, , , \u2013]. In the cases of R\u2013O [, ]/O\u2013T /R\u2013O\u2013T  multiphase coexistence, the d33 can remain a high value of > 150 pC/N after acceptor doping, but the improvement of Qm is unsatisfactory due to the intrinsic easy polarization rotation and active domain wall motion in the phase boundary region. It is difficult to realize a superior comprehensive electromechanical performance with d33 > 150 pC/N and Qm > 500 in KNN-based ceramics up to now. In addition to d33 and Qm, a high TC and favorable thermal stability are also desired in practical applications. Based on the above results, the direction of our exploration is to achieve high d33, Qm, TC, and good thermal stability simultaneously in KNN-based ceramics. In order to obtain a well-balanced performance of d33 and Qm, we select (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 as the matrix in this work and CuO is used as the acceptor dopant to modulate the microstructure and electromechanical properties. (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 is chosen for the following reasons. First, the introduction of LiE^{+} and TaE^{5+} into KNN has been indicated to lower the O\u2013T phase transition temperature, leading to the gradual increase of room-temperature d33 (200\u2013250 pC/N) without seriously sacrificing the TC (> 300 \xb0C) [, , , \u2013]. Especially, the composition (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 is near the O\u2013T phase boundary but in the O phase region at room temperature according to the phase diagram proposed by Saito et al.. Such a subtle control of composition is expected to ensure a favorable piezoelectricity of KNN-based matrix and in the meanwhile help the addition of CuO generate an apparent \u201chardening\u201d effect and effectively improve the Qm. On this basis, it is found that the addition of 1 mol% CuO into (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 can produce a high d33 ~ 180 pC/N, kp ~ 48%, and Qm ~ 665 simultaneously. Moreover, the TC is at a high level of 310 \xb0C with the variation of d33 less than 20% in the wide temperature range of 20\u2013280 \xb0C, and a high Qm of larger than 250 can be maintained until 100 \xb0C. # CONCLUSION
In this work, CuO was doped into the preferable KNN-based lead-free system, (K0.5Na0.5)0.98Li0.02Nb0.82Ta0.18O3 (LT-KNN), which locates near the O\u2013T phase boundary but in the O phase region. The introduction of 1 mol% CuO causes the greatly improved ceramic quality with high density and uniform grain growth, contributing to the decrease of dielectric loss and improvement of poling efficiency. Compared with the poor-quality LT-KNN ceramics, the CuO-modified LT-KNN ceramics display a higher d33. Moreover, the acceptor doping of CuE^{2+} instead of (Nb/Ta)E^{5+} promotes the generation of oxygen vacancies and defect dipoles, which have the pinning effect on the domain wall motion and lead to obvious increase of Qm. However, it is noted that excessive addition of CuO will lead to the decline of d33 and Qm due to the deteriorated ceramic quality and worse \u201chardening\u201d effect at a high CuO content. As a result, a superior comprehensive electromechanical performance with d33 = 180 pC/N, kp = 48% and Qm = 665 is achieved in the 1 mol % CuO doped LT-KNN ceramics. Moreover, the addition of CuO delays the O\u2013T phase transition while maintaining a high Tc of ~ 310 \xb0C. Combined with the pinning effect of internal bias field, the 1 mol% CuO modified LT-KNN ceramics show favorable d33 stability in the wide temperature range of 20\u2013280 \xb0C, and a high Qm of larger than 250 can be achieved until 100 \xb0C, demonstrating their good potential in high-temperature applications.
