# INTRODUCTION
Pb(Zr, Ti)O3-based ceramics with excellent electrical properties have been widely utilized in various electronic and microelectronic devices, e.g., sensors, actuators, and ultrasonic transducers [, ]. Because of the strong ferroelectricity with large remnant polarization of 38 \u03bcC/cmE^{2} , Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 (BNT) ceramics are regarded as one of the most potential candidates for lead-free piezoelectric materials. However, the performance of pure BNT ceramics is still not enough for practical applications. For example, because of the high coercive field of 7.3 kV/mm  and high electrical conductivity, it is difficult to pole pure BNT ceramics. In order to improve the electrical properties of BNT and increase the possibility of practicability, several kinds of attempts have been employed to improve the electrical properties of BNT ceramics, including ion substitution and solid solution [, , , \u2013]. Among these attempts, ion substitutions have been utilized to modify electrical properties of BNT ceramics [, , ]. Previously, it was reported that the addition of Li can promote the electrical properties of BNT ceramics [, ]. However, only limited investigations on the effects of Hf on the physical properties of BNT ceramics were reported. As a result, it is highly expected to study the influences of Li and Hf on the structure and properties of BNT-based ceramics. In this work, HfE^{4+} and LiE^{+} were added to BNT ceramics [(Bi0.5Na0.5\u2212yLiy)(Ti1\u2212xHfx)O3 (BNLyTHx)] in order to improve the electrical properties, and the influences of Li and Hf contents on the structure and electrical properties were importantly investigated.

# CONCLUSION
Here, we investigated the effects of Hf and Li on the structure and electrical properties of BNT ceramics, and then the addition of appropriate Hf and Li can benefit the improvement of piezoelectric and dielectric properties. As a result, our investigation can further understand the effects of site engineering (Hf and Li) on structure and properties of BNT materials.

# INTRODUCTION
Pb(Zr, Ti)O3-based ceramics with excellent electrical properties have been widely utilized in various electronic and microelectronic devices, e.g., sensors, actuators, and ultrasonic transducers [, ]. Because of the strong ferroelectricity with large remnant polarization of 38 \u03bcC/cmE^{2} , Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 (BNT) ceramics are regarded as one of the most potential candidates for lead-free piezoelectric materials. However, the performance of pure BNT ceramics is still not enough for practical applications. For example, because of the high coercive field of 7.3 kV/mm  and high electrical conductivity, it is difficult to pole pure BNT ceramics. In order to improve the electrical properties of BNT and increase the possibility of practicability, several kinds of attempts have been employed to improve the electrical properties of BNT ceramics, including ion substitution and solid solution [, , , \u2013]. Among these attempts, ion substitutions have been utilized to modify electrical properties of BNT ceramics [, , ]. Previously, it was reported that the addition of Li can promote the electrical properties of BNT ceramics [, ]. However, only limited investigations on the effects of Hf on the physical properties of BNT ceramics were reported. As a result, it is highly expected to study the influences of Li and Hf on the structure and properties of BNT-based ceramics. In this work, HfE^{4+} and LiE^{+} were added to BNT ceramics [(Bi0.5Na0.5\u2212yLiy)(Ti1\u2212xHfx)O3 (BNLyTHx)] in order to improve the electrical properties, and the influences of Li and Hf contents on the structure and electrical properties were importantly investigated.

# CONCLUSION
Here, we investigated the effects of Hf and Li on the structure and electrical properties of BNT ceramics, and then the addition of appropriate Hf and Li can benefit the improvement of piezoelectric and dielectric properties. As a result, our investigation can further understand the effects of site engineering (Hf and Li) on structure and properties of BNT materials.
