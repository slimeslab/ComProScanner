# TITLE
Achieving superior energy harvesting performance in Sr-doped (Pb,La,Sb)(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics based on optimization of FOM

# ABSTRACT
The piezoelectric circular diaphragms (PCDs) applied to energy harvesters have great application potential for replacing chemical batteries to power microelectronic devices. However, the output power of PCDs is highly restricted by the value of E^{2}/ (defined as FOM) of selected materials. Herein, the (PbLaSbSr)(ZrTi)O (abbreviated as PLSSZT,  = 0–0.06) ceramics prepared by the traditional solid-state method are reported. The results show that when  = 0.02, a morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) with a coexistence of rhombohedral (R) and tetragonal (T) phases is found which greatly optimizes the comprehensive properties of the ceramics (i.e.,  ~ 850 pC/N,  ~ 62%, strain ~ 0.27% at 40 kV/cm). Meanwhile, the ceramics show a relatively low  of 3700 under 10 kHz, thus a large FOM can be achieved. Therefore, the PCD based on the (PbLaSbSr)(ZrTi)O ceramics possesses a high output power of 8.3 mW under the 15 g proof mass, and can drive multiple LEDs, which is of reference significance for promoting the development of energy harvesters.

# INTRODUCTION
At present, with the vigorous development of the micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS), countless numbers of wireless sensor networks will be operated for continuous monitoring, detection, and communication [, \u2013]. Applying chemical batteries is a common method which, however, is restricted for its limited life span, environmental pollution after being discarded, and unreliability in harsh environment [, \u2013]. Based on the piezoelectric effect, piezoelectric energy harvesters (PEHs) can convert mechanical energy which is ubiquitous in the environment into electrical energy. Since PEH can be miniaturized and integrated into complex structures easily, it has become a promising candidate for using in sensors, monitors, detectors, and so on [, , ]. For a PEH, when an external stress is pressed, the generating output power (P) is highly affected by the piezoelectric coefficient (d33) and the dielectric constant (\u03b5r) of materials, which is given by the following equation:1P=d\xb7F\xb7f\xb7\u03b4/\u03b5r\xb7A,E^{33}E^{2}E^{2}\\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \\usepackage{amsmath} \\usepackage{wasysym} \\usepackage{amsfonts} \\usepackage{amssymb} \\usepackage{amsbsy} \\usepackage{mathrsfs} \\usepackage{upgreek} \\setlength{\\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \\begin{document}$$P = d_{{33}} ^{2} \\cdot F^{2} \\cdot f \\cdot {\\delta \\mathord{\\left/ {\\vphantom {\\delta {\\left( {\\varepsilon _{r} \\cdot A} \\right)}}} \\right. \\kern-\\nulldelimiterspace} {\\left( {\\varepsilon _{r} \\cdot A} \\right)}},$$\\end{document} where F, f, \u03b4, and A represent the pressed external stress, vibration frequency, thickness, and the area of piezoelectric element. According to the equation, the output power is proportional to the value of d33E^{2}/\u03b5r, which can be defined as the figure of merit (FOM) [, ]. For enlarging FOM, it is more conducive to obtain a high value of d33 because it governs the power generation capacity of PEHs [, ]. In recent years, many researches have been carried out on enhancing the d33 by doping. However, it is not suitable for energy harvesting to blindly increase the value of d33, because the ultrahigh \u03b5r can be achieved at the same time it will severely limit the enhancement of FOM. For example, Li et al. prepared Sm-doped Pb(Mg1/3Nb2/3)O3\u2013PbTiO3 (PMN\u2013PT) ceramics which showed an ultrahigh d33 value of 1500 pC/N, while \u03b5r was also high as 13,000. Guo et al. introduced EuE^{3+} into PMN\u2013PT and achieved an ultrahigh d33 of 1420 pC/N and a \u03b5r of 12,200. However, the Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT)-based piezoceramics which can satisfy the demand of having a high d33 and a relatively low \u03b5r simultaneously are fit for PEHs to harvest energy [, , \u2013]. In this case, the hard PZT-based piezoceramics are not appropriate because of their low value of d33, for example, ceramics of PZT-4 show a d33 of ~ 315 pC/N, PZT-82 of ~ 265 pC/N, PZT-8 of ~ 210 pC/N [, \u2013]. Similar to the PZT-5H, the soft PZT-based piezoceramics show a d33 of ~ 550 pC/N, a \u03b5r of 3000. Similar to the PZT-51, the soft PZT-based piezoceramics show a d33 of ~ 380 pC/N, a \u03b5r 1500. Hence, the soft PZT-based piezoceramics are more worthy of choice [, \u2013]. On the basis of using soft PZT-based piezoceramics, it is a challenging task to increase d33 while balancing \u03b5r to efficiently provide more power for piezoelectric energy harvesters. Rai and Sharma investigated that the pair doping of LaE^{3+} and SbE^{3+} in PZT leads to a diffuse phase transition and the increasing of electrical properties. Yufeng et al. synthesized the Pb0.94La0.06(Zr0.58Ti0.42)O3 ceramics with 1 wt% Sb2O3 addition, and a high d33 of 800 pC/N and a \u03b5r of 4700 are obtained. In addition, to some extent, the doping of SrE^{2+} can restrict the increase of \u03b5r, which is shown by Liu et al.. In this work, the (Pb0.925\u2212xLa0.04Sb0.035Srx)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics were prepared to optimize the energy harvesting performance of the piezoceramics. When doped with 2 mol% SrE^{2+}, the phase structure is near the MPB, showing a mixed R\u2013T phase, and a high remnant polarization (Pr) of 36.4 \xb5C/cmE^{2} and a high saturated polarization (Ps) of 45.9 \xb5C/cmE^{2} are achieved simultaneously. Therefore, excellent piezoelectric properties are promoted (~ 850 pC/N). Meanwhile, the value of \u03b5r is relatively low which is about 3700 under 10 kHz at room temperature. The high value of d33 and relatively low \u03b5r bring the (Pb0.905La0.04Sb0.035Sr0.02)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics excellent energy harvesting performance. Hence, the PEHs manufactured by the PLSSZT ceramics with SrE^{2+} = 2 mol% exhibit an excellent output power property of 8.3 mW under the resonant frequency, and can drive multiple LEDs. The result shows that the output power is proportional to the FOM value which is verified by assessing the performance of PCDs of four components with different d33 and \u03b5r, demonstrating that the optimization of d33E^{2}/\u03b5r is highly effective in improving energy harvesting.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, the (Pb0.925\u2212xLa0.04Sb0.035Srx)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics with pure perovskite were prepared by traditional solid-state reaction methods. With the increase of SrE^{2+} content, the Curie temperature moves to room temperature, which leads to the \u03b5r increasing in the PLSSZT ceramics. XRD results reveal that when x = 0.02, the phase structure shows a coexistence R\u2013T phase close to MPB, which greatly promotes the polarization characteristics of the PLSSZT ceramics. Therefore, a high Pr of 36.4 \xb5C/cmE^{2}, Ps of 45.9 \xb5C/cmE^{2} are achieved at x = 0.02. As a consequence, the (Pb0.905La0.04Sb0.035Sr0.02)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics show a high d33 of ~ 850 pC/N with kp of ~ 62% and strain of ~ 0.27% at 40 kV/cm. Furthermore, the piezoelectric energy harvester based on the (Pb0.905La0.04Sb0.035Sr0.02)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics displays a high output voltage of 11.2 V and power of 8.3 mW, and can drive multiple LEDs. By assessing the performance of PCDs of four components with different d33 and \u03b5r, it is shown that the piezoelectric energy harvesting performance is proportional to the value of FOM. This work not only promotes the development of piezoelectric energy devices, but also further verifies the evaluability of FOM value in piezoelectric energy harvesting performance.

# TITLE
Achieving superior energy harvesting performance in Sr-doped (Pb,La,Sb)(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics based on optimization of FOM

# ABSTRACT
The piezoelectric circular diaphragms (PCDs) applied to energy harvesters have great application potential for replacing chemical batteries to power microelectronic devices. However, the output power of PCDs is highly restricted by the value of E^{2}/ (defined as FOM) of selected materials. Herein, the (PbLaSbSr)(ZrTi)O (abbreviated as PLSSZT,  = 0–0.06) ceramics prepared by the traditional solid-state method are reported. The results show that when  = 0.02, a morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) with a coexistence of rhombohedral (R) and tetragonal (T) phases is found which greatly optimizes the comprehensive properties of the ceramics (i.e.,  ~ 850 pC/N,  ~ 62%, strain ~ 0.27% at 40 kV/cm). Meanwhile, the ceramics show a relatively low  of 3700 under 10 kHz, thus a large FOM can be achieved. Therefore, the PCD based on the (PbLaSbSr)(ZrTi)O ceramics possesses a high output power of 8.3 mW under the 15 g proof mass, and can drive multiple LEDs, which is of reference significance for promoting the development of energy harvesters.

# INTRODUCTION
At present, with the vigorous development of the micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS), countless numbers of wireless sensor networks will be operated for continuous monitoring, detection, and communication [, \u2013]. Applying chemical batteries is a common method which, however, is restricted for its limited life span, environmental pollution after being discarded, and unreliability in harsh environment [, \u2013]. Based on the piezoelectric effect, piezoelectric energy harvesters (PEHs) can convert mechanical energy which is ubiquitous in the environment into electrical energy. Since PEH can be miniaturized and integrated into complex structures easily, it has become a promising candidate for using in sensors, monitors, detectors, and so on [, , ]. For a PEH, when an external stress is pressed, the generating output power (P) is highly affected by the piezoelectric coefficient (d33) and the dielectric constant (\u03b5r) of materials, which is given by the following equation:1P=d\xb7F\xb7f\xb7\u03b4/\u03b5r\xb7A,E^{33}E^{2}E^{2}\\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \\usepackage{amsmath} \\usepackage{wasysym} \\usepackage{amsfonts} \\usepackage{amssymb} \\usepackage{amsbsy} \\usepackage{mathrsfs} \\usepackage{upgreek} \\setlength{\\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \\begin{document}$$P = d_{{33}} ^{2} \\cdot F^{2} \\cdot f \\cdot {\\delta \\mathord{\\left/ {\\vphantom {\\delta {\\left( {\\varepsilon _{r} \\cdot A} \\right)}}} \\right. \\kern-\\nulldelimiterspace} {\\left( {\\varepsilon _{r} \\cdot A} \\right)}},$$\\end{document} where F, f, \u03b4, and A represent the pressed external stress, vibration frequency, thickness, and the area of piezoelectric element. According to the equation, the output power is proportional to the value of d33E^{2}/\u03b5r, which can be defined as the figure of merit (FOM) [, ]. For enlarging FOM, it is more conducive to obtain a high value of d33 because it governs the power generation capacity of PEHs [, ]. In recent years, many researches have been carried out on enhancing the d33 by doping. However, it is not suitable for energy harvesting to blindly increase the value of d33, because the ultrahigh \u03b5r can be achieved at the same time it will severely limit the enhancement of FOM. For example, Li et al. prepared Sm-doped Pb(Mg1/3Nb2/3)O3\u2013PbTiO3 (PMN\u2013PT) ceramics which showed an ultrahigh d33 value of 1500 pC/N, while \u03b5r was also high as 13,000. Guo et al. introduced EuE^{3+} into PMN\u2013PT and achieved an ultrahigh d33 of 1420 pC/N and a \u03b5r of 12,200. However, the Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT)-based piezoceramics which can satisfy the demand of having a high d33 and a relatively low \u03b5r simultaneously are fit for PEHs to harvest energy [, , \u2013]. In this case, the hard PZT-based piezoceramics are not appropriate because of their low value of d33, for example, ceramics of PZT-4 show a d33 of ~ 315 pC/N, PZT-82 of ~ 265 pC/N, PZT-8 of ~ 210 pC/N [, \u2013]. Similar to the PZT-5H, the soft PZT-based piezoceramics show a d33 of ~ 550 pC/N, a \u03b5r of 3000. Similar to the PZT-51, the soft PZT-based piezoceramics show a d33 of ~ 380 pC/N, a \u03b5r 1500. Hence, the soft PZT-based piezoceramics are more worthy of choice [, \u2013]. On the basis of using soft PZT-based piezoceramics, it is a challenging task to increase d33 while balancing \u03b5r to efficiently provide more power for piezoelectric energy harvesters. Rai and Sharma investigated that the pair doping of LaE^{3+} and SbE^{3+} in PZT leads to a diffuse phase transition and the increasing of electrical properties. Yufeng et al. synthesized the Pb0.94La0.06(Zr0.58Ti0.42)O3 ceramics with 1 wt% Sb2O3 addition, and a high d33 of 800 pC/N and a \u03b5r of 4700 are obtained. In addition, to some extent, the doping of SrE^{2+} can restrict the increase of \u03b5r, which is shown by Liu et al.. In this work, the (Pb0.925\u2212xLa0.04Sb0.035Srx)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics were prepared to optimize the energy harvesting performance of the piezoceramics. When doped with 2 mol% SrE^{2+}, the phase structure is near the MPB, showing a mixed R\u2013T phase, and a high remnant polarization (Pr) of 36.4 \xb5C/cmE^{2} and a high saturated polarization (Ps) of 45.9 \xb5C/cmE^{2} are achieved simultaneously. Therefore, excellent piezoelectric properties are promoted (~ 850 pC/N). Meanwhile, the value of \u03b5r is relatively low which is about 3700 under 10 kHz at room temperature. The high value of d33 and relatively low \u03b5r bring the (Pb0.905La0.04Sb0.035Sr0.02)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics excellent energy harvesting performance. Hence, the PEHs manufactured by the PLSSZT ceramics with SrE^{2+} = 2 mol% exhibit an excellent output power property of 8.3 mW under the resonant frequency, and can drive multiple LEDs. The result shows that the output power is proportional to the FOM value which is verified by assessing the performance of PCDs of four components with different d33 and \u03b5r, demonstrating that the optimization of d33E^{2}/\u03b5r is highly effective in improving energy harvesting.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, the (Pb0.925\u2212xLa0.04Sb0.035Srx)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics with pure perovskite were prepared by traditional solid-state reaction methods. With the increase of SrE^{2+} content, the Curie temperature moves to room temperature, which leads to the \u03b5r increasing in the PLSSZT ceramics. XRD results reveal that when x = 0.02, the phase structure shows a coexistence R\u2013T phase close to MPB, which greatly promotes the polarization characteristics of the PLSSZT ceramics. Therefore, a high Pr of 36.4 \xb5C/cmE^{2}, Ps of 45.9 \xb5C/cmE^{2} are achieved at x = 0.02. As a consequence, the (Pb0.905La0.04Sb0.035Sr0.02)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics show a high d33 of ~ 850 pC/N with kp of ~ 62% and strain of ~ 0.27% at 40 kV/cm. Furthermore, the piezoelectric energy harvester based on the (Pb0.905La0.04Sb0.035Sr0.02)(Zr0.55Ti0.45)0.99O3 ceramics displays a high output voltage of 11.2 V and power of 8.3 mW, and can drive multiple LEDs. By assessing the performance of PCDs of four components with different d33 and \u03b5r, it is shown that the piezoelectric energy harvesting performance is proportional to the value of FOM. This work not only promotes the development of piezoelectric energy devices, but also further verifies the evaluability of FOM value in piezoelectric energy harvesting performance.
