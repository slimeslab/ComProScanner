# INTRODUCTION
For many decades, lead zirconium titanate Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT) has been one of the most widely used piezoelectric materials in electronic industry, due to its high piezoelectric properties close to a morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) between tetragonal and rhombohedral phases. However in recent times, due to environmental concerns about the harmful effects of lead, there has been a strong urge to find lead-free alternatives to PZT which has lead content of about 60wt%. As a consequence, efforts have been made towards improving the piezoelectric properties of various lead-free systems such as BaTiO3 (BT) , KNaNbO3 , Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 , (Bi0.5Na0.5)TiO3\u2013BaTiO3 and (Ba0.95Sr0.05)(Zr0.05Ti0.95)O3. Among all the lead-free alternatives, potassium sodium niobate [(K,Na)NbO3] system is considered to be one of the best possible candidates because of its relatively high Curie temperature (T c), good ferroelectric properties and high electromechanical coupling coefficients, especially when produced by hot pressing or other unconventional sintering techniques like spark plasma sintering (SPS) and cold isostatic pressing (CIP). The hot pressed (K,Na)NbO3 ceramics have been reported to possess high T c (420\xb0C), \u03c1 (4.46g/cmE^{3}), d 33 (160pC/N) and a large electromechanical coupling coefficient k p (0.45). However, their electrical properties were degraded (d 33~80pC/N and k p ~0.36) in conventional sintering due to the highly volatile nature of the alkali members present in the composition that leads to poor densification and disorder in the stoichiometric ratio. Improvements in densification and piezoelectric properties have been observed for potassium sodium niobate system modified with LiNbO3 , LiTaO3 , SrTiO3 , BiFeO3 , LiSbO3 and CaTiO3. Among the potassium sodium niobate based contenders, the composition K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (KNN) has been paid much attention since the discovery of KNN-based textured ceramics with properties comparable to those of unmodified PZT ceramics. Combined modification of KNN ceramics with Li, Ta, and Sb has yielded high d 33 of 400pC/N which is much higher than that of unmodified KNN (80pC/N). KNN ceramics modified with Li and Sb have been reported to produce various changes in their electrical and piezoelectric properties. Since the Pauling electronegativity of Sb is much higher than that of Nb (2.05 versus 1.5), partial substitution of SbE^{5+} for NbE^{5+} induces a strong covalency in the KNN perovskite, which promotes ferroelectricity and piezoelectricity. Detailed literature survey on Sb modified KNN ceramics (K0.5Na0.5) (Nb1\u2212x Sb x )O3 shows that the optimum piezoelectric properties occur for SbE^{5+} content of 4mol%. There is however a lack of systematic study about the effect of Li on the dielectric and piezoelectric properties of this particular composition, (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3.

# CONCLUSION
In the present work, alkaline niobate based lead-free (K0.5Na0.5)1\u2212x Li x (Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3 piezoceramics with x=0, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04, 0.05, and 0.06 were synthesized by conventional ceramic processing using nanoscale powders. The phase transition temperatures exhibited strong dependence on the Li content, with T c value shifting from 315\xb0C to 332\xb0C and T ot from 165\xb0C to 133\xb0C as Li content increases from 0 to 0.06. The bulk density, room temperature dielectric constant, remnant polarization and piezoelectric charge coefficient were found to be optimum for the ceramic composition (K0.49Na0.49Li0.02)(Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3, with the values 4.38g/cmE^{3}, 445, 14.9\u03bcC/cmE^{2} and 180pC/N respectively. A homogeneous grain size distribution with the average grain size of about 1.6\u03bcm was observed for this composition. The lead-free (K0.5Na0.5)1\u2212x Li x (Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3 ceramics modified with optimum Li content and prepared from nanocrystalline powders can thus result in, without the use of any sintering agent, improved dielectric, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties of the KNN system.

# INTRODUCTION
For many decades, lead zirconium titanate Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT) has been one of the most widely used piezoelectric materials in electronic industry, due to its high piezoelectric properties close to a morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) between tetragonal and rhombohedral phases. However in recent times, due to environmental concerns about the harmful effects of lead, there has been a strong urge to find lead-free alternatives to PZT which has lead content of about 60wt%. As a consequence, efforts have been made towards improving the piezoelectric properties of various lead-free systems such as BaTiO3 (BT) , KNaNbO3 , Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 , (Bi0.5Na0.5)TiO3\u2013BaTiO3 and (Ba0.95Sr0.05)(Zr0.05Ti0.95)O3. Among all the lead-free alternatives, potassium sodium niobate [(K,Na)NbO3] system is considered to be one of the best possible candidates because of its relatively high Curie temperature (T c), good ferroelectric properties and high electromechanical coupling coefficients, especially when produced by hot pressing or other unconventional sintering techniques like spark plasma sintering (SPS) and cold isostatic pressing (CIP). The hot pressed (K,Na)NbO3 ceramics have been reported to possess high T c (420\xb0C), \u03c1 (4.46g/cmE^{3}), d 33 (160pC/N) and a large electromechanical coupling coefficient k p (0.45). However, their electrical properties were degraded (d 33~80pC/N and k p ~0.36) in conventional sintering due to the highly volatile nature of the alkali members present in the composition that leads to poor densification and disorder in the stoichiometric ratio. Improvements in densification and piezoelectric properties have been observed for potassium sodium niobate system modified with LiNbO3 , LiTaO3 , SrTiO3 , BiFeO3 , LiSbO3 and CaTiO3. Among the potassium sodium niobate based contenders, the composition K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (KNN) has been paid much attention since the discovery of KNN-based textured ceramics with properties comparable to those of unmodified PZT ceramics. Combined modification of KNN ceramics with Li, Ta, and Sb has yielded high d 33 of 400pC/N which is much higher than that of unmodified KNN (80pC/N). KNN ceramics modified with Li and Sb have been reported to produce various changes in their electrical and piezoelectric properties. Since the Pauling electronegativity of Sb is much higher than that of Nb (2.05 versus 1.5), partial substitution of SbE^{5+} for NbE^{5+} induces a strong covalency in the KNN perovskite, which promotes ferroelectricity and piezoelectricity. Detailed literature survey on Sb modified KNN ceramics (K0.5Na0.5) (Nb1\u2212x Sb x )O3 shows that the optimum piezoelectric properties occur for SbE^{5+} content of 4mol%. There is however a lack of systematic study about the effect of Li on the dielectric and piezoelectric properties of this particular composition, (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3.

# CONCLUSION
In the present work, alkaline niobate based lead-free (K0.5Na0.5)1\u2212x Li x (Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3 piezoceramics with x=0, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04, 0.05, and 0.06 were synthesized by conventional ceramic processing using nanoscale powders. The phase transition temperatures exhibited strong dependence on the Li content, with T c value shifting from 315\xb0C to 332\xb0C and T ot from 165\xb0C to 133\xb0C as Li content increases from 0 to 0.06. The bulk density, room temperature dielectric constant, remnant polarization and piezoelectric charge coefficient were found to be optimum for the ceramic composition (K0.49Na0.49Li0.02)(Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3, with the values 4.38g/cmE^{3}, 445, 14.9\u03bcC/cmE^{2} and 180pC/N respectively. A homogeneous grain size distribution with the average grain size of about 1.6\u03bcm was observed for this composition. The lead-free (K0.5Na0.5)1\u2212x Li x (Nb0.96Sb0.04)O3 ceramics modified with optimum Li content and prepared from nanocrystalline powders can thus result in, without the use of any sintering agent, improved dielectric, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties of the KNN system.
