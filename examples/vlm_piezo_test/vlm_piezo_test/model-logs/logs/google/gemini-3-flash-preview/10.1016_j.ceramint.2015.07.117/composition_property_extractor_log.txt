# INTRODUCTION
Among these ceramics, K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (abbreviated as KNN) is considered to be one of the most promising candidates for lead-free piezoelectric ceramics. KNN is a solid solution of ferroelectric KNbO3 and antiferroelectric NaNbO3 ; near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB), KNN exhibits ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties that are enhanced relative to those of pure KNbO3. Currently, an increasing number of researchers believe that the existence of polymorphic phase transitions (PPT) also contributes to the good piezoelectric properties at room temperature exhibited by KNN. Nevertheless, one of the main obstacles for the development of KNN ceramics is the difficulty in processing and densification. It is usually very difficult to obtain dense and well-sintered pure KNN ceramics by conventional sintering methods because the alkaline constituent elements of KNN are highly volatile at the higher temperatures required for the sintering of KNN. To obtain dense KNN ceramics, a number of investigations have focused on improving sintering activity. One approach is to synthesize fine KNN powder precursors to enhance the sintering activity to take advantage of the inverse proportionality between the driving force for sintering and the grain size. To reduce the grain size, sol-gel routine, molten salt synthesis, hydrothermal and microwave\u2013hydrothermal (MH) methods have been used to prepare KNN powders. In our previous work , fine powders were prepared by using the hydrothermal method at 230\xb0C for 24h. On the contrary, crystalline and homogeneous KNN powders were successfully prepared at 160\xb0C for 7h by using the microwave\u2013hydrothermal method. The advantages of an lower temperature and a shorter holding time than those required in the hydrothermal method make the microwave\u2013hydrothermal method much more attractive for synthesis of KNN powders with a small grain size. It is well known that Ta ions possess the same chemical valence (+5) and similar atomic radius (0.068nm for Ta compared with 0.069nm for Nb) as Nb ions. To improve the piezoelectric properties of KNN ceramics, some studies have examined doping Ta ions into a KNN solid solution, partially substituting NbE^{5+}. Saito et al. found that the highest piezoelectric d33 constant in the (K, Na)NbO3\u2013LiTaO3 system was 300 pC/N, with texturing the materials resulting in an enhanced value of d33=416 pC/N. Lv et al. studied the (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb1\u2212x Ta x )O3 (KNNT) ceramics system. It was found that Ta substitution decreases both T C and T O\u2013T, but increases T R\u2013O. The ceramics with x=0.30 show a high value of piezoelectric coefficient with d33=205 pC/N. Matsubara et al. have also shown that (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb0.7Ta0.3)O3 ceramics exhibit an enhanced d33 value. In our previous studies , it was found that the value of d33 increased with the increasing amount of Ta-doping (x\u22640.3), and the highest d33 value of 210 pC/N was obtained for (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb0.7Ta0.3)O3 ceramics. Thus, KNN ceramics with Ta-doping synthesized from KNNT powders obtained by MH have been shown to exhibit enhanced piezoelectric property. In this paper, (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb1\u2212x Ta x )O3 (0\u2264x\u22640.4) powders were successfully synthesized by the microwave\u2013hydrothermal method, and (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb1\u2212x Ta x )O3 ceramics were sintered under atmospheric pressure using an ordinary sintering method.

# CONCLUSION
Dispersive, homogeneous and highly crystalline K0.5Na0.5Nb1\u2212x Ta x O3 (x=0.2, 0.3, 0.4) powders have been successfully synthesized by a two-stage MH synthesis method. The first stage of the reaction was performed at 130\xb0C for 2h, and then, the temperature was raised to 180\xb0C for 7.5h. The results show that the pure K0.5Na0.5Nb1\u2212x Ta x O3 and particles with small sizes close to 500\u2013800nm were obtained. The dense K0.5Na0.5Nb1\u2212x Ta x O3 ceramics were obtained by sintering at 1090\xb0C for 2h under normal atmospheric conditions. Following polarization, it was observed that the d33 piezoelectric coefficient increased with the increasing Ta content and reached its highest value of 233 pC/N when x=0.3; this can be attributed to Ta doping and the better morphology of the initial crystallite powders synthesized by the two-stage MH method. Moreover, the two-stage MH method is simple and efficient and may be an attractive route for preparing other piezoelectric ceramics.

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
To synthesize pure perovskite KNNT powders, MH experiments were carried out at different temperatures (170\xb0C, 180\xb0C and 190\xb0C) for 7.5h with an alkali concentration of 6mol/L. The XRD patterns of the obtained KNNT powders are shown in ; (b) is the magnification of (a) in the 2\u03b8=43\u201348\xb0 range. It can be observed that all of the samples exhibit a monoclinic perovskite structure (JCPDS Card no. 01-077-0038). However, both the KNNT and Ta2O5 phase peaks are observed in the patterns of prepared powders, indicating that although the KNNT powders were successfully synthesized, Ta2O5 was not sufficiently reacted at the increased temperatures; this was also confirmed by the EDS results ( ). shows that the actual Ta-doping amount is much less than designed, indicating that an unreacted Ta2O5 phase is present in the obtained powders. In addition, as shown in , the Ta2O5 peaks gradually weakened and the KNNT diffraction peaks at approximately 45\xb0 slightly shifted to the right with increasing temperature ((b)) due to the substitution of Nb (0.069nm) by Ta (0.068nm). This may indicate that a higher temperature can enhance the synthesis of the KNNT powders by facilitating Ta dissolving into the KNNT crystal lattice. shows the SEM images of KNNT powders obtained at different reaction temperatures. The cubic-like particles become larger with the increasing reaction temperature, indicating that the higher temperatures are beneficial for the synthesis of KNNT powder; these results are in agreement with the XRD results. Although well crystallized KNNT grains were obtained at 170\xb0C and 190\xb0C, the phenomenon of agglomeration was still serious. However, well-dispersed powders were obtained for a reaction temperature of 180\xb0C ((b)). Moreover, the amount of Ta-doping at 180\xb0C is larger than that at 170\xb0C and is similar to that at 190\xb0C, as shown in. Therefore, it can be concluded that microwave hydrothermal reaction at 180\xb0C was beneficial for the synthesis of KNNT powders. shows the XRD patterns of KNNT powders synthesized at 180\xb0C for 7.5h and 9.5h. The intensities of the Ta2O5 diffraction peaks did not decrease when the reaction time was increased from 7.5h to 9.5h, as expected, indicating that the increased the reaction time did not help Ta2O5 to react sufficiently. Hence, it is reasonable to suggest that it is difficult to obtain a complete reaction of Ta2O5 by either increasing the reaction temperature or prolonging the holding time. This may be attributed to the fact that the Ta2O5 density (\u03c1=8.2g/cmE^{3}) is higher than that of Nb2O5 (\u03c1=4.5g/cmE^{3}) so that Ta2O5 powders tend to sediment at the bottom of the autoclave with some Nb2O5 powders located on the top, making it difficult for the Ta2O5 to sufficiently contact the alkaline solution. Consequently, it is necessary to enhance the activity of Ta2O5 powders prior to the crystallization. To enhance Ta2O5 activity first and then obtain pure KNN1\u2212x T x powders, a two-stage synthesis procedure during MH was adopted to prepare the KNN1\u2212x T x (designed x=0.3) particles. Following a certain time of holding at the low temperature T1 (110\xb0C, 120\xb0C, 130\xb0C or 140\xb0C), the temperature was rapidly increased to second stage with T2=180\xb0C for 7.5h. shows the XRD patterns of the prepared powders synthesized in the first stage under various conditions. The diffraction peaks of K4Na4(Nb, Ta)6O19\xb79H2O (JCPDS Card no. 00-014-0360) and (K, Na)(Nb, Ta)O3 (JCPDS Card no. 01-074-2442) can be clearly seen in all samples. The SEM images of the prepared powders are shown in. Large K4Na4(Nb, Ta)6O19\xb79H2O particles are observed with the small (K, Na)(Nb, Ta)O3 crystals grown on their surface (shown by arrows in ) that were analyzed by EDS. shows that as temperature T1 increased from 110\xb0C to 130\xb0C, the large K4Na4(Nb, Ta)6O19\xb79H2O particles gradually became smaller and the amount of Ta2O5 decreased, suggesting that K4Na4(Nb, Ta)6O19\xb79H2O was an intermediate compound that further reacted with Ta2O5 to form (K, Na)(Nb, Ta)O3 crystals. Therefore, it can be concluded that the small (K, Na)(Nb, Ta)O3 crystals that were generated in the first stage acted as the crystal nuclei in the second stage of MH synthesis. shows the representative XRD pattern of final KNN1\u2212x T x powders obtained by the two-stage MH synthesis method. Examination of (a)\u2013(c) found that the intensity of the Ta2O5 diffraction peaks decreased with increasing T1, with the Ta2O5 diffraction disappearing completely when T1 was increased to 130\xb0C. This may be attributed to the increased rate of nucleation of KNNT nuclei with the rising first synthesis temperature. It is known that the kinetics of the Ta2O5 and Nb2O5 reactions and the nucleation and crystal growth processes all depend on the temperature during synthesis, so the Ta2O5 and Nb2O5 activities increased with increasing temperature. As mentioned above, in this study, the reaction with the alkaline solution was more difficult for Ta2O5 than for Nb2O5. Therefore, the two-stage synthesis method was proposed to accelerate the reaction rate of Ta2O5 by increasing the first-stage temperature T1, which subsequently led to the acceleration of the KNNT crystal nucleation rate. However, as T1 was continuously increased from 130\xb0C to 140\xb0C and the holding time was shortened from 2h to 1h, the characteristic Ta2O5 peaks appeared again ((c) and (d)); this means that despite the increased T1, the holding time cannot be reduced, so T130(2h)\u2013T180(7.5h) is optimal for the synthesis of pure KNN1\u2212x T x powders. shows the SEM images of the final KNNT powders obtained by the two-stage MH method. All of the as-prepared samples are well crystallized, homogeneous, regular and well-dispersed compared to the sample synthesized with the one-stage synthesis process (T180(7.5h)) ((b)). The grain sizes of these samples are close to 500\u2013800nm. This may be due to great number of nuclei introduced at the first stage of the synthesis; as the temperature was continuously raised to T2, the rate of crystallization was accelerated, leading to the generation of homogeneous and regular crystals. These results confirmed that the two-stage method during MH is beneficial for the synthesis KNN1\u2212x T x powders with better morphology. Therefore, based on the XRD and SEM results, to obtain well crystallized, homogeneous and fine KNNT samples without a Ta2O5 impurity phase, which may significantly affect the sintering activity, two-stage synthesis at temperatures T1=130\xb0C (2h) and T2=180\xb0C (7.5h) was adopted for the following studies. shows the XRD patterns of the KNN1\u2212x T x (x=0, x=0.2, x=0.3, x=0.4) powders that were obtained by applying the two-stage MH synthesis. # INTRODUCTION
Among these ceramics, K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (abbreviated as KNN) is considered to be one of the most promising candidates for lead-free piezoelectric ceramics. KNN is a solid solution of ferroelectric KNbO3 and antiferroelectric NaNbO3 ; near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB), KNN exhibits ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties that are enhanced relative to those of pure KNbO3. Currently, an increasing number of researchers believe that the existence of polymorphic phase transitions (PPT) also contributes to the good piezoelectric properties at room temperature exhibited by KNN. Nevertheless, one of the main obstacles for the development of KNN ceramics is the difficulty in processing and densification. It is usually very difficult to obtain dense and well-sintered pure KNN ceramics by conventional sintering methods because the alkaline constituent elements of KNN are highly volatile at the higher temperatures required for the sintering of KNN. To obtain dense KNN ceramics, a number of investigations have focused on improving sintering activity. One approach is to synthesize fine KNN powder precursors to enhance the sintering activity to take advantage of the inverse proportionality between the driving force for sintering and the grain size. To reduce the grain size, sol-gel routine, molten salt synthesis, hydrothermal and microwave\u2013hydrothermal (MH) methods have been used to prepare KNN powders. In our previous work , fine powders were prepared by using the hydrothermal method at 230\xb0C for 24h. On the contrary, crystalline and homogeneous KNN powders were successfully prepared at 160\xb0C for 7h by using the microwave\u2013hydrothermal method. The advantages of an lower temperature and a shorter holding time than those required in the hydrothermal method make the microwave\u2013hydrothermal method much more attractive for synthesis of KNN powders with a small grain size. It is well known that Ta ions possess the same chemical valence (+5) and similar atomic radius (0.068nm for Ta compared with 0.069nm for Nb) as Nb ions. To improve the piezoelectric properties of KNN ceramics, some studies have examined doping Ta ions into a KNN solid solution, partially substituting NbE^{5+}. Saito et al. found that the highest piezoelectric d33 constant in the (K, Na)NbO3\u2013LiTaO3 system was 300 pC/N, with texturing the materials resulting in an enhanced value of d33=416 pC/N. Lv et al. studied the (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb1\u2212x Ta x )O3 (KNNT) ceramics system. It was found that Ta substitution decreases both T C and T O\u2013T, but increases T R\u2013O. The ceramics with x=0.30 show a high value of piezoelectric coefficient with d33=205 pC/N. Matsubara et al. have also shown that (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb0.7Ta0.3)O3 ceramics exhibit an enhanced d33 value. In our previous studies , it was found that the value of d33 increased with the increasing amount of Ta-doping (x\u22640.3), and the highest d33 value of 210 pC/N was obtained for (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb0.7Ta0.3)O3 ceramics. Thus, KNN ceramics with Ta-doping synthesized from KNNT powders obtained by MH have been shown to exhibit enhanced piezoelectric property. In this paper, (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb1\u2212x Ta x )O3 (0\u2264x\u22640.4) powders were successfully synthesized by the microwave\u2013hydrothermal method, and (K0.5Na0.5)(Nb1\u2212x Ta x )O3 ceramics were sintered under atmospheric pressure using an ordinary sintering method.

# CONCLUSION
Dispersive, homogeneous and highly crystalline K0.5Na0.5Nb1\u2212x Ta x O3 (x=0.2, 0.3, 0.4) powders have been successfully synthesized by a two-stage MH synthesis method. The first stage of the reaction was performed at 130\xb0C for 2h, and then, the temperature was raised to 180\xb0C for 7.5h. The results show that the pure K0.5Na0.5Nb1\u2212x Ta x O3 and particles with small sizes close to 500\u2013800nm were obtained. The dense K0.5Na0.5Nb1\u2212x Ta x O3 ceramics were obtained by sintering at 1090\xb0C for 2h under normal atmospheric conditions. Following polarization, it was observed that the d33 piezoelectric coefficient increased with the increasing Ta content and reached its highest value of 233 pC/N when x=0.3; this can be attributed to Ta doping and the better morphology of the initial crystallite powders synthesized by the two-stage MH method. Moreover, the two-stage MH method is simple and efficient and may be an attractive route for preparing other piezoelectric ceramics.
