# TITLE
The contribution of nonlinear behavior for large piezoelectric response in BNT-BT-based ceramics

# ABSTRACT
Bismuth sodium–barium titanate (BNT-BT)-based ceramics with multi-phase coexistence exhibit promising piezoelectricity, while the enhancement mechanism of phase transition on the piezoelectricity is worthy of further study. Here, we developed a new composition of (BiNa)BaTi(NbLu)O (named as BNTBT-NL) by introducing (NbLu)E^{4+} complex ions to reduce the driving field from 4 to 3. A large electro-strain of 0.48% (piezoelectric stain coefficient * = 686 pm/V) is obtained in  = 0.15 ceramics. # INTRODUCTION
Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 (abbreviated as BNT)-based ceramics exhibit a large spontaneous polarization of ~40 \u03bcC/cmE^{2} at room temperature, which is attributed to the hybridization between Bi 6s and O 2p orbitals, leading to the high polarizable nature and exploiting potential application in actuators [, ]. The BNT-based ferroelectrics through chemical doping have become a hotspot, which profits from its improvement of electromechanical properties [, , \u2013]. Especially, a large recoverable strain of 0.24% is realized in MnE^{2+}-modified BNT-based ceramics, which originate from the presence of acceptor-induced defect dipoles. It has been reported that both intrinsic and extrinsic contributions are responsible for the electric-field-induced strain in ferroelectrics [, \u2013]. The extrinsic contribution from irreversible non-180\xb0 domain walls motion, as well as reversible domain walls under low driving field, is independent of various matrix materials and is vital to realize large electro-strain. Normally, the extrinsic contribution due to the non-180\xb0 domain walls is strongly influenced by many factors, including grain size, lattice deformation and crystal structure. With an external electric field, the non-180\xb0 domains align along the direction of the electric field, while the Pd keeps the origin direction and creates a strong restoring force for the spontaneous polarization (Ps) to switch back to the original position and the domain walls to reorient to their random direction, realizing a large reversible electro-strain. A large electro-strain of 0.24% is obtained in Fe2O3-modified BNT ceramics, which benefit from the reorientation of defect dipoles with high polarity induced by the large applied electric field as well as the domain switching. Nevertheless, quantitative studies on the relationship between extrinsic contribution of non-180\xb0 domains and excellent piezoelectric properties are scarce, a deep understanding of the impact of the extrinsic contribution of non-180\xb0 domain walls motion, and phase transition on the piezoelectric properties may be necessary. In this work, we introduce complex ions into BNT-based ceramics to break the long-range order of ferroelectrics and reduce the energy barrier of phase transition and domain walls switching. Pan et al. studied a large strain of 0.462% in (Fe0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} co-doped Bi1/2 (Na0.82K0.12)1/2TiO3 piezoceramics. Yin et al. reported a large piezoelectric coefficient of 598 pC/N in LuE^{3+}-doped BaTiO3-based ceramics. However, little effort has been paid to the effect of B-site (Nb0.5Lu0.5)E^{4+} complex ions substitution. Based on the former researches, we designed a non-toxic new ternary solid solution through introducing B-site complex-ion (Nb0.5Lu0.5)E^{4+} into the (Bi0.5Na0.5)0.94Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The Rayleigh law, and XRD reviled refinement are utilized to investigate the evolution of structure and properties under an external electric field. Our results demonstrate that the extrinsic contribution of the non-180\xb0 domain walls motion accounts for 51.6% of the total piezoelectric response for poled ceramics, which plays an important role in the large piezoelectric response. In addition, the phase transition from P4bm to R3c and the reorientation of defect dipoles under large electric field should be responsible for the enhancement of piezoelectric performance.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, the physical mechanism of properties enhancement for BNT-BT ceramics by introducing the (Nb0.5Lu0.5)E^{4+} complex ions to break the long-range order of ferroelectrics and lower the driving field of ergodic state transform to nonergodic state, a large electro-strain of 0.48% with the d33* of 686 pm/V is obtained. According to the structure and properties diversity between unpoled and poled samples, as well as using Rayleigh calculation and the conclusion is that the enhancement of piezoelectricity for x = 0.015 ceramics is attributed to the energy barriers of the phase transition are reduced by complex ions\u2019 modification. Besides, the coexistence and appropriate phase contents of R3c and P4bm that lower the whole system free energy barrier are also beneficial to enhance piezoelectricity. The coexistence of ergodic and nonergodic states improves the ferroelectric and dielectric properties of BNT-based ceramics observably, and the quantification of the extrinsic contributions by Rayleigh analysis provides an understanding of complex relationship between the phase structures and piezoelectric performance.

# TITLE
The contribution of nonlinear behavior for large piezoelectric response in BNT-BT-based ceramics

# ABSTRACT
Bismuth sodium–barium titanate (BNT-BT)-based ceramics with multi-phase coexistence exhibit promising piezoelectricity, while the enhancement mechanism of phase transition on the piezoelectricity is worthy of further study. Here, we developed a new composition of (BiNa)BaTi(NbLu)O (named as BNTBT-NL) by introducing (NbLu)E^{4+} complex ions to reduce the driving field from 4 to 3. A large electro-strain of 0.48% (piezoelectric stain coefficient * = 686 pm/V) is obtained in  = 0.15 ceramics. With further studying the properties between poled and unpoled  = 0.15 ceramics, both the intrinsic and extrinsic contribution increased together with the coexistence of 3 and 4 phase, which are responsible for exploring the origin of the excellent piezoelectric properties of BNT-NL ceramics. In this work, (NbLu)E^{4+} complex ions are used to lower phase transition energy barriers and enhance the piezoelectric characteristics of BNT-based ceramics. Rayleigh analysis provides an effective way to quantify the impact of intrinsic and extrinsic contributions on the piezoelectric properties of ceramics.The mechanism and Rayleigh analysis of piezoelectric response in BNTBT-NL ceramics.

# INTRODUCTION
Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3 (abbreviated as BNT)-based ceramics exhibit a large spontaneous polarization of ~40 \u03bcC/cmE^{2} at room temperature, which is attributed to the hybridization between Bi 6s and O 2p orbitals, leading to the high polarizable nature and exploiting potential application in actuators [, ]. The BNT-based ferroelectrics through chemical doping have become a hotspot, which profits from its improvement of electromechanical properties [, , \u2013]. Especially, a large recoverable strain of 0.24% is realized in MnE^{2+}-modified BNT-based ceramics, which originate from the presence of acceptor-induced defect dipoles. It has been reported that both intrinsic and extrinsic contributions are responsible for the electric-field-induced strain in ferroelectrics [, \u2013]. The extrinsic contribution from irreversible non-180\xb0 domain walls motion, as well as reversible domain walls under low driving field, is independent of various matrix materials and is vital to realize large electro-strain. Normally, the extrinsic contribution due to the non-180\xb0 domain walls is strongly influenced by many factors, including grain size, lattice deformation and crystal structure. With an external electric field, the non-180\xb0 domains align along the direction of the electric field, while the Pd keeps the origin direction and creates a strong restoring force for the spontaneous polarization (Ps) to switch back to the original position and the domain walls to reorient to their random direction, realizing a large reversible electro-strain. A large electro-strain of 0.24% is obtained in Fe2O3-modified BNT ceramics, which benefit from the reorientation of defect dipoles with high polarity induced by the large applied electric field as well as the domain switching. Nevertheless, quantitative studies on the relationship between extrinsic contribution of non-180\xb0 domains and excellent piezoelectric properties are scarce, a deep understanding of the impact of the extrinsic contribution of non-180\xb0 domain walls motion, and phase transition on the piezoelectric properties may be necessary. In this work, we introduce complex ions into BNT-based ceramics to break the long-range order of ferroelectrics and reduce the energy barrier of phase transition and domain walls switching. Pan et al. studied a large strain of 0.462% in (Fe0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} co-doped Bi1/2 (Na0.82K0.12)1/2TiO3 piezoceramics. Yin et al. reported a large piezoelectric coefficient of 598 pC/N in LuE^{3+}-doped BaTiO3-based ceramics. However, little effort has been paid to the effect of B-site (Nb0.5Lu0.5)E^{4+} complex ions substitution. Based on the former researches, we designed a non-toxic new ternary solid solution through introducing B-site complex-ion (Nb0.5Lu0.5)E^{4+} into the (Bi0.5Na0.5)0.94Ba0.06TiO3 ceramics. The Rayleigh law, and XRD reviled refinement are utilized to investigate the evolution of structure and properties under an external electric field. Our results demonstrate that the extrinsic contribution of the non-180\xb0 domain walls motion accounts for 51.6% of the total piezoelectric response for poled ceramics, which plays an important role in the large piezoelectric response. In addition, the phase transition from P4bm to R3c and the reorientation of defect dipoles under large electric field should be responsible for the enhancement of piezoelectric performance.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, the physical mechanism of properties enhancement for BNT-BT ceramics by introducing the (Nb0.5Lu0.5)E^{4+} complex ions to break the long-range order of ferroelectrics and lower the driving field of ergodic state transform to nonergodic state, a large electro-strain of 0.48% with the d33* of 686 pm/V is obtained. According to the structure and properties diversity between unpoled and poled samples, as well as using Rayleigh calculation and the conclusion is that the enhancement of piezoelectricity for x = 0.015 ceramics is attributed to the energy barriers of the phase transition are reduced by complex ions\u2019 modification. Besides, the coexistence and appropriate phase contents of R3c and P4bm that lower the whole system free energy barrier are also beneficial to enhance piezoelectricity. The coexistence of ergodic and nonergodic states improves the ferroelectric and dielectric properties of BNT-based ceramics observably, and the quantification of the extrinsic contributions by Rayleigh analysis provides an understanding of complex relationship between the phase structures and piezoelectric performance.
