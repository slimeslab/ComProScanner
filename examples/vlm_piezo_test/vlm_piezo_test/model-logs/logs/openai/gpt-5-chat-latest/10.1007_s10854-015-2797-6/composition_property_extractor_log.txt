# TITLE
Effects of Sr substitution on the structural, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 lead-free ceramics

# ABSTRACT
We have systematically studied the effects of Sr substitution on the microstructure, crystal structure, Curie temperature, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of (BaSr)(ZrTi)O (0 ≤  ≤ 0.40) ceramics prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method. Both X-ray diffraction and dielectric measurements reveal that with increasing Sr content, the room-temperature crystal structure of the samples evolves from tetragonal to orthorhombic around  = 0.2 where the two phases coexist. Dielectric measurements show that Sr doping significantly reduces the Curie temperature () linearly from 114 °C for  = 0 to 10 °C for  = 0.4 with / ≈ −265 °C/mol. At room temperature, the sample with  = 0.11 exhibits enhanced piezoelectric properties with  ~ 380 pC/N and the planar mode electromechanical coupling coefficient  = 0.38.

# INTRODUCTION
Barium titanate (BaTiO3) is one of the most widely studied lead-free piezoelectric materials [\u2013]. Recently, BaTiO3-based lead-free ceramics have attracted an ever-increasing amount of interest since the discovery of a surprisingly high piezoelectric coefficient of d33 ~ 620 pC/N in (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Zr0.1Ti0.9)O3 (abbreviated as BCZT) ceramics by Liu et al.. In order to further enhance d33 and the Curie temperature much effort has been made to optimize the processing [\u2013] or to choose appropriate addition [\u2013] as well as to find new BaTiO3-based ceramics with excellent piezoelectric properties by co-doping at A and B sites [\u2013]. Xue et al.  reported that CaE^{2+} and SnE^{4+} co-doped 0.3(Ba0.7Ca0.3)TiO3\u20130.7Ba(Sn0.12Ti0.88)O3 ceramics have a high d33 ~ 530 pC/N. Zhu et al.  achieved further enhanced d33 ~ 670 pC/N for CaE^{2+} and SnE^{4+} co-doped (Ba0.95Ca0.05)(Ti0.89Sn0.11)O3 in which the rhombohedral, pseudocubic, orthorhombic phases coexist at room temperature. Zhou et al.  found that d33 of CaE^{2+} and HfE^{4+} co-doped 0.5Ba(Ti0.8Hf0.2)O3\u20130.5(Ba0.7Ca0.3)TiO3 ceramics reaches 550 pC/N at room temperature. Jiang et al.  observed that 4 mol% SnE^{4+} doped (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Ti0.9Zr1\u2212xSnx)O3 ceramics possesses a high d33 ~ 665 pC/N. It is summarized that all these co-doping push the ferroelectric-to-ferroelectric phase transition to higher temperatures to form the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) or polymorphic phase transition (PPT) [, ]. As a congener element, the ionic radius of SrE^{2+} (1.12 \xc5) is larger than that of CaE^{2+} (0.99 \xc5) but smaller than that of BaE^{2+} (1.34 \xc5). SrE^{2+} ion substitution for A site ion has been employed to modify the electrical, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics. Sen et al.  studied the impedance spectroscopy of (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(Zr0.05Ti0.95)O3 (x = 0, 0.03, 0.06 and 0.09) ceramics and found Debye-like relaxation and Arrhenius-type electrical conductivity. Lee et al.  reported the fabrication of high frequency ultrasonic transducer based on the (Ba0.95Sr0.05)(Zr0.05Ti0.95)O3 ceramics with excellent piezoelectric activities (d33 = 300 pC/N). Moreover, Chan et al.  achieved a high dielectric tunability of 79 % for the (Ba0.75Sr0.25)(Zr0.1Ti0.9)O3 ceramics. Although the properties of Sr-modified (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics have been studied by several groups, a systematical investigation on the structural, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of Sr-doped Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics is still missing and remains unaddressed. There is no doubt that a comprehensive understanding of the impact of Sr-substitution for A site ions on the properties of Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 system would help to better understand the origin of the high piezoelectricity of this class of lead-free materials. In this paper, we have prepared a series of (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(ZryTi1\u2212y)O3 (BSZT) (0 \u2264 x \u2264 0.40, y = 0.05, 0.1) ceramics using the solid-state reaction technique and systematically studied the effects of SrE^{2+} substitution for BaE^{2+} on the structural evolution, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of the samples. We have observed a Sr-substitution-induced tetragonal to orthorhombic phase transition around x = 0.2 where the two phases coexist. Our data provides useful insight into the role played by the Sr substitution and would help to better understand the structure\u2013property relationships of similar lead-free ceramics.

# CONCLUSION
We have prepared a series of single-phase (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(ZryTi1\u2212y)O3 lead-free ceramics by solid-state reaction technique and studied the effects of Sr doping on the structural, microstructure, dielectric, ferroelectric, piezoelectric properties of samples. Powder XRD measurements reveal that upon Sr doping the samples undergo a doping-induced tetragonal-to-orthorhombic phase transition near x = 0.2 at room temperature, which is further collaborated by the temperature dependent dielectric measurements. Based on the dielectric data, a phase diagram was established for the BSZT ceramics. Because of the highest remnant polarization and the uniform microstructure and highest density, the x = 0.11 sample shows an enhanced piezoelectricity (d33 ~ 380 pC/N). Our systematic investigation of the BSZT series would help to better understand and optimize the properties of BaTiO3-base lead-free ceramics.

# TITLE
Effects of Sr substitution on the structural, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 lead-free ceramics

# ABSTRACT
We have systematically studied the effects of Sr substitution on the microstructure, crystal structure, Curie temperature, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of (BaSr)(ZrTi)O (0 ≤  ≤ 0.40) ceramics prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method. Both X-ray diffraction and dielectric measurements reveal that with increasing Sr content, the room-temperature crystal structure of the samples evolves from tetragonal to orthorhombic around  = 0.2 where the two phases coexist. Dielectric measurements show that Sr doping significantly reduces the Curie temperature () linearly from 114 °C for  = 0 to 10 °C for  = 0.4 with / ≈ −265 °C/mol. At room temperature, the sample with  = 0.11 exhibits enhanced piezoelectric properties with  ~ 380 pC/N and the planar mode electromechanical coupling coefficient  = 0.38.

# INTRODUCTION
Barium titanate (BaTiO3) is one of the most widely studied lead-free piezoelectric materials [\u2013]. Recently, BaTiO3-based lead-free ceramics have attracted an ever-increasing amount of interest since the discovery of a surprisingly high piezoelectric coefficient of d33 ~ 620 pC/N in (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Zr0.1Ti0.9)O3 (abbreviated as BCZT) ceramics by Liu et al.. In order to further enhance d33 and the Curie temperature much effort has been made to optimize the processing [\u2013] or to choose appropriate addition [\u2013] as well as to find new BaTiO3-based ceramics with excellent piezoelectric properties by co-doping at A and B sites [\u2013]. Xue et al.  reported that CaE^{2+} and SnE^{4+} co-doped 0.3(Ba0.7Ca0.3)TiO3\u20130.7Ba(Sn0.12Ti0.88)O3 ceramics have a high d33 ~ 530 pC/N. Zhu et al.  achieved further enhanced d33 ~ 670 pC/N for CaE^{2+} and SnE^{4+} co-doped (Ba0.95Ca0.05)(Ti0.89Sn0.11)O3 in which the rhombohedral, pseudocubic, orthorhombic phases coexist at room temperature. Zhou et al.  found that d33 of CaE^{2+} and HfE^{4+} co-doped 0.5Ba(Ti0.8Hf0.2)O3\u20130.5(Ba0.7Ca0.3)TiO3 ceramics reaches 550 pC/N at room temperature. Jiang et al.  observed that 4 mol% SnE^{4+} doped (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Ti0.9Zr1\u2212xSnx)O3 ceramics possesses a high d33 ~ 665 pC/N. It is summarized that all these co-doping push the ferroelectric-to-ferroelectric phase transition to higher temperatures to form the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) or polymorphic phase transition (PPT) [, ]. As a congener element, the ionic radius of SrE^{2+} (1.12 \xc5) is larger than that of CaE^{2+} (0.99 \xc5) but smaller than that of BaE^{2+} (1.34 \xc5). SrE^{2+} ion substitution for A site ion has been employed to modify the electrical, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics. Sen et al.  studied the impedance spectroscopy of (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(Zr0.05Ti0.95)O3 (x = 0, 0.03, 0.06 and 0.09) ceramics and found Debye-like relaxation and Arrhenius-type electrical conductivity. Lee et al.  reported the fabrication of high frequency ultrasonic transducer based on the (Ba0.95Sr0.05)(Zr0.05Ti0.95)O3 ceramics with excellent piezoelectric activities (d33 = 300 pC/N). Moreover, Chan et al.  achieved a high dielectric tunability of 79 % for the (Ba0.75Sr0.25)(Zr0.1Ti0.9)O3 ceramics. Although the properties of Sr-modified (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics have been studied by several groups, a systematical investigation on the structural, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of Sr-doped Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 ceramics is still missing and remains unaddressed. There is no doubt that a comprehensive understanding of the impact of Sr-substitution for A site ions on the properties of Ba(Zr,Ti)O3 system would help to better understand the origin of the high piezoelectricity of this class of lead-free materials. In this paper, we have prepared a series of (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(ZryTi1\u2212y)O3 (BSZT) (0 \u2264 x \u2264 0.40, y = 0.05, 0.1) ceramics using the solid-state reaction technique and systematically studied the effects of SrE^{2+} substitution for BaE^{2+} on the structural evolution, dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of the samples. We have observed a Sr-substitution-induced tetragonal to orthorhombic phase transition around x = 0.2 where the two phases coexist. Our data provides useful insight into the role played by the Sr substitution and would help to better understand the structure\u2013property relationships of similar lead-free ceramics.

# CONCLUSION
We have prepared a series of single-phase (Ba1\u2212xSrx)(ZryTi1\u2212y)O3 lead-free ceramics by solid-state reaction technique and studied the effects of Sr doping on the structural, microstructure, dielectric, ferroelectric, piezoelectric properties of samples. Powder XRD measurements reveal that upon Sr doping the samples undergo a doping-induced tetragonal-to-orthorhombic phase transition near x = 0.2 at room temperature, which is further collaborated by the temperature dependent dielectric measurements. Based on the dielectric data, a phase diagram was established for the BSZT ceramics. Because of the highest remnant polarization and the uniform microstructure and highest density, the x = 0.11 sample shows an enhanced piezoelectricity (d33 ~ 380 pC/N). Our systematic investigation of the BSZT series would help to better understand and optimize the properties of BaTiO3-base lead-free ceramics.
