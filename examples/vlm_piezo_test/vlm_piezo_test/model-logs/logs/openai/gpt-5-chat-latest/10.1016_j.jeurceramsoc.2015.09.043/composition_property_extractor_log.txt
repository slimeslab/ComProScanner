# INTRODUCTION
Lead-based ferroelectric materials such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT) have been widely utilized for piezoelectric or dielectric components and other electromechanical devices. Since the bismuth-based perovskite ceramics, Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 (BNT) was found to possess promising piezoelectric and ferroelectric properties, various promising BNT-based solid solutions have been proposed: examples including the binary Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3 (BNT-BT) , Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (BNT-KNN) , the ternary Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-Bi1/2K1/2TiO3-BaTiO3 (BNT-BKT-BT) , Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-(K0.5Na0.5)NbO3 (BNT-BT-KNN) and Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-BiAlO3 (BNT-BT-BA). Zhang et al. reported a Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (BNT-BT-KNN) ceramics having a giant strain response of 0.45% at 80kV/cm driving field equivalently 560pm/V. Pham et al. studied a Nb-doped Bi1/2(Na0.82K0.18)1/2TiO3 (BNKTN) ceramics near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) and reported a large unipolar strain of 0.448% at 70kV/cm. The subsequent researches on the mechanism of the giant strain in BNT-based systems, as well as new BNT-based compositions further accelerated studies on BNT-based ceramics. However, the practical application of BNT-based ceramics is hindered due to the fact that large strain response needs a high electric field to trigger. Jin et al. introduced (Al0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} into the B-site of BNT and found a large strain S max/E max of 575pm/V and high piezoelectric constant d33 of 210 pC/N. Li et al. prepared Fe-modified 0.875Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3-0.06Bi0.5Li0.5TiO3-0.065BaTiO3-0.005Mn ceramic with a large unipolar strain of 0.4% under 40kV/cm. In light of these observations, a new ternary solid solution was designed and characterized through introducing B-site complex-ion (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} into the MPB solid solution (Bi1/2Na1/2)0.935Ba0.065TiO3 (BNBT). It is expected that (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} modification in BNBT ceramics can enhance the electromechanical properties. In this work, we fabricated BNBT-based ceramics with (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} addition using a conventional solid-state reaction method and systematically studied the influence of compositional modification on the crystal structure, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties and field-induced strain behaviors.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, lead-free BNBT-x FN piezoelectric ceramics were prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method and the crystal structure, microstructure and electrical properties were systematically investigated. All BNBT-x FN ceramics form the pure perovskite phase structure, and no obvious change in the crystal structure is observed. A large field-induced strain of 0.422% at a low applied field of 50kV/cm was observed at the critical composition of x =0.020 which corresponds to the S max/E max of 844 pm/V. The dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of BNBT-x FN ceramics were enhanced by a proper amount of (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}. Improved Pr =35.6\u03bcC/cmE^{2}, low Ec of 25.2kV/cm, and high d33 =213pC/N were observed at x =0.010. These results show our research can benefit the developments of Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 ceramics and widen their applications range.

# INTRODUCTION
Lead-based ferroelectric materials such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT) have been widely utilized for piezoelectric or dielectric components and other electromechanical devices. Since the bismuth-based perovskite ceramics, Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 (BNT) was found to possess promising piezoelectric and ferroelectric properties, various promising BNT-based solid solutions have been proposed: examples including the binary Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3 (BNT-BT) , Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (BNT-KNN) , the ternary Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-Bi1/2K1/2TiO3-BaTiO3 (BNT-BKT-BT) , Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-(K0.5Na0.5)NbO3 (BNT-BT-KNN) and Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-BiAlO3 (BNT-BT-BA). Zhang et al. reported a Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (BNT-BT-KNN) ceramics having a giant strain response of 0.45% at 80kV/cm driving field equivalently 560pm/V. Pham et al. studied a Nb-doped Bi1/2(Na0.82K0.18)1/2TiO3 (BNKTN) ceramics near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) and reported a large unipolar strain of 0.448% at 70kV/cm. The subsequent researches on the mechanism of the giant strain in BNT-based systems, as well as new BNT-based compositions further accelerated studies on BNT-based ceramics. However, the practical application of BNT-based ceramics is hindered due to the fact that large strain response needs a high electric field to trigger. Jin et al. introduced (Al0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} into the B-site of BNT and found a large strain S max/E max of 575pm/V and high piezoelectric constant d33 of 210 pC/N. Li et al. prepared Fe-modified 0.875Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3-0.06Bi0.5Li0.5TiO3-0.065BaTiO3-0.005Mn ceramic with a large unipolar strain of 0.4% under 40kV/cm. In light of these observations, a new ternary solid solution was designed and characterized through introducing B-site complex-ion (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} into the MPB solid solution (Bi1/2Na1/2)0.935Ba0.065TiO3 (BNBT). It is expected that (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} modification in BNBT ceramics can enhance the electromechanical properties. In this work, we fabricated BNBT-based ceramics with (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} addition using a conventional solid-state reaction method and systematically studied the influence of compositional modification on the crystal structure, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties and field-induced strain behaviors.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, lead-free BNBT-x FN piezoelectric ceramics were prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method and the crystal structure, microstructure and electrical properties were systematically investigated. All BNBT-x FN ceramics form the pure perovskite phase structure, and no obvious change in the crystal structure is observed. A large field-induced strain of 0.422% at a low applied field of 50kV/cm was observed at the critical composition of x =0.020 which corresponds to the S max/E max of 844 pm/V. The dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of BNBT-x FN ceramics were enhanced by a proper amount of (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}. Improved Pr =35.6\u03bcC/cmE^{2}, low Ec of 25.2kV/cm, and high d33 =213pC/N were observed at x =0.010. These results show our research can benefit the developments of Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 ceramics and widen their applications range.

# INTRODUCTION
Lead-based ferroelectric materials such as lead zirconate titanate (PZT) have been widely utilized for piezoelectric or dielectric components and other electromechanical devices. Since the bismuth-based perovskite ceramics, Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 (BNT) was found to possess promising piezoelectric and ferroelectric properties, various promising BNT-based solid solutions have been proposed: examples including the binary Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3 (BNT-BT) , Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (BNT-KNN) , the ternary Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-Bi1/2K1/2TiO3-BaTiO3 (BNT-BKT-BT) , Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-(K0.5Na0.5)NbO3 (BNT-BT-KNN) and Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-BiAlO3 (BNT-BT-BA). Zhang et al. reported a Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3-BaTiO3-K0.5Na0.5NbO3 (BNT-BT-KNN) ceramics having a giant strain response of 0.45% at 80kV/cm driving field equivalently 560pm/V. Pham et al. studied a Nb-doped Bi1/2(Na0.82K0.18)1/2TiO3 (BNKTN) ceramics near the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) and reported a large unipolar strain of 0.448% at 70kV/cm. The subsequent researches on the mechanism of the giant strain in BNT-based systems, as well as new BNT-based compositions further accelerated studies on BNT-based ceramics. However, the practical application of BNT-based ceramics is hindered due to the fact that large strain response needs a high electric field to trigger. Jin et al. introduced (Al0.5Nb0.5)E^{4+} into the B-site of BNT and found a large strain S max/E max of 575pm/V and high piezoelectric constant d33 of 210 pC/N. Li et al. prepared Fe-modified 0.875Bi0.5Na0.5TiO3-0.06Bi0.5Li0.5TiO3-0.065BaTiO3-0.005Mn ceramic with a large unipolar strain of 0.4% under 40kV/cm. In light of these observations, a new ternary solid solution was designed and characterized through introducing B-site complex-ion (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} into the MPB solid solution (Bi1/2Na1/2)0.935Ba0.065TiO3 (BNBT). It is expected that (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} modification in BNBT ceramics can enhance the electromechanical properties. In this work, we fabricated BNBT-based ceramics with (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+} addition using a conventional solid-state reaction method and systematically studied the influence of compositional modification on the crystal structure, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties and field-induced strain behaviors.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, lead-free BNBT-x FN piezoelectric ceramics were prepared by the conventional solid-state reaction method and the crystal structure, microstructure and electrical properties were systematically investigated. All BNBT-x FN ceramics form the pure perovskite phase structure, and no obvious change in the crystal structure is observed. A large field-induced strain of 0.422% at a low applied field of 50kV/cm was observed at the critical composition of x =0.020 which corresponds to the S max/E max of 844 pm/V. The dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties of BNBT-x FN ceramics were enhanced by a proper amount of (Fe1/2Nb1/2)E^{4+}. Improved Pr =35.6\u03bcC/cmE^{2}, low Ec of 25.2kV/cm, and high d33 =213pC/N were observed at x =0.010. These results show our research can benefit the developments of Bi1/2Na1/2TiO3 ceramics and widen their applications range.
