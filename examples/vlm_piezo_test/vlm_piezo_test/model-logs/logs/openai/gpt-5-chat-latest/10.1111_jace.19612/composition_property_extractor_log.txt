To comprehend the Sm-doping effects on local heterogeneities in KNSN ceramics, the CP, which is associated with the relaxation process of polar clusters or PNRs, 40 has been investigated as a function of composition. The observed soft mode phenomena imply a displacive phase transition, whereas the existence of the CP denotes an order-disorder phase transition of either a crystal or a ceramic. In an orthorhombic phase of pure KNN ceramic, the low-frequency B 2 (TO 1 ) mode is seen at about 54 cm -1 , whereas the overdamped E(TO1 ) mode at around 72 cm -1 is observed in the mixed (orthorhombic + tetragonal) phases of the KNSN (0.010 ≤ x ≤ 0.020) in the present study. Without temperature-dependent Raman scattering results, it is difficult to opine about the soft-mode nature of the B 2 (TO1) and E(TO1 ) modes. Hence, the order-disorder nature of ferroelectric phase transition is found due to the existence of prominent CP in KNSN ceramics. 34,35,40 It is well established that the dynamic PNRs begin to appear in the paraelectric phase at the so-called Burns temperature ( T B ). 41 These PNRs become static at an intermediate temperature ( T *). 34,35 Due to the local polarization freezing in the ferroelectric phase, these static PNRs transform into randomly oriented nano-domain states and convert them into macro-domain states. 42,43 Note that in the lower part in Figure 3C, the Γ CP , which is associated with the relaxation process of fluctuating local polar clusters, 34,35,40 decreases at x = 0.005, then increases with Sm-doping, and almost constant (slightly scattered) in the range of 0.010 ≤ x ≤ 0.020 of the KNSN ceramics. With increasing Sm composition in the same structural phase, the correlation among nano-domain states may be bolstered owing to the increase of number density and/or size of randomly oriented nano-domain states, resulting in a decrease in the value of the Γ CP. 6,39,44 Theanomalous change of the Γ CP in KNSN ( x = 0.010) indicates the structural phase transition of KNSN ceramics. In the range of 0.010 ≤ x ≤ 0.020, the value of Γ CP is almost constant (Figure 3C), where KNSN belongs to (orthorhombic + tetragonal) phases. These findings indicate that the correlation among nano-domain states may be weak and/or broken giving rise to the almost constant fluctuations of domain wall motion because of the coexistence of phases. 6,39

The compositional-driven structural phase transition takes place in ferroelectric solid solutions. 23 When the composition approaches the MPB, an exceptional increase in the dielectric, ferroelectric, ferroelastic, and piezoelectric properties are obtained due to the flattening of the free energy profile. 23 So, the general guiding principle to increase the piezoelectric properties is the construction of MPBinferroelectric solid solutions. 23,25 This approach has been well verified by various research groups. 23,25 However, there is a limit on the flattening of the free energy profile as the compositional driven phase transition is a first-order phase transition. 23 Thus, further enhancement of the piezoelectric properties above a critical value has not beenobtainedincaseoftheMPB.However,ithasalsobeen reported that the critical volume fraction of the local structural heterogeneity creates higher interfacial energy due to the small size with higher specific area as compared to the KNN matrix. So the nanoscale structural heterogeneity effectively creates interfacial energy and the competition between interfacial energy and bulk energy may need further flattening of the free energy profile and result in an enhancement of piezoelectric properties. 25
Based on the detailed literature survey, two important questions arise: (i) does structural heterogeneity play an important role in the enhancement of the piezoelectric properties for ferroelectric systems? (ii) is structural heterogeneity limited to only Pb-based relaxor (such as PMN-PT and PZT) ferroelectric systems for enhanced piezoelectric response?. 49 To understand these questions, we have systematically studied the effect of Sm on KNN. It has been reported that the enhancement of piezoelectric properties in case of KNN system sometimes associated with the homogeneous distribution of grains with better microstructure. 18 Here, the grain size of x = 0.05% Sm is similar to the pure KNN ( x = 0), so the change in the piezoelectric properties is not associated with the grain size and microstructure of the Sm-doped KNN system. It has also been presented in Section 1 that the shifting of the T O-T toward RT is one of the well-accepted approaches to increase the piezoelectric coefficient. 18 As there is a small increase in the T O-T value and T C values are nearly unaffected with substitution of Sm in KNN-Sm system, so the enhancement of dielectric and piezoelectric properties is
not associated with the change in the phase transition temperature. The linear variation of dielectric constant with frequency allows us to find the slope, and the relative change in the magnitude of the slope can be used as the (qualitative) measure of the structural heterogeneity present in the system. 49 It has been observed from Figure S5 that structural heterogeneity parameter increases for x = 0.05% Sm (which is a maximum for the present study) compared to pure KNN ( x = 0.0), and after that, it decreases. So, the change in the structural heterogeneity is associated with a change in Sm concentration in KNSN ceramics. The induction of the tetragonal ( P 4 mm ) phase in the parent orthorhombic ( Amm 2) phase due to the Sm-substitutions increases the structural disorder, which has been reflected in terms of broadening of the pc reflections (Figure 1). The fraction of the tetragonal phases also increases with the increase in the Sm concentrations (Table S1). The tetragonal ( P 4 mm ) phase can be incorporated with the parent Amm 2 phase to account for the local structural heterogeneity. 24 The structural heterogeneity/disorder induced by tetragonal ( P 4 mm ) phase maynot be compatible with long-range ferroelectric ordering of orthorhombic ( Amm 2) phase; therefore, it breaks the long-range ferroelectric ordering above the optimal concentration. As a result, a decrease in the ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties is observed above the critical concentration. 50 Similar explanations have also been reported for Sm-doped PMN-PT system by Kumar et al. 24

# INTRODUCTION
Ferroelectric and piezoelectric materials belonging to perovskite structure show the highest piezoelectric coefficients and strong electromechanical coupling and, hence, are widely used for various device applications, such as sensors, actuators, capacitors, transducers, underwater acoustics, and ultrasonic medical imaging. 1,2 Among the plethora of ferroelectric materials, the lead (Pb)based ferroelectric perovskites, such as Pb(Zr x Ti 1 -x )O3 (PZT), (1 -x )Pb(Mg1/3 Nb 2/3 O 3 )x PbTiO3 (PMNx PT), are primarily used in various electronic devices due to their excellent dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties. 1-6 However, the high lead content ( &gt; 60 wt%) in these ferroelectric perovskites causes serious concern due to lead toxicity. 4,6 The release of poisonous Pb into the atmosphere cannot be avoided during the hightemperature synthesis due to its high vapor pressure. 2 To increase the awareness of environmental safety worldwide, restrictions on using hazardous Pb-based material systems in various electronic devices were imposed by directives on 'Waste of Electrical and Electronic Equipment' and 'Restriction of certain Hazardous Substances.' 4,5,7,8 Hence, alternatives to lead-based materials in various electronic devices are needed, and research and development are now concentrated on eco-friendly and environmentally benign materials. Various lead-free ferroelectric systems, such as BaTiO3 (BT), (Na0.5 Bi 0.5 )TiO3 (NBT), (K0.5 Bi 0.5 )TiO3 (KBT), and (K0.5 Na 0.5 )NbO 3 (KNN), are widely studied by different research groups around the globe. 2-13 Among those ferroelectric systems, alkali niobate-based KNN ceramics have received significant attention since 2003 after the famous work in textured (Li, Ta, and Sb) modified KNN ceramic by Saito et al. 14
KNN is ferroelectric material with a high Curie temperature ( T C ∼ 420 ◦ C), a moderate piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 = 80 pC/N), high remanent polarization ( P r = 33 µ C/cm 2 ), and a high coercive field (20 kV/cm). 7,15 At room temperature (RT), KNN crystallizes into an orthorhombic crystal structure with the space group (SG) Amm 2. 7,16,17 Similar to BaTiO 3 , the KNN system undergoes a sequence of phase transitions as a function of temperature. With increasing temperature, KNN transforms from

The moderate piezoelectric coefficient of non-textured KNN is not suitable for devices. Hence, the piezoelectric coefficient of KNN has to be enhanced to match that of Pb-based materials for successful replacement. Recently, various research has been carried out on non-textured KNN ferroelectric systems to enhance the piezoelectric properties, 18 and various strategies, such as hot pressing, spark plasma sintering, microwave sintering, controlled atmosphere sintering, two-step sintering, the addition of sintering aids, and chemical modification (doping, solid solutions), have been adopted by different research groups. 7,8,19,20 The synthesis of stoichiometric KNN itself is a challenge due to the evaporation of the volatile potassium (K) and sodium (Na) at high processing temperatures, which leads to non-stoichiometry and degradation of piezoelectric properties. 15 Hence, the synthesis of highdensity KNNceramicswithprecise stoichiometry, material stability, and reproducibility under ordinary synthesis conditions is still a challenge. Therefore, KNN-based ceramics are usually prepared with a narrow processing window, that is, a small temperature range and short dwell time for sintering, to avoid the significant volatilization of alkali metal elements and to suppress abnormal grain growth (AGG). 21 It has also been established that the enhancement of the piezoelectric properties is due to the shifting of PPT to near RT. 18 However, shifting the PPT toward RT leads to narrow temperature stability of the piezoelectric properties, which can restrict its use in some applications. Thefabrication of solid solutions around the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) is one of the suitable approaches to enhance the piezoelectric and ferroelectric properties. Lessons from the PZT, which is the solid solution of PbTiO3 and PbZrO3, suggest compositions near the MPB enhancethepiezoelectric and ferroelectric properties. This enhancement of piezoelectric properties at the MPB is due
to the flattening of free energy as a result of the easy polarization rotation pathway. 22 Another strategy to improve the piezoelectric properties of a material is by inducing local structural heterogeneity. 22,23 Using mesoscale phase field simulations, it has been reported that structural heterogeneity can further flatten the free energy profile, and enhance the piezoelectric properties. 22,23
Few reports are available on the Pb-based systems where the substitution of Sm in PMN-PT enhanced the structural heterogeneity. A high piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 ∼ 1500 pC/N) in 2.5 mol% Sm-doped Pb(Mg1/3 Nb 2/3 )O 3-PbTiO3 (PMN-PT) ceramics was reported by Li et al. The ultrahigh piezoelectricity is due to the occupancy of Sm 3 + at the A-site of PMN-PT, which produces local structural heterogeneity. 22 Kumaret al. also reported a high piezoelectric coefficient d 33 of 1160 pC/N in Sm-doped 0.71Pb(Mg1/3 Nb 2/3 )O 3 -0.29PbTiO 3 ceramics and supported the effect structural-polar heterogeneity at local scale due to the existence of different crystallographic phases. The structural heterogeneity can interact with the long-range ferroelectric; as a result, an enhanced piezoelectric property is observed. 24 Local compositional heterogeneity, in contrast, reduces the piezoelectric properties, as reported by Wang et al. on Sm-doped PMN-PT. They reported a decrease in the d 33 value for the sample with local compositional heterogeneity compared to the sample without local compositional heterogeneity. 25 Li et al. reported ultrahigh d 33 of ∼ 1500 pC/N and high dielectric permittivity of &gt; 13,000 in Sm-doped PMN-PT ceramics. The higher values of d 33 and Ɛ r are due to local structural heterogeneity. 23 Theoretically, based on the density functional theory (DFT) calculations, it has also been seen that Sm is the most effective element for substitution to induce local structure heterogeneity in the PMN-PT system. 22
The substitution of higher valence and small-size ions (e.g., Sm 3 + ions) at the A-site changes the crystal structure and also introduces atomic disorders/local structural heterogeneity in such materials. 22 Seshadri et al. studied the temperature-dependent piezoelectric coefficient d 33 in Sm-, La-, and Nb-doped Pb1 -x Zr x TiO3 (PZT) ceramics. Among the prepared samples, the 2 mol% Sm-doped PZT shows the highest d 33 (915 pm/V) value. The highest d 33 is due to the coexistence of tetragonal and pseudo-cubic phases below T C. 26 A phase transition from antiferroelectric (AFE) P-phase to AFE R-phase has been reported by Wang et al. for 0.88Na 1 -3 x Sm x NbO3-0.12Sr0.7 Bi 0.2 TiO 3 ceramics. For x &gt; 0.01, this ceramic shows relaxor-like behavior. 27 The photoluminescence and electrical properties of Sm-doped 0.948(K0.5 Na 0.5 NbO 3 )-0.052(LiSbO 3 ) (KNN-5.2LS) ceramics were studied by Hao et al. They have reported that Sm2 O 3 substitution into KNN-5.2LS induces a phase transition from the mixed phases of orthorhombic and tetragonal phases to a pseudo-cubic phase and shifts the PPT below the RT. The 0.4 mol% Smdoped KNN-5.2LS shows the strongest reddish-orange emission (upon blue light excitation), the highest piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 = 176 pC/N, d* 33 = 230 pm/V), and maximumsensing sensitivity (7.5 × 10 -4 Kat 293 K). 18 Sun et al. studied the photochromism, luminescence modulation, and ferroelectric energy storage properties of 1 mol% Sm-doped K0.5Na0.5 NbO3 ceramic. They have reported that the samples show a reversible color change from green to pale gray by alternating visible light irradiation ( λ &gt; 407 nm) and thermal stimulus (230 ◦ C). 28 Sm-doped K0.5 Na 0.5 NbO3 ceramics have been prepared by Lin et al., and their optical transmittance, photochromism, photoluminescence, and PL modulation properties have been investigated. The 2 mol% Sm-doped KNN shows the highest transparency ( T ∼ 55%) at 700 nm and reversible photochromism by alternating the illumination and thermal stimulus. Sm doping in the KNN system improves sintering behavior, and a higher density ( &gt; 95% of theoretical density) has been obtained. 28 Apart from the aforementioned enhanced piezoelectric properties, the Sm-doped KNNsystemalsoshowsnormalferroelectric behavior with improved ferroelectric properties. 29
To the best of our knowledge, very few reports systematically correlate the composition, structure, and ferroelectric/dielectric properties of the Sm-doped KNN systems.

# CONCLUSION
In summary, high-density KNSN ceramics were successfully fabricated by the conventional solid-state reaction route. The XRD and Raman data confirmed a compositional-driven structural phase transition from orthorhombic ( Amm 2) phase to the orthorhombic + tetragonal ( Amm 2 + P 4 mm ) dual phase with an
increase in Sm concentration at RT. Unlike ferroelectric to paraelectric phase transition, the T O-T transitions manifest as relaxation behavior in the dielectric constant and loss peak in Sm-modified KNN systems. The induction of structural heterogeneity at the inter-ferroelectric phase boundary in Sm-modified KNN systems is the origin of the appearance of the relaxation behavior. The dielectric constant at RT increases initially with Sm concentration, goes through a maximum for x = 0.005, and decreases after that. Among the prepared samples of (K0.5 Na 0.5 ) 1 -3 x Sm x )NbO3, the ceramic with x = 0.005 has the optimum ferroelectric properties and maximum piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 = 94 pC/N). The rise in the degree of local structural heterogeneity due to Sm doping is the reason for the enhanced piezoelectric properties of ((K 0.5 Na0.5 ) 1 -3 x Sm x )NbO3 ceramics with critical composition x = 0.005. # INTRODUCTION
Ferroelectric and piezoelectric materials belonging to perovskite structure show the highest piezoelectric coefficients and strong electromechanical coupling and, hence, are widely used for various device applications, such as sensors, actuators, capacitors, transducers, underwater acoustics, and ultrasonic medical imaging. 1,2 Among the plethora of ferroelectric materials, the lead (Pb)based ferroelectric perovskites, such as Pb(Zr x Ti 1 -x )O3 (PZT), (1 -x )Pb(Mg1/3 Nb 2/3 O 3 )x PbTiO3 (PMNx PT), are primarily used in various electronic devices due to their excellent dielectric, ferroelectric, and piezoelectric properties. 1-6 However, the high lead content ( &gt; 60 wt%) in these ferroelectric perovskites causes serious concern due to lead toxicity. 4,6 The release of poisonous Pb into the atmosphere cannot be avoided during the hightemperature synthesis due to its high vapor pressure. 2 To increase the awareness of environmental safety worldwide, restrictions on using hazardous Pb-based material systems in various electronic devices were imposed by directives on 'Waste of Electrical and Electronic Equipment' and 'Restriction of certain Hazardous Substances.' 4,5,7,8 Hence, alternatives to lead-based materials in various electronic devices are needed, and research and development are now concentrated on eco-friendly and environmentally benign materials. Various lead-free ferroelectric systems, such as BaTiO3 (BT), (Na0.5 Bi 0.5 )TiO3 (NBT), (K0.5 Bi 0.5 )TiO3 (KBT), and (K0.5 Na 0.5 )NbO 3 (KNN), are widely studied by different research groups around the globe. 2-13 Among those ferroelectric systems, alkali niobate-based KNN ceramics have received significant attention since 2003 after the famous work in textured (Li, Ta, and Sb) modified KNN ceramic by Saito et al. 14
KNN is ferroelectric material with a high Curie temperature ( T C ∼ 420 ◦ C), a moderate piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 = 80 pC/N), high remanent polarization ( P r = 33 µ C/cm 2 ), and a high coercive field (20 kV/cm). 7,15 At room temperature (RT), KNN crystallizes into an orthorhombic crystal structure with the space group (SG) Amm 2. 7,16,17 Similar to BaTiO 3 , the KNN system undergoes a sequence of phase transitions as a function of temperature. The moderate piezoelectric coefficient of non-textured KNN is not suitable for devices. Hence, the piezoelectric coefficient of KNN has to be enhanced to match that of Pb-based materials for successful replacement. Recently, various research has been carried out on non-textured KNN ferroelectric systems to enhance the piezoelectric properties, 18 and various strategies, such as hot pressing, spark plasma sintering, microwave sintering, controlled atmosphere sintering, two-step sintering, the addition of sintering aids, and chemical modification (doping, solid solutions), have been adopted by different research groups. 7,8,19,20 The synthesis of stoichiometric KNN itself is a challenge due to the evaporation of the volatile potassium (K) and sodium (Na) at high processing temperatures, which leads to non-stoichiometry and degradation of piezoelectric properties. 15 Hence, the synthesis of highdensity KNNceramicswithprecise stoichiometry, material stability, and reproducibility under ordinary synthesis conditions is still a challenge. Therefore, KNN-based ceramics are usually prepared with a narrow processing window, that is, a small temperature range and short dwell time for sintering, to avoid the significant volatilization of alkali metal elements and to suppress abnormal grain growth (AGG). 21 It has also been established that the enhancement of the piezoelectric properties is due to the shifting of PPT to near RT. 18 However, shifting the PPT toward RT leads to narrow temperature stability of the piezoelectric properties, which can restrict its use in some applications. Thefabrication of solid solutions around the morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) is one of the suitable approaches to enhance the piezoelectric and ferroelectric properties. Lessons from the PZT, which is the solid solution of PbTiO3 and PbZrO3, suggest compositions near the MPB enhancethepiezoelectric and ferroelectric properties. This enhancement of piezoelectric properties at the MPB is due
to the flattening of free energy as a result of the easy polarization rotation pathway. 22 Another strategy to improve the piezoelectric properties of a material is by inducing local structural heterogeneity. 22,23 Using mesoscale phase field simulations, it has been reported that structural heterogeneity can further flatten the free energy profile, and enhance the piezoelectric properties. 22,23
Few reports are available on the Pb-based systems where the substitution of Sm in PMN-PT enhanced the structural heterogeneity. A high piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 ∼ 1500 pC/N) in 2.5 mol% Sm-doped Pb(Mg1/3 Nb 2/3 )O 3-PbTiO3 (PMN-PT) ceramics was reported by Li et al. The ultrahigh piezoelectricity is due to the occupancy of Sm 3 + at the A-site of PMN-PT, which produces local structural heterogeneity. 22 Kumaret al. also reported a high piezoelectric coefficient d 33 of 1160 pC/N in Sm-doped 0.71Pb(Mg1/3 Nb 2/3 )O 3 -0.29PbTiO 3 ceramics and supported the effect structural-polar heterogeneity at local scale due to the existence of different crystallographic phases. The structural heterogeneity can interact with the long-range ferroelectric; as a result, an enhanced piezoelectric property is observed. 24 Local compositional heterogeneity, in contrast, reduces the piezoelectric properties, as reported by Wang et al. on Sm-doped PMN-PT. They reported a decrease in the d 33 value for the sample with local compositional heterogeneity compared to the sample without local compositional heterogeneity. 25 Li et al. reported ultrahigh d 33 of ∼ 1500 pC/N and high dielectric permittivity of &gt; 13,000 in Sm-doped PMN-PT ceramics. The higher values of d 33 and Ɛ r are due to local structural heterogeneity. 23 Theoretically, based on the density functional theory (DFT) calculations, it has also been seen that Sm is the most effective element for substitution to induce local structure heterogeneity in the PMN-PT system. 22
The substitution of higher valence and small-size ions (e.g., Sm 3 + ions) at the A-site changes the crystal structure and also introduces atomic disorders/local structural heterogeneity in such materials. 22 Seshadri et al. studied the temperature-dependent piezoelectric coefficient d 33 in Sm-, La-, and Nb-doped Pb1 -x Zr x TiO3 (PZT) ceramics. Among the prepared samples, the 2 mol% Sm-doped PZT shows the highest d 33 (915 pm/V) value. The highest d 33 is due to the coexistence of tetragonal and pseudo-cubic phases below T C. 26 A phase transition from antiferroelectric (AFE) P-phase to AFE R-phase has been reported by Wang et al. for 0.88Na 1 -3 x Sm x NbO3-0.12Sr0.7 Bi 0.2 TiO 3 ceramics. For x &gt; 0.01, this ceramic shows relaxor-like behavior. 27 The photoluminescence and electrical properties of Sm-doped 0.948(K0.5 Na 0.5 NbO 3 )-0.052(LiSbO 3 ) (KNN-5.2LS) ceramics were studied by Hao et al. They have reported that Sm2 O 3 substitution into KNN-5.2LS induces a phase transition from the mixed phases of orthorhombic and tetragonal phases to a pseudo-cubic phase and shifts the PPT below the RT. # CONCLUSION
In summary, high-density KNSN ceramics were successfully fabricated by the conventional solid-state reaction route. The XRD and Raman data confirmed a compositional-driven structural phase transition from orthorhombic ( Amm 2) phase to the orthorhombic + tetragonal ( Amm 2 + P 4 mm ) dual phase with an
increase in Sm concentration at RT. Unlike ferroelectric to paraelectric phase transition, the T O-T transitions manifest as relaxation behavior in the dielectric constant and loss peak in Sm-modified KNN systems. The induction of structural heterogeneity at the inter-ferroelectric phase boundary in Sm-modified KNN systems is the origin of the appearance of the relaxation behavior. The dielectric constant at RT increases initially with Sm concentration, goes through a maximum for x = 0.005, and decreases after that. Among the prepared samples of (K0.5 Na 0.5 ) 1 -3 x Sm x )NbO3, the ceramic with x = 0.005 has the optimum ferroelectric properties and maximum piezoelectric coefficient ( d 33 = 94 pC/N). The rise in the degree of local structural heterogeneity due to Sm doping is the reason for the enhanced piezoelectric properties of ((K 0.5 Na0.5 ) 1 -3 x Sm x )NbO3 ceramics with critical composition x = 0.005.
