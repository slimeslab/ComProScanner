Concerning the relative permittivity and as for previously published results [54], a broad anomaly is observed, with a maximum around /C25 300 8 C, which is higher than previously reported for this system (150-200 8 C). This maximum can be attributed to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition only close to KBT, since the x ¼ 0.05 sample appears as tetragonal, but the other compositions present a pseudocubic symmetry up to x ¼ 0.4 and a through cubic symmetry for 0.5 /C20 x /C20 1. Indeed, such behavior is characterized by the occurrence of a very broad e ( T ) peak (or e 0 ( T ) in comple x notation of relative permittivity) and a strong frequency dispersion in the peak temperature ( T m) and in the magnitude of e 0 below T m [62]. This anomaly can also be observed in the tan( d ) (or e 00 ) curves, the temperature for the maximum in e 0 appearing always higher than in e 00 (or tan( d )) curves. The absence of an obvious phase transition associated to these anomalies is also generally one of the representative features of relaxor materials [63]. As is well known for the ''model'' relaxor, PbMg1/3 Nb2/3O3 (PMN), such behavior is due to the occurrence of polar nanoregions (PNRs), which induces random-site electric dipoles, and the breadth of the e 0 ( T ) peak is a manifestation of the dipolar glass-like response of these materials [62]. If the relaxor nature of NBT and KBT materials is known for a long time and widely accepted, their origin was still controversial up to recent times. Indeed, NBT presents a long-range ferroelectric order at room temperature, but at the so-called depolarization temperature ( T d /C25 220 8 C), relaxor behavior was observed, together with a broad maximum in the e ( T ) curve around /C25 300 8 C, and an apparently antiferroelectric behavior between these two temperatures. Recent studies have clearly demonstrated for NBT, and thus probably also for KBT, that the dielectric anomalies have their origin in the occurrence of PNRs: The peculiar temperature T d of NBT (and thus also for KBT), where the ferroelectric order disappears, is the temperature for which long-range ferroelectric order reverts back to the relaxor state upon heating [64]. The behavior of these materials is thus clearly of relaxor type, with a long-range ferroelectric order established at room temperature for NBT and KBT. As for other comple x perovskite materials, the occurrence of a relaxor behavior for the KBT-KBZ solid solution is thus not surprising and can be considered as a consequence of the mixed occupation of A- and B-sites [65]. Indeed, for NBT and KBT, A-sites are occupied both by Bi 3 þ and by an alkaline ion (Na þ , K þ ). In addition, a ferroelectric/relaxor crossover can also be observed when compositional disorder is introduced by chemical substitutions [66] in ferroelectric materials, the behavior changing from 'pure'' ferroelectric to ''pure'' relaxor behavior. Such phenomena have been observed for example for BaTiO3-BaZrO3-CaTiO3 system, for which
A-and B site substitutions are simultaneously introduced [67], and also for Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based solid solutions with A- and/or B-site substitution [33, 36, 41]: these chemical modifications generally induce an increase of the diffusivity of the phase transitions together with a relaxor behavior. Thus, it is not surprising that the long-range ferroelectric order of KBT is suppressed by the Zr/Ti substitution and that the high chemical disorder induce relaxor behavior. Moreover, considering the general trend induced by Zr 4 þ content, the value of the relative permittivity at the maximum appears as decreased by KBZ substitution, together with a ''flattening'' of the curves. This evolution was expected on the basis of structural considerations: as the Zr 4 þ ion is larger than the Ti 4 þ one, the substitution implies a decrease of the off-centering of the B-ion in the BO6 octahedron, thus destroying the ferroelectric behavior and reducing the polar character of the cell. In addition, some of the samples also present a second peak, not frequency dependent ( T /C25 580-600 8 C). 4 Conclusions In the present work, we studied the possibility of substituting Zr 4 þ for Ti 4 þ in B-site of the K1/2Bi1/2TiO3 perovskite compound. The X-ray diffraction results demonstrated that the tetragonal symmetry of KBT is observed only for low Zr content ( x ¼ 0.05). For higher values of x , the tetragonal distortion of KBT is suppressed and a cubic symmetry is observed for the zirconium-rich part of the K1/2 Bi1/2 Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution ( x &gt; 0.5). For the range corresponding to the transition between these two symmetries (0.1 /C20 x &lt; 0.5), the powders obtained directly after chemical reaction were structurally disordered and appeared as a mi x of two perovskite phases with different compositions and symmetry. For the pseudocubic and cubic range, ( x ¼ 0.1 to x ¼ 1.0), the '' a '' lattice parameter increase with x -values, according to the ionic radius of the Zr 4 þ ion, larger than the Ti 4 þ one. For the tetragonal and pseudocubic ranges, piezoelectric properties were detected with a rapid decrease of the d 33 coefficient induced by the substitution. For low values of x (tetragonal range, x ¼ 0.05), a dielectric anomaly associated to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition of KBT is observed. For higher values of x , a broad dielectric anomaly is observed around 300 8 C, which corresponds to relaxor behavior.

1 Introduction The perovskite compounds ABO3 are one of the most common oxide structures. Thus, they have been extensively studied, since they present a very large panel of interesting physical properties such as ferroelectricity and piezoelectricity [1-8], superconductivity [9-11], giant magneto-resistance [12-14]. The interest for such compounds was recently renewed since multiferroic properties were evidenced in such materials [15-17], as well as new peculiar ferroelectric properties at the nanoscale [18, 19] or for thin-film form [20] and new physical phenomena due to interface properties [21-23]. Concerning the piezoelectric properties, the industrially used compounds mainly belong
modulated by binary or ternary solid-solution formation, such as NBT-KBT [33-35], NBT-BaTiO3 [36], NBTKBT-BaTiO3 [37], NBT-KBT-Li0.5Bi0.5TiO3 [38, 39], NBT-SrTiO3 [40, 41], double substitutions like for NBT-PbZrO3 [42], NBT-BiScO3 [42], NBT-BiFeO3 [42-44], NBT-KBT-BiAlO3 [45] or more comple x substitution on A-sites [46]. Some of these substitutions improve the piezoelectric properties of NBT and KBT, while some of them suppress the ferroelectricity, inducing sometimes interesting dielectric properties and/or relaxor behavior. Thus, as for the PZT, the understanding of the relationships between structure and electrical properties is necessary, in order to be able to modulate and control the properties of the compounds before using them in devices. As for PZT, Zr 4 þ ions would logically replace Ti 4 þ on B-sites. In order to test for this substitution, we selected the compound K1/2Bi1/2TiO3 (KBT), a ferroelectric compound [10] of tetragonal symmetry (space group P4mm [ a ¼ 3.994 A ˚ , c ¼ 4.038 A ˚ [47, 48]). The tolerance factor of KBT is larger than one ( t /C25 1.03) and thus implies structural distortion by displacement of cations, like in BaTiO3 ( t /C25 1.06) and PbTiO3 ( t /C25 1.02), a lone-pair effect being observed in the last one (like for Bi 3 þ ion). As the ionic radius of the Zr 4 þ ion is larger than that of Ti 4 þ ( r Ti 4 þ ¼ 0.605 A ˚ and r Zr 4 þ ¼ 0.72 A ˚ for coordination number ¼ 6 [49]), this structural distortion will decrease when substituting Zr 4 þ for Ti 4 þ . For the hypothetical compound K1/2Bi1/2ZrO3 (KBZ), the calculated tolerance factor is smaller than unity ( t /C25 0.97), thus similar to that of BaZrO3 ( t /C25 1.00, cubic, space group Pm3m) and of PbZrO3 ( t /C25 0.96, orthorhombic, space group Pbam). Thus, a modification of structural distortion type can be predicted for the K 1/2 Bi1/2 Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution, the change probably occurring around x /C25 0.5 ( t /C25 1). Furthermore, this substitution will also suppress the polar (ferroelectric) behavior of KBT: a tolerance factor smaller than unity generally implies structural distortions by octahedra rotations, which are unfavorable to ferroelectricity, but lead more to antiferroelectric behavior. Moreover, (i) the calculated tolerance factor lies between those of BaZrO3 (paraelectric) and PbZrO3 (antiferroelectric), and (ii) Pb 2 þ and (K þ /Bi 3 þ ) pseudoion present similar ionic radii and lone-pair effect ( r Pb 2 þ ¼ 1.49 A ˚ and r K þ = Bi 3 þ ¼ 1.51 A ˚ for coordination number ¼ 12 [49]). At the present time, very few data are available for the KBZ compound or the KBT-KBZ solid solution. Smolenski et al. [50] reported in 1967 for the possible existence of KBZ. A cubic-like X-ray diffraction pattern was published in 1985 [51] and a JCPDS/PDF card based upon this report was assigned to this compound [52]. But considering the tolerance factor calculated for KBZ, the real structure probably corresponds to distortion by octahedron tilting and thus to a noncubic unit cell [53]. Concerning the K1/2Bi1/2Ti(1x )Zr x O3 system (KBT-KBZ), a continuous solid-solution was reported in a short study mainly dedicated to the dielectric properties [54]. A symmetry change from tetragonal to rhomboedral was also reported for x /C25 0.1, with a very small rhomboedral distortion ( d r &lt; 10 min angle). Thus, the published results are ambiguous concerning both the hypothetical KBZ compound and the KBT-KBZ solid solution. As a consequence, the aim of the present study is: (i) to confirm for the possibility of the Zr/Ti substitution in the K1/2Bi1/2Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution. (ii) to verify the existence of the K1/2Bi1/2ZrO3 compound and (iii) to measure the dielectric and piezoelectric properties of such materials. 2 Experimental procedure All the samples were elaborated by solid-state reaction using ZrO2 (SigmaAldrich 99.99%), TiO2 (Sigma-Aldrich 99.8%), KNO3 (Ventron &gt; 99%) and Bi2O3 (Sigma-Aldrich 99.9%). Concerning the relative permittivity and as for previously published results [54], a broad anomaly is observed, with a maximum around /C25 300 8 C, which is higher than previously reported for this system (150-200 8 C). This maximum can be attributed to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition only close to KBT, since the x ¼ 0.05 sample appears as tetragonal, but the other compositions present a pseudocubic symmetry up to x ¼ 0.4 and a through cubic symmetry for 0.5 /C20 x /C20 1. Indeed, such behavior is characterized by the occurrence of a very broad e ( T ) peak (or e 0 ( T ) in comple x notation of relative permittivity) and a strong frequency dispersion in the peak temperature ( T m) and in the magnitude of e 0 below T m [62]. This anomaly can also be observed in the tan( d ) (or e 00 ) curves, the temperature for the maximum in e 0 appearing always higher than in e 00 (or tan( d )) curves. The absence of an obvious phase transition associated to these anomalies is also generally one of the representative features of relaxor materials [63]. As is well known for the ''model'' relaxor, PbMg1/3 Nb2/3O3 (PMN), such behavior is due to the occurrence of polar nanoregions (PNRs), which induces random-site electric dipoles, and the breadth of the e 0 ( T ) peak is a manifestation of the dipolar glass-like response of these materials [62]. If the relaxor nature of NBT and KBT materials is known for a long time and widely accepted, their origin was still controversial up to recent times. Indeed, NBT presents a long-range ferroelectric order at room temperature, but at the so-called depolarization temperature ( T d /C25 220 8 C), relaxor behavior was observed, together with a broad maximum in the e ( T ) curve around /C25 300 8 C, and an apparently antiferroelectric behavior between these two temperatures. Recent studies have clearly demonstrated for NBT, and thus probably also for KBT, that the dielectric anomalies have their origin in the occurrence of PNRs: The peculiar temperature T d of NBT (and thus also for KBT), where the ferroelectric order disappears, is the temperature for which long-range ferroelectric order reverts back to the relaxor state upon heating [64]. The behavior of these materials is thus clearly of relaxor type, with a long-range ferroelectric order established at room temperature for NBT and KBT. As for other comple x perovskite materials, the occurrence of a relaxor behavior for the KBT-KBZ solid solution is thus not surprising and can be considered as a consequence of the mixed occupation of A- and B-sites [65]. Indeed, for NBT and KBT, A-sites are occupied both by Bi 3 þ and by an alkaline ion (Na þ , K þ ). In addition, a ferroelectric/relaxor crossover can also be observed when compositional disorder is introduced by chemical substitutions [66] in ferroelectric materials, the behavior changing from 'pure'' ferroelectric to ''pure'' relaxor behavior. Such phenomena have been observed for example for BaTiO3-BaZrO3-CaTiO3 system, for which
A-and B site substitutions are simultaneously introduced [67], and also for Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based solid solutions with A- and/or B-site substitution [33, 36, 41]: these chemical modifications generally induce an increase of the diffusivity of the phase transitions together with a relaxor behavior. Thus, it is not surprising that the long-range ferroelectric order of KBT is suppressed by the Zr/Ti substitution and that the high chemical disorder induce relaxor behavior. Moreover, considering the general trend induced by Zr 4 þ content, the value of the relative permittivity at the maximum appears as decreased by KBZ substitution, together with a ''flattening'' of the curves. This evolution was expected on the basis of structural considerations: as the Zr 4 þ ion is larger than the Ti 4 þ one, the substitution implies a decrease of the off-centering of the B-ion in the BO6 octahedron, thus destroying the ferroelectric behavior and reducing the polar character of the cell. In addition, some of the samples also present a second peak, not frequency dependent ( T /C25 580-600 8 C). 4 Conclusions In the present work, we studied the possibility of substituting Zr 4 þ for Ti 4 þ in B-site of the K1/2Bi1/2TiO3 perovskite compound. The X-ray diffraction results demonstrated that the tetragonal symmetry of KBT is observed only for low Zr content ( x ¼ 0.05). For higher values of x , the tetragonal distortion of KBT is suppressed and a cubic symmetry is observed for the zirconium-rich part of the K1/2 Bi1/2 Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution ( x &gt; 0.5). For the range corresponding to the transition between these two symmetries (0.1 /C20 x &lt; 0.5), the powders obtained directly after chemical reaction were structurally disordered and appeared as a mi x of two perovskite phases with different compositions and symmetry. For the pseudocubic and cubic range, ( x ¼ 0.1 to x ¼ 1.0), the '' a '' lattice parameter increase with x -values, according to the ionic radius of the Zr 4 þ ion, larger than the Ti 4 þ one. For the tetragonal and pseudocubic ranges, piezoelectric properties were detected with a rapid decrease of the d 33 coefficient induced by the substitution. For low values of x (tetragonal range, x ¼ 0.05), a dielectric anomaly associated to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition of KBT is observed. For higher values of x , a broad dielectric anomaly is observed around 300 8 C, which corresponds to relaxor behavior.

Concerning the relative permittivity and as for previously published results [54], a broad anomaly is observed, with a maximum around /C25 300 8 C, which is higher than previously reported for this system (150-200 8 C). This maximum can be attributed to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition only close to KBT, since the x ¼ 0.05 sample appears as tetragonal, but the other compositions present a pseudocubic symmetry up to x ¼ 0.4 and a through cubic symmetry for 0.5 /C20 x /C20 1. Indeed, such behavior is characterized by the occurrence of a very broad e ( T ) peak (or e 0 ( T ) in comple x notation of relative permittivity) and a strong frequency dispersion in the peak temperature ( T m) and in the magnitude of e 0 below T m [62]. This anomaly can also be observed in the tan( d ) (or e 00 ) curves, the temperature for the maximum in e 0 appearing always higher than in e 00 (or tan( d )) curves. The absence of an obvious phase transition associated to these anomalies is also generally one of the representative features of relaxor materials [63]. As is well known for the ''model'' relaxor, PbMg1/3 Nb2/3O3 (PMN), such behavior is due to the occurrence of polar nanoregions (PNRs), which induces random-site electric dipoles, and the breadth of the e 0 ( T ) peak is a manifestation of the dipolar glass-like response of these materials [62]. If the relaxor nature of NBT and KBT materials is known for a long time and widely accepted, their origin was still controversial up to recent times. Indeed, NBT presents a long-range ferroelectric order at room temperature, but at the so-called depolarization temperature ( T d /C25 220 8 C), relaxor behavior was observed, together with a broad maximum in the e ( T ) curve around /C25 300 8 C, and an apparently antiferroelectric behavior between these two temperatures. Recent studies have clearly demonstrated for NBT, and thus probably also for KBT, that the dielectric anomalies have their origin in the occurrence of PNRs: The peculiar temperature T d of NBT (and thus also for KBT), where the ferroelectric order disappears, is the temperature for which long-range ferroelectric order reverts back to the relaxor state upon heating [64]. The behavior of these materials is thus clearly of relaxor type, with a long-range ferroelectric order established at room temperature for NBT and KBT. As for other comple x perovskite materials, the occurrence of a relaxor behavior for the KBT-KBZ solid solution is thus not surprising and can be considered as a consequence of the mixed occupation of A- and B-sites [65]. Indeed, for NBT and KBT, A-sites are occupied both by Bi 3 þ and by an alkaline ion (Na þ , K þ ). In addition, a ferroelectric/relaxor crossover can also be observed when compositional disorder is introduced by chemical substitutions [66] in ferroelectric materials, the behavior changing from 'pure'' ferroelectric to ''pure'' relaxor behavior. Such phenomena have been observed for example for BaTiO3-BaZrO3-CaTiO3 system, for which
A-and B site substitutions are simultaneously introduced [67], and also for Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based solid solutions with A- and/or B-site substitution [33, 36, 41]: these chemical modifications generally induce an increase of the diffusivity of the phase transitions together with a relaxor behavior. Thus, it is not surprising that the long-range ferroelectric order of KBT is suppressed by the Zr/Ti substitution and that the high chemical disorder induce relaxor behavior. Moreover, considering the general trend induced by Zr 4 þ content, the value of the relative permittivity at the maximum appears as decreased by KBZ substitution, together with a ''flattening'' of the curves. This evolution was expected on the basis of structural considerations: as the Zr 4 þ ion is larger than the Ti 4 þ one, the substitution implies a decrease of the off-centering of the B-ion in the BO6 octahedron, thus destroying the ferroelectric behavior and reducing the polar character of the cell. In addition, some of the samples also present a second peak, not frequency dependent ( T /C25 580-600 8 C). 4 Conclusions In the present work, we studied the possibility of substituting Zr 4 þ for Ti 4 þ in B-site of the K1/2Bi1/2TiO3 perovskite compound. The X-ray diffraction results demonstrated that the tetragonal symmetry of KBT is observed only for low Zr content ( x ¼ 0.05). For higher values of x , the tetragonal distortion of KBT is suppressed and a cubic symmetry is observed for the zirconium-rich part of the K1/2 Bi1/2 Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution ( x &gt; 0.5). For the range corresponding to the transition between these two symmetries (0.1 /C20 x &lt; 0.5), the powders obtained directly after chemical reaction were structurally disordered and appeared as a mi x of two perovskite phases with different compositions and symmetry. For the pseudocubic and cubic range, ( x ¼ 0.1 to x ¼ 1.0), the '' a '' lattice parameter increase with x -values, according to the ionic radius of the Zr 4 þ ion, larger than the Ti 4 þ one. For the tetragonal and pseudocubic ranges, piezoelectric properties were detected with a rapid decrease of the d 33 coefficient induced by the substitution. For low values of x (tetragonal range, x ¼ 0.05), a dielectric anomaly associated to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition of KBT is observed. For higher values of x , a broad dielectric anomaly is observed around 300 8 C, which corresponds to relaxor behavior.

1 Introduction The perovskite compounds ABO3 are one of the most common oxide structures. Thus, they have been extensively studied, since they present a very large panel of interesting physical properties such as ferroelectricity and piezoelectricity [1-8], superconductivity [9-11], giant magneto-resistance [12-14]. The interest for such compounds was recently renewed since multiferroic properties were evidenced in such materials [15-17], as well as new peculiar ferroelectric properties at the nanoscale [18, 19] or for thin-film form [20] and new physical phenomena due to interface properties [21-23]. Concerning the piezoelectric properties, the industrially used compounds mainly belong
modulated by binary or ternary solid-solution formation, such as NBT-KBT [33-35], NBT-BaTiO3 [36], NBTKBT-BaTiO3 [37], NBT-KBT-Li0.5Bi0.5TiO3 [38, 39], NBT-SrTiO3 [40, 41], double substitutions like for NBT-PbZrO3 [42], NBT-BiScO3 [42], NBT-BiFeO3 [42-44], NBT-KBT-BiAlO3 [45] or more comple x substitution on A-sites [46]. Some of these substitutions improve the piezoelectric properties of NBT and KBT, while some of them suppress the ferroelectricity, inducing sometimes interesting dielectric properties and/or relaxor behavior. Thus, as for the PZT, the understanding of the relationships between structure and electrical properties is necessary, in order to be able to modulate and control the properties of the compounds before using them in devices. As for PZT, Zr 4 þ ions would logically replace Ti 4 þ on B-sites. In order to test for this substitution, we selected the compound K1/2Bi1/2TiO3 (KBT), a ferroelectric compound [10] of tetragonal symmetry (space group P4mm [ a ¼ 3.994 A ˚ , c ¼ 4.038 A ˚ [47, 48]). The tolerance factor of KBT is larger than one ( t /C25 1.03) and thus implies structural distortion by displacement of cations, like in BaTiO3 ( t /C25 1.06) and PbTiO3 ( t /C25 1.02), a lone-pair effect being observed in the last one (like for Bi 3 þ ion). As the ionic radius of the Zr 4 þ ion is larger than that of Ti 4 þ ( r Ti 4 þ ¼ 0.605 A ˚ and r Zr 4 þ ¼ 0.72 A ˚ for coordination number ¼ 6 [49]), this structural distortion will decrease when substituting Zr 4 þ for Ti 4 þ . For the hypothetical compound K1/2Bi1/2ZrO3 (KBZ), the calculated tolerance factor is smaller than unity ( t /C25 0.97), thus similar to that of BaZrO3 ( t /C25 1.00, cubic, space group Pm3m) and of PbZrO3 ( t /C25 0.96, orthorhombic, space group Pbam). Thus, a modification of structural distortion type can be predicted for the K 1/2 Bi1/2 Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution, the change probably occurring around x /C25 0.5 ( t /C25 1). Furthermore, this substitution will also suppress the polar (ferroelectric) behavior of KBT: a tolerance factor smaller than unity generally implies structural distortions by octahedra rotations, which are unfavorable to ferroelectricity, but lead more to antiferroelectric behavior. Moreover, (i) the calculated tolerance factor lies between those of BaZrO3 (paraelectric) and PbZrO3 (antiferroelectric), and (ii) Pb 2 þ and (K þ /Bi 3 þ ) pseudoion present similar ionic radii and lone-pair effect ( r Pb 2 þ ¼ 1.49 A ˚ and r K þ = Bi 3 þ ¼ 1.51 A ˚ for coordination number ¼ 12 [49]). At the present time, very few data are available for the KBZ compound or the KBT-KBZ solid solution. Smolenski et al. [50] reported in 1967 for the possible existence of KBZ. A cubic-like X-ray diffraction pattern was published in 1985 [51] and a JCPDS/PDF card based upon this report was assigned to this compound [52]. But considering the tolerance factor calculated for KBZ, the real structure probably corresponds to distortion by octahedron tilting and thus to a noncubic unit cell [53]. Concerning the K1/2Bi1/2Ti(1x )Zr x O3 system (KBT-KBZ), a continuous solid-solution was reported in a short study mainly dedicated to the dielectric properties [54]. A symmetry change from tetragonal to rhomboedral was also reported for x /C25 0.1, with a very small rhomboedral distortion ( d r &lt; 10 min angle). Thus, the published results are ambiguous concerning both the hypothetical KBZ compound and the KBT-KBZ solid solution. As a consequence, the aim of the present study is: (i) to confirm for the possibility of the Zr/Ti substitution in the K1/2Bi1/2Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution. (ii) to verify the existence of the K1/2Bi1/2ZrO3 compound and (iii) to measure the dielectric and piezoelectric properties of such materials. 2 Experimental procedure All the samples were elaborated by solid-state reaction using ZrO2 (SigmaAldrich 99.99%), TiO2 (Sigma-Aldrich 99.8%), KNO3 (Ventron &gt; 99%) and Bi2O3 (Sigma-Aldrich 99.9%). The ZrO2 and TiO2 raw materials were first separately ball milled in ethanol for 2 h at 390-400 rpm using agate balls in an agate container (planetary mill ''pulverisette'', Fritsch, IdarOberstein, Germany), in order to reduce their mean particle sizes. They were then dried at 200 8 Cinorder to suppress any residual water. After drying, these precursors were weighed according to the desired composition and homogenized by ball milling in ethanol for 2 h at 390-400 rpm. The obtained powders were then calcined at 900 8 C for 4 h, milled again and annealed between 900 and 1000 8 C for 4 h ( x ¼ 0.4 to x ¼ 1), while this second calcination was performed for 10 h for the Ti rich range ( x ¼ 0 to x ¼ 0.3). In order to determine the best calcination temperatures, simultaneous thermogravimetric and differential thermal analysis (TG/DSC Netzsch STA 409, Selb, Germany) was performed on the initial mi x of raw powders for different compositions, using a 10 8 C/min heating rate. After calcination, the obtained powders were studied by X-ray diffraction (Bruker D8, u /2 u configuration, LinxEye high-speed detector, CuK a 1 radiation, step size 0.08 8 , Bruker AXS, Karlsruhe, Germany). The powders were then pressed into 10 mm diameter disks ( /C25 43 MPa) and sintered at 1000-1070 8 C for 1 h. The obtained pellets were then electroded using platinum paint sintered at 800 8 C for half an hour. The electrical characterizations were performed on these pellets using an impedance analyzer (HP 4194A, Agilent technologies, Santa Clara, California, USA) at several frequencies between 100 Hz and 1 MHz. This one was carried out in a silicone oil bath at room temperature using a 10 kV DC high-voltage source (SL10, Spellman, New York, USA). Concerning the relative permittivity and as for previously published results [54], a broad anomaly is observed, with a maximum around /C25 300 8 C, which is higher than previously reported for this system (150-200 8 C). This maximum can be attributed to the tetragonal-cubic phase transition only close to KBT, since the x ¼ 0.05 sample appears as tetragonal, but the other compositions present a pseudocubic symmetry up to x ¼ 0.4 and a through cubic symmetry for 0.5 /C20 x /C20 1. Indeed, such behavior is characterized by the occurrence of a very broad e ( T ) peak (or e 0 ( T ) in comple x notation of relative permittivity) and a strong frequency dispersion in the peak temperature ( T m) and in the magnitude of e 0 below T m [62]. This anomaly can also be observed in the tan( d ) (or e 00 ) curves, the temperature for the maximum in e 0 appearing always higher than in e 00 (or tan( d )) curves. The absence of an obvious phase transition associated to these anomalies is also generally one of the representative features of relaxor materials [63]. As is well known for the ''model'' relaxor, PbMg1/3 Nb2/3O3 (PMN), such behavior is due to the occurrence of polar nanoregions (PNRs), which induces random-site electric dipoles, and the breadth of the e 0 ( T ) peak is a manifestation of the dipolar glass-like response of these materials [62]. If the relaxor nature of NBT and KBT materials is known for a long time and widely accepted, their origin was still controversial up to recent times. Indeed, NBT presents a long-range ferroelectric order at room temperature, but at the so-called depolarization temperature ( T d /C25 220 8 C), relaxor behavior was observed, together with a broad maximum in the e ( T ) curve around /C25 300 8 C, and an apparently antiferroelectric behavior between these two temperatures. Recent studies have clearly demonstrated for NBT, and thus probably also for KBT, that the dielectric anomalies have their origin in the occurrence of PNRs: The peculiar temperature T d of NBT (and thus also for KBT), where the ferroelectric order disappears, is the temperature for which long-range ferroelectric order reverts back to the relaxor state upon heating [64]. The behavior of these materials is thus clearly of relaxor type, with a long-range ferroelectric order established at room temperature for NBT and KBT. As for other comple x perovskite materials, the occurrence of a relaxor behavior for the KBT-KBZ solid solution is thus not surprising and can be considered as a consequence of the mixed occupation of A- and B-sites [65]. Indeed, for NBT and KBT, A-sites are occupied both by Bi 3 þ and by an alkaline ion (Na þ , K þ ). In addition, a ferroelectric/relaxor crossover can also be observed when compositional disorder is introduced by chemical substitutions [66] in ferroelectric materials, the behavior changing from 'pure'' ferroelectric to ''pure'' relaxor behavior. Such phenomena have been observed for example for BaTiO3-BaZrO3-CaTiO3 system, for which
A-and B site substitutions are simultaneously introduced [67], and also for Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based solid solutions with A- and/or B-site substitution [33, 36, 41]: these chemical modifications generally induce an increase of the diffusivity of the phase transitions together with a relaxor behavior. Thus, it is not surprising that the long-range ferroelectric order of KBT is suppressed by the Zr/Ti substitution and that the high chemical disorder induce relaxor behavior. Moreover, considering the general trend induced by Zr 4 þ content, the value of the relative permittivity at the maximum appears as decreased by KBZ substitution, together with a ''flattening'' of the curves. This evolution was expected on the basis of structural considerations: as the Zr 4 þ ion is larger than the Ti 4 þ one, the substitution implies a decrease of the off-centering of the B-ion in the BO6 octahedron, thus destroying the ferroelectric behavior and reducing the polar character of the cell. In addition, some of the samples also present a second peak, not frequency dependent ( T /C25 580-600 8 C). 4 Conclusions In the present work, we studied the possibility of substituting Zr 4 þ for Ti 4 þ in B-site of the K1/2Bi1/2TiO3 perovskite compound. The X-ray diffraction results demonstrated that the tetragonal symmetry of KBT is observed only for low Zr content ( x ¼ 0.05). For higher values of x , the tetragonal distortion of KBT is suppressed and a cubic symmetry is observed for the zirconium-rich part of the K1/2 Bi1/2 Ti(1x )Zr x O3 solid solution ( x &gt; 0.5). For the range corresponding to the transition between these two symmetries (0.1 /C20 x &lt; 0.5), the powders obtained directly after chemical reaction were structurally disordered and appeared as a mi x of two perovskite phases with different compositions and symmetry. For the pseudocubic and cubic range, ( x ¼ 0.1 to x ¼ 1.0), the '' a '' lattice parameter increase with x -values, according to the ionic radius of the Zr 4 þ ion, larger than the Ti 4 þ one.
