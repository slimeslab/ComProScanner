# TITLE
Microstructure and electrical properties of tantalum doped (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Zr0.10Ti0.90)O3 ceramics

# ABSTRACT
(BaCa)(ZrTi)TaO ceramics (BCZTT, 0 ≤  ≤ 3.0 mol%) were prepared via a conventional solid-state reaction method, and the effect of Ta substitution on the microstructure and electrical properties of the BCZTT ceramics was investigated. The microstructure of the BCZT ceramics is strongly affected by Ta doping. Low-concentration Ta addition ( = 0.1 mol%) promotes the grain growth of the BCZT ceramics. However, high-concentration Ta addition ( > 0.5 mol%) effectively hinders the grain growth. The dielectric, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties of the BCZT ceramics are improved by addition of a small amount of Ta. For the ceramics with  = 0.3 mol%, electrical performance reaches optimum:  = 7,770,  (the temperature of the dielectric maximum) = 101.8 °C,  = 17.9 μC/cmE^{2},  = 9.1 μC/cmE^{2},  = 0.54 kV/mm and  = 303 pC/N. The relaxor properties of undoped and Ta-doped BCZT ceramics were depicted, and the diffusivity increases with the increasing Ta contents.

# INTRODUCTION
As alternatives to the toxic lead zirconate titanate (PZT) ceramics, lead-free piezoelectric ceramics hold potential in various applications, such as actuators, sensors, piezoelectric resonators and so on [\u2013]. The general formula of perovskites is ABO3, where A and B stand for the cations located at different sites of the lattice. According to common knowledge, donor doping creates A-site vacancies, promoting ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties (so-called \u201csoftening behavior\u201d). In 2009, Liu et al. obtained an extraordinary d33 of 620 pC/N in Ba0.85Ca0.15Zr0.10Ti0.90O3 (BCZT) ceramics after systematic study of Ba0.7Ca0.3TiO3\u2013BaZr0.2Ti0.8O3 solution system [, ], showing that BCZT is a promising alternative of PZT and other lead-based piezoelectrics. By sintering at an optimized temperature of 1,540 \xb0C, a d33 of 650 pC/N can be obtained in BCZT ceramics. Other than optimizing processing procedure, doping is also a way to further improve the electrical properties of BCZT. The effects of isovalent dopants (SrE^{2+}  at the A site), accepters (ZnE^{2+}  and CuE^{2+}  at the B site) and self-compensation rare earth (RE) ions (PrE^{3+}, CeE^{4+}, YE^{3+}, SmE^{3+} [\u2013] ) on BCZT ceramics have been discussed. By Sr doping, an optimized d33 of 534 pC/N was observed in 3 mol% Sr-doped BCZT ceramics, while the Curie temperature decreases as the Sr content increases. By addition of ZnO into BCZT to an amount of 0.06 mol%, the tricritical point of BCZT ceramics was shifted to room temperature, and an optimized d33 of 521 pC/N was obtained. By addition of Pr2O3, the sintering temperature of BCZT ceramics is lowered to 1,400 \xb0C, and a d33 of 460 pC/N was obtained when 0.06 wt% Pr2O3 was added. By addition of other RE oxides into BCZT, the researchers obtained optimized piezoelectric properties with addition content of 0.04\u20130.06 wt% [\u2013]. A sixfold coordination TaE^{5+} ion is suitable as a B-site dopant in BaTiO3- and Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based perovskites, since its radius is close to that of a TiE^{4+} ion. Xue et al.  discussed the influence of Ta doping on the densification of BaTiO3 ceramics during a hot-pressing process, and proved that Ta doping promotes the densification during the late stage of sintering. Zhai et al.  and Nguyen et al.  reported that Ta doping enhances the ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties in Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based systems as a donor dopant. Therefore, like Ta-doped BaTiO3 and Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based perovskites, Ta-doped BCZT may also exhibit an enhancement in the dielectric, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties. However, to our knowledge, there have been no reports on the effect of Ta doping on BCZT ceramics so far. In this work, (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Zr0.10Ti0.90)1\u2212xTaxO3 (BCZTT) ceramics were prepared via a solid-state reaction method. Microstructure and the electrical properties of the BCZTT ceramics were studied. The evolution of structure of the BCZTT ceramics with increasing Ta content was analyzed.

# TITLE
Microstructure and electrical properties of tantalum doped (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Zr0.10Ti0.90)O3 ceramics

# ABSTRACT
(BaCa)(ZrTi)TaO ceramics (BCZTT, 0 ≤  ≤ 3.0 mol%) were prepared via a conventional solid-state reaction method, and the effect of Ta substitution on the microstructure and electrical properties of the BCZTT ceramics was investigated. The microstructure of the BCZT ceramics is strongly affected by Ta doping. Low-concentration Ta addition ( = 0.1 mol%) promotes the grain growth of the BCZT ceramics. However, high-concentration Ta addition ( > 0.5 mol%) effectively hinders the grain growth. The dielectric, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties of the BCZT ceramics are improved by addition of a small amount of Ta. For the ceramics with  = 0.3 mol%, electrical performance reaches optimum:  = 7,770,  (the temperature of the dielectric maximum) = 101.8 °C,  = 17.9 μC/cmE^{2},  = 9.1 μC/cmE^{2},  = 0.54 kV/mm and  = 303 pC/N. The relaxor properties of undoped and Ta-doped BCZT ceramics were depicted, and the diffusivity increases with the increasing Ta contents.

# INTRODUCTION
As alternatives to the toxic lead zirconate titanate (PZT) ceramics, lead-free piezoelectric ceramics hold potential in various applications, such as actuators, sensors, piezoelectric resonators and so on [\u2013]. The general formula of perovskites is ABO3, where A and B stand for the cations located at different sites of the lattice. According to common knowledge, donor doping creates A-site vacancies, promoting ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties (so-called \u201csoftening behavior\u201d). In 2009, Liu et al. obtained an extraordinary d33 of 620 pC/N in Ba0.85Ca0.15Zr0.10Ti0.90O3 (BCZT) ceramics after systematic study of Ba0.7Ca0.3TiO3\u2013BaZr0.2Ti0.8O3 solution system [, ], showing that BCZT is a promising alternative of PZT and other lead-based piezoelectrics. By sintering at an optimized temperature of 1,540 \xb0C, a d33 of 650 pC/N can be obtained in BCZT ceramics. Other than optimizing processing procedure, doping is also a way to further improve the electrical properties of BCZT. The effects of isovalent dopants (SrE^{2+}  at the A site), accepters (ZnE^{2+}  and CuE^{2+}  at the B site) and self-compensation rare earth (RE) ions (PrE^{3+}, CeE^{4+}, YE^{3+}, SmE^{3+} [\u2013] ) on BCZT ceramics have been discussed. By Sr doping, an optimized d33 of 534 pC/N was observed in 3 mol% Sr-doped BCZT ceramics, while the Curie temperature decreases as the Sr content increases. By addition of ZnO into BCZT to an amount of 0.06 mol%, the tricritical point of BCZT ceramics was shifted to room temperature, and an optimized d33 of 521 pC/N was obtained. By addition of Pr2O3, the sintering temperature of BCZT ceramics is lowered to 1,400 \xb0C, and a d33 of 460 pC/N was obtained when 0.06 wt% Pr2O3 was added. By addition of other RE oxides into BCZT, the researchers obtained optimized piezoelectric properties with addition content of 0.04\u20130.06 wt% [\u2013]. A sixfold coordination TaE^{5+} ion is suitable as a B-site dopant in BaTiO3- and Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based perovskites, since its radius is close to that of a TiE^{4+} ion. Xue et al.  discussed the influence of Ta doping on the densification of BaTiO3 ceramics during a hot-pressing process, and proved that Ta doping promotes the densification during the late stage of sintering. Zhai et al.  and Nguyen et al.  reported that Ta doping enhances the ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties in Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based systems as a donor dopant. Therefore, like Ta-doped BaTiO3 and Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3-based perovskites, Ta-doped BCZT may also exhibit an enhancement in the dielectric, ferroelectric and piezoelectric properties. However, to our knowledge, there have been no reports on the effect of Ta doping on BCZT ceramics so far. In this work, (Ba0.85Ca0.15)(Zr0.10Ti0.90)1\u2212xTaxO3 (BCZTT) ceramics were prepared via a solid-state reaction method. Microstructure and the electrical properties of the BCZTT ceramics were studied. The evolution of structure of the BCZTT ceramics with increasing Ta content was analyzed.
