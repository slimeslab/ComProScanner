# TITLE
Phase modified ferroelectricity in Na0.5-xKxBi0.5TiO3

# ABSTRACT
Rietveld analysis on (NaK)BiTiO { = 0, 15%, 20%, 22.5%, 25%, & 30%}, depicts change in structural symmetry from R3c for NBT to R3m and P4mm in NBT-KBT solid solutions. A mixed phase with R3m and P4mm is obtained for doping from 20 to 25%, where 22.5% composition has an equal proportion of both phases. Frequency and temperature-dependent dielectric relaxation studies for (NaK)BiTiO shows a transition from a high-temperature paraelectric state to ergodic relaxor state (T = 300–330˚C) with a crossover in domain-structure & number density at  = 22.5%. The H-N analysis with relaxation time ‘τ,’ relaxation time distribution function, and asymmetry parameter ‘β’ strongly supports enhanced piezoelectric properties for  = 22.5% specimen. Enhanced piezoelectric coefficient  = 208pC/N is observed for 22.5% K-doped NBT, with an electromechanical coupling factor of ~ 44%.

# INTRODUCTION
There are extensive studies on Pb(Mn1/3Nb2/3)O3 (PMN), PbZrxTi(1\u2212x)O3 (PZT) based electromechanical relaxors with the tuning of their morphotropic phase boundary-induced piezoelectricity. Random bonds settle the cooperation between the PNR, and quenched site disorder-induced random fields control the PNR characteristic (stability and shape) and drive the phase transition. Understanding \u201crelaxorness\u201d requires incorporating local stress and strain to explain PNR formation and their phase transition. The PNRs have associated local symmetry (rhombohedral R3c in PMN) linked with the A-O-B/B\u2019 bonding. In solid solutions, say PMN-PT, composition modification deteriorates the stability of the parent-local symmetry of PNRs. Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3(NBT) and KxNa1\u2212xNbO3 dielectric properties, depolarization temperature, and conductivity are tailored via additives [, ]. In NBT-based solid solutions, Bi provides A-site hybridization with the oxygen-electrons (covalent bonding) anticipated for excellent piezoelectricity. Also, NBT being susceptible to A- and B-site substituted solid solutions, shows suitable candidature for piezoelectricity-tuning for industrial applications. The A-site with local ionic bonding cum covalent bonding introduces site disorder in the system affecting the PNR character. NBT undergoes a complex structural transition from a high-temperature cubic phase to a tetragonal (P4bm) state over a temperature window [, ]. In NBT, residues of octahedral tilts of tetragonal symmetry can be present at room temperature owing to compatible energies of local distortions. The structural modulations have observed apparent dielectric signals, with frequency-independent maxima in dielectric constant below 350\u02daC. On cooling, dipolar interactions are sustained in the system with lowered local symmetry (R3c rhombohedral symmetry). Below 200\u02daC non-ergodic relaxor-ferroelectric state is introduced. The high conductivity of NBT hinders the poling of the specimen and restricts the piezoelectric coefficient to low values. Among alkali perovskite, K0.5Bi0.5TiO3 (KBT) exhibits ferroelectricity with lamellar domains below 270\u02daC, there-above relaxor state up to Curie temperature 380\u02daC. Here, ferroelectricity is associated with tetragonal (P4mm) symmetry possessing partial ordering of displacement of BiE^{3+} and TiE^{4+} cations relative to their oxygen cage. However, the low density of ordinarily fired KBT systems prohibits complete poling. Literature has documented many strategies for A-site doped NBT, A-site vacancy-induced system, or non-stoichiometric (BiE^{3+} excess) systems. Piezoelectricity in NBT is operated by A-site, unlike B-site controlled piezoelectricity in PT. In analogy to lead-based systems, morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) is introduced in A-site doped NBT solid solutions. In the MPB system, the co-existence of multi-phase ferroelectricity acquaints instability at the phase boundary, favoring domain variants with enhanced domain-wall mobility. A solid solution between NBT and KBT raises inter-ferroelectric instability at the rhombohedral-tetragonal phase boundary. At MPB-free energy profile flattens anisotropically and favors efficient polarization switching along the poling E-field, yielding a stronger strain effect. Enhanced dielectric and piezoelectric responses are claimed at MPB of NBT-KBT solid solution, specifically for 20% K-doping at the Na-site. Though relatively fewer details are analyzed for system crossing structural symmetry on either side of MPB. The study of NBT-KBT solid solutions across the MPB insights into the modified dipolar dynamics due to two different symmetries associated with the Na-rich & K-rich regions. The present study attempts to analyze the dielectric relaxation study with structural- and polar-domain disorders in the NKBT piezoelectric. A crossover akin to the \u201cmixed-alkali effect in oxide glasses\u201d is reported here in the dielectric polarization properties of NKBT. H-N formalism has been utilized to discover the dynamics reflected here with the changes in the Curie temperature TC, dielectric constant maximum, relaxation time, asymmetry of the relaxation, and remnant polarization. The analysis guides strong piezoelectricity in NKBT22.5% solid solution.

# CONCLUSION
In NBT-KBT solid solution, the dielectric properties are strongly correlated with structural properties exhibiting a change in phase from R3c to R3m and P4mm. The phase-modified dielectric properties have been analyzed with Havriliak Negami\u2019s formalism. The mixed polar regions associated with rhombohedral and tetragonal symmetries in approximately equal proportion for the NKBT22.5 specimen yield more significant polarization with easy domain-wall motion. Studied NKBT ceramics show a phase modifier effect with a crossover in the electrical (ac) properties, dielectric relaxation features, and polarization after x = 22.5% composition. The piezoelectric property enhances to 208pC/N with kp = 43.8% for NKBT22.5 following the structural and dielectric analysis.

# TITLE
Phase modified ferroelectricity in Na0.5-xKxBi0.5TiO3

# ABSTRACT
Rietveld analysis on (NaK)BiTiO { = 0, 15%, 20%, 22.5%, 25%, & 30%}, depicts change in structural symmetry from R3c for NBT to R3m and P4mm in NBT-KBT solid solutions. A mixed phase with R3m and P4mm is obtained for doping from 20 to 25%, where 22.5% composition has an equal proportion of both phases. Frequency and temperature-dependent dielectric relaxation studies for (NaK)BiTiO shows a transition from a high-temperature paraelectric state to ergodic relaxor state (T = 300–330˚C) with a crossover in domain-structure & number density at  = 22.5%. The H-N analysis with relaxation time ‘τ,’ relaxation time distribution function, and asymmetry parameter ‘β’ strongly supports enhanced piezoelectric properties for  = 22.5% specimen. Enhanced piezoelectric coefficient  = 208pC/N is observed for 22.5% K-doped NBT, with an electromechanical coupling factor of ~ 44%.

# INTRODUCTION
There are extensive studies on Pb(Mn1/3Nb2/3)O3 (PMN), PbZrxTi(1\u2212x)O3 (PZT) based electromechanical relaxors with the tuning of their morphotropic phase boundary-induced piezoelectricity. Random bonds settle the cooperation between the PNR, and quenched site disorder-induced random fields control the PNR characteristic (stability and shape) and drive the phase transition. Understanding \u201crelaxorness\u201d requires incorporating local stress and strain to explain PNR formation and their phase transition. The PNRs have associated local symmetry (rhombohedral R3c in PMN) linked with the A-O-B/B\u2019 bonding. In solid solutions, say PMN-PT, composition modification deteriorates the stability of the parent-local symmetry of PNRs. Na0.5Bi0.5TiO3(NBT) and KxNa1\u2212xNbO3 dielectric properties, depolarization temperature, and conductivity are tailored via additives [, ]. In NBT-based solid solutions, Bi provides A-site hybridization with the oxygen-electrons (covalent bonding) anticipated for excellent piezoelectricity. Also, NBT being susceptible to A- and B-site substituted solid solutions, shows suitable candidature for piezoelectricity-tuning for industrial applications. The A-site with local ionic bonding cum covalent bonding introduces site disorder in the system affecting the PNR character. NBT undergoes a complex structural transition from a high-temperature cubic phase to a tetragonal (P4bm) state over a temperature window [, ]. In NBT, residues of octahedral tilts of tetragonal symmetry can be present at room temperature owing to compatible energies of local distortions. The structural modulations have observed apparent dielectric signals, with frequency-independent maxima in dielectric constant below 350\u02daC. On cooling, dipolar interactions are sustained in the system with lowered local symmetry (R3c rhombohedral symmetry). Below 200\u02daC non-ergodic relaxor-ferroelectric state is introduced. The high conductivity of NBT hinders the poling of the specimen and restricts the piezoelectric coefficient to low values. Among alkali perovskite, K0.5Bi0.5TiO3 (KBT) exhibits ferroelectricity with lamellar domains below 270\u02daC, there-above relaxor state up to Curie temperature 380\u02daC. Here, ferroelectricity is associated with tetragonal (P4mm) symmetry possessing partial ordering of displacement of BiE^{3+} and TiE^{4+} cations relative to their oxygen cage. However, the low density of ordinarily fired KBT systems prohibits complete poling. Literature has documented many strategies for A-site doped NBT, A-site vacancy-induced system, or non-stoichiometric (BiE^{3+} excess) systems. Piezoelectricity in NBT is operated by A-site, unlike B-site controlled piezoelectricity in PT. In analogy to lead-based systems, morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) is introduced in A-site doped NBT solid solutions. In the MPB system, the co-existence of multi-phase ferroelectricity acquaints instability at the phase boundary, favoring domain variants with enhanced domain-wall mobility. A solid solution between NBT and KBT raises inter-ferroelectric instability at the rhombohedral-tetragonal phase boundary. At MPB-free energy profile flattens anisotropically and favors efficient polarization switching along the poling E-field, yielding a stronger strain effect. Enhanced dielectric and piezoelectric responses are claimed at MPB of NBT-KBT solid solution, specifically for 20% K-doping at the Na-site. Though relatively fewer details are analyzed for system crossing structural symmetry on either side of MPB. The study of NBT-KBT solid solutions across the MPB insights into the modified dipolar dynamics due to two different symmetries associated with the Na-rich & K-rich regions. The present study attempts to analyze the dielectric relaxation study with structural- and polar-domain disorders in the NKBT piezoelectric. A crossover akin to the \u201cmixed-alkali effect in oxide glasses\u201d is reported here in the dielectric polarization properties of NKBT. H-N formalism has been utilized to discover the dynamics reflected here with the changes in the Curie temperature TC, dielectric constant maximum, relaxation time, asymmetry of the relaxation, and remnant polarization. The analysis guides strong piezoelectricity in NKBT22.5% solid solution.

# CONCLUSION
In NBT-KBT solid solution, the dielectric properties are strongly correlated with structural properties exhibiting a change in phase from R3c to R3m and P4mm. The phase-modified dielectric properties have been analyzed with Havriliak Negami\u2019s formalism. The mixed polar regions associated with rhombohedral and tetragonal symmetries in approximately equal proportion for the NKBT22.5 specimen yield more significant polarization with easy domain-wall motion. Studied NKBT ceramics show a phase modifier effect with a crossover in the electrical (ac) properties, dielectric relaxation features, and polarization after x = 22.5% composition. The piezoelectric property enhances to 208pC/N with kp = 43.8% for NKBT22.5 following the structural and dielectric analysis.
