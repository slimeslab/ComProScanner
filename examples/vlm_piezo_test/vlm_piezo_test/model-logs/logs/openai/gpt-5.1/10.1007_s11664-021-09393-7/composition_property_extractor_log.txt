# TITLE
Enhancement of Piezoelectric Properties in Low-Temperature Sintering PZN–PZT Ceramics by Sr2+ Substitution

# ABSTRACT
Pb(ZrTi)(ZnNb)O (PZN–PZT) piezoceramics with SrE^{2+} ion substitution were sintered as low as 900°C by the solid-state reaction route. The specimens with various SrE^{2+} ion concentrations were compared according to their microstructure, phase structure, piezoelectricity, ferroelectricity, and dielectric relaxation. The substitution of the smaller SrE^{2+} ions for the A-site PbE^{2+} ions in the PZN–PZT system improves the electrical properties. The samples with 4 mol% SrE^{2+} substitutions have optimized piezoelectric properties (piezoelectric constant  = 496 pC/N, Curie temperature  = 244°C, electromechanical coupling coefficient  = 0.58). Rietveld refinement from the XRD data reveals that the tetragonal lattice constants along with the  and  axes (, ) for the PZN–PZT decrease with an increased SrE^{2+} substitution content. The  decreases faster than the , which leads to decreased tetragonality (/), thus leading to shrinkage of the oxygen octahedrons in the ABO perovskite lattice. This lattice distortion facilitates mass transport, thereby increasing the bulk density and grain size of the perovskite PZN–PZT system.

# INTRODUCTION
Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT)-based perovskite piezoceramics have been widely utilized as piezoelectric transformers, sensors, actuators, and other applications in light of their excellent electrical properties and low cost.E^{,} However, the conventional sintering temperature for PZT-based piezoelectric piezoceramics is approximately 1200\xb0C. In addition, multilayer piezoelectric ceramics need to co-fire the internal metal layers with the ceramic layers to improve the integration of the piezoelectric components.E^{None} At about 1200\xb0C sintering temperature, the internal electrodes have to use expensive platinum or palladium. If the sintering temperature is lower than 900\xb0C, the internal electrodes can use pure silver, thus greatly reducing the production cost.E^{,} To meet the requirements of commercialization and miniaturization for piezoelectric devices, the piezoceramic layers in a multilayer sheet need to be co-fired with the silver inner electrode layers below 900\xb0C. The Curie temperature (Tc) for the piezoceramics needs to be as high as possible (> 230\xb0C) to use the lead-free Zn-Sn-based solder to weld the conducting wire and components. As a typical ferroelectric relaxor, Pb(Zn1/3Nb2/3)O3 (PZN) piezoceramics with good electrical properties and a Tc of 140\xb0C have been broadly utilized as electromechanical devices.E^{, , \u2013} The PZT has a high Tc (232\u2212492\xb0C), but relatively low piezoelectric performance. Excellent electrical performance and stable perovskite structure can be obtained by forming solid solutions with a certain proportion of the PZN and the PZT.E^{, , \u2013} The modification of PZN\u2013PZT piezoceramics has attracted much attention.E^{,} Deng et al.E^{None} synthesized La-substituted 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT(51/49) piezoceramics by a hot-pressing method and obtained outstanding piezoelectric properties (kp = 0.70 and d33 = 845 pC/N). Vittayakorn et al.E^{None} prepared 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT(50/50) piezoceramics using the columbite method at a sintering temperature of 1225\xb0C and achieved outstanding electrical properties (kp = 0.70, d33 = 690 pC/N, and Tc = 299\xb0C). Chandarak et al.E^{None} concluded that the MPB composition for PZN\u2013PZT(52/48) system is clearly at 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT. Low-temperature sintering of PZN\u2013PZT piezoceramics has also been reported in recent years. Fan et al.E^{None} synthesized 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT(49/51)+Li2CO3+Sm2O3 piezoceramics at a sintering temperature of 900\xb0C and achieved optimal electrical properties (d33 = 483 pC/N, Tc = 394\xb0C, and kp = 0.65). Ngamjarurojana et al.E^{None} reported that 900\xb0C sintering 0.16Pb(Zn1/3Nb2/3)O3\u20130.04Pb(Ni1/3Nb2/3)O3\u20130.8Pb(Zr0.48Ti0.52)O3 piezoceramics have outstanding electrical properties (kp = 0.56, d33 = 350 pC/N, and Qm = 1042). Hou et al.E^{None} prepared (Pb0.95Sr0.05)(Zr0.50Ti0.50)0.80(Zn1/3Nb2/3)0.20O3 +MnO2 piezoceramics at a sintering temperature of 1000\xb0C and obtained good piezoelectric properties (Qm = 1360, kp = 0.62, tan\u03b4 = 0.002, \u03b5r = 1240, Tc = 320\xb0C, and d33 = 325 pC/N). Lee et al.E^{None} prepared 0.5 wt% MnO2 doped 80% PZT(50/50)\u201320%PZN piezoceramics at a sintering temperature of 930\xb0C and obtained outstanding electrical properties (kp = 0.62, d33= 330 pC/N, and Qm = 1000). In this work, we prepared Pb1\u2212xSrx(Zr0.49Ti0.51)0.7(Zn1/3Nb2/3)0.3O3 (PZN\u2013PZT) piezoceramics at a low sintering temperature (900\xb0C) by the traditional solid-reaction method. The substitution of various content SrE^{2+} ions for the A-site PbE^{2+} ions enhances the piezoelectric properties.

# CONCLUSION
PZN\u2013PZT piezoceramics were sintered as low as 900\xb0C. Optimized piezoelectric properties (kp = 0.58, Tc = 244\xb0C, d33 = 496 pC/N, Qm = 40, \u03b5r = 3000, Pr = 32.80 \u03bcC/cmE^{2}, and Ec = 0.98 kV/mm) were obtained in 4 mol% SrE^{2+} substituted piezoceramics. As the SrCO3 content increases, the grain size first increases to a maximum and then gradually decreases. Most electrical properties such as d33, kp, and Pr show similar trends. The lattice parameters (a, c) demonstrate that c and a decrease with the increased SrCO3 content, while c decreases faster than a, which leads to a decreased tetragonality (c/a). The substitution of PbE^{2+} ions at the A-site with the SrE^{2+} ions leads to shrinkage of the oxygen octahedron.

# TITLE
Enhancement of Piezoelectric Properties in Low-Temperature Sintering PZN–PZT Ceramics by Sr2+ Substitution

# ABSTRACT
Pb(ZrTi)(ZnNb)O (PZN–PZT) piezoceramics with SrE^{2+} ion substitution were sintered as low as 900°C by the solid-state reaction route. The specimens with various SrE^{2+} ion concentrations were compared according to their microstructure, phase structure, piezoelectricity, ferroelectricity, and dielectric relaxation. The substitution of the smaller SrE^{2+} ions for the A-site PbE^{2+} ions in the PZN–PZT system improves the electrical properties. The samples with 4 mol% SrE^{2+} substitutions have optimized piezoelectric properties (piezoelectric constant  = 496 pC/N, Curie temperature  = 244°C, electromechanical coupling coefficient  = 0.58). Rietveld refinement from the XRD data reveals that the tetragonal lattice constants along with the  and  axes (, ) for the PZN–PZT decrease with an increased SrE^{2+} substitution content. The  decreases faster than the , which leads to decreased tetragonality (/), thus leading to shrinkage of the oxygen octahedrons in the ABO perovskite lattice. This lattice distortion facilitates mass transport, thereby increasing the bulk density and grain size of the perovskite PZN–PZT system.

# INTRODUCTION
Pb(Zr,Ti)O3 (PZT)-based perovskite piezoceramics have been widely utilized as piezoelectric transformers, sensors, actuators, and other applications in light of their excellent electrical properties and low cost.E^{,} However, the conventional sintering temperature for PZT-based piezoelectric piezoceramics is approximately 1200\xb0C. In addition, multilayer piezoelectric ceramics need to co-fire the internal metal layers with the ceramic layers to improve the integration of the piezoelectric components.E^{None} At about 1200\xb0C sintering temperature, the internal electrodes have to use expensive platinum or palladium. If the sintering temperature is lower than 900\xb0C, the internal electrodes can use pure silver, thus greatly reducing the production cost.E^{,} To meet the requirements of commercialization and miniaturization for piezoelectric devices, the piezoceramic layers in a multilayer sheet need to be co-fired with the silver inner electrode layers below 900\xb0C. The Curie temperature (Tc) for the piezoceramics needs to be as high as possible (> 230\xb0C) to use the lead-free Zn-Sn-based solder to weld the conducting wire and components. As a typical ferroelectric relaxor, Pb(Zn1/3Nb2/3)O3 (PZN) piezoceramics with good electrical properties and a Tc of 140\xb0C have been broadly utilized as electromechanical devices.E^{, , \u2013} The PZT has a high Tc (232\u2212492\xb0C), but relatively low piezoelectric performance. Excellent electrical performance and stable perovskite structure can be obtained by forming solid solutions with a certain proportion of the PZN and the PZT.E^{, , \u2013} The modification of PZN\u2013PZT piezoceramics has attracted much attention.E^{,} Deng et al.E^{None} synthesized La-substituted 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT(51/49) piezoceramics by a hot-pressing method and obtained outstanding piezoelectric properties (kp = 0.70 and d33 = 845 pC/N). Vittayakorn et al.E^{None} prepared 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT(50/50) piezoceramics using the columbite method at a sintering temperature of 1225\xb0C and achieved outstanding electrical properties (kp = 0.70, d33 = 690 pC/N, and Tc = 299\xb0C). Chandarak et al.E^{None} concluded that the MPB composition for PZN\u2013PZT(52/48) system is clearly at 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT. Low-temperature sintering of PZN\u2013PZT piezoceramics has also been reported in recent years. Fan et al.E^{None} synthesized 0.3PZN\u20130.7PZT(49/51)+Li2CO3+Sm2O3 piezoceramics at a sintering temperature of 900\xb0C and achieved optimal electrical properties (d33 = 483 pC/N, Tc = 394\xb0C, and kp = 0.65). Ngamjarurojana et al.E^{None} reported that 900\xb0C sintering 0.16Pb(Zn1/3Nb2/3)O3\u20130.04Pb(Ni1/3Nb2/3)O3\u20130.8Pb(Zr0.48Ti0.52)O3 piezoceramics have outstanding electrical properties (kp = 0.56, d33 = 350 pC/N, and Qm = 1042). Hou et al.E^{None} prepared (Pb0.95Sr0.05)(Zr0.50Ti0.50)0.80(Zn1/3Nb2/3)0.20O3 +MnO2 piezoceramics at a sintering temperature of 1000\xb0C and obtained good piezoelectric properties (Qm = 1360, kp = 0.62, tan\u03b4 = 0.002, \u03b5r = 1240, Tc = 320\xb0C, and d33 = 325 pC/N). Lee et al.E^{None} prepared 0.5 wt% MnO2 doped 80% PZT(50/50)\u201320%PZN piezoceramics at a sintering temperature of 930\xb0C and obtained outstanding electrical properties (kp = 0.62, d33= 330 pC/N, and Qm = 1000). In this work, we prepared Pb1\u2212xSrx(Zr0.49Ti0.51)0.7(Zn1/3Nb2/3)0.3O3 (PZN\u2013PZT) piezoceramics at a low sintering temperature (900\xb0C) by the traditional solid-reaction method. The substitution of various content SrE^{2+} ions for the A-site PbE^{2+} ions enhances the piezoelectric properties.

# CONCLUSION
PZN\u2013PZT piezoceramics were sintered as low as 900\xb0C. Optimized piezoelectric properties (kp = 0.58, Tc = 244\xb0C, d33 = 496 pC/N, Qm = 40, \u03b5r = 3000, Pr = 32.80 \u03bcC/cmE^{2}, and Ec = 0.98 kV/mm) were obtained in 4 mol% SrE^{2+} substituted piezoceramics. As the SrCO3 content increases, the grain size first increases to a maximum and then gradually decreases. Most electrical properties such as d33, kp, and Pr show similar trends. The lattice parameters (a, c) demonstrate that c and a decrease with the increased SrCO3 content, while c decreases faster than a, which leads to a decreased tetragonality (c/a). The substitution of PbE^{2+} ions at the A-site with the SrE^{2+} ions leads to shrinkage of the oxygen octahedron.
